# Corsica and Mediterranean World

This file owns the island, family, language, and Mediterranean conditions that precede Napoleon's French career.

## Island geography and divided authority {#island-geography-and-divided-authority}

**Status:** Source-supported. Corsica's mountains, ports, villages, kin networks, and shifting authorities shape movement and allegiance.

An island is not a single community. A coastal route, mountain pass, parish, and port expose different information and risks. Settings must identify the local authority, language, household, season, and route before treating Corsica as Napoleon's childhood backdrop.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon early years, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/1769-1793-napoleon-bonapartes-early-years/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Paoli and family allegiance {#paoli-and-family-allegiance}

**Status:** Source-critical. The Paoli relationship is a political and family conflict whose meaning changes across revolutionary events and retrospective accounts.

The Bonapartes' choices involve kinship, property, language, military danger, and competing ideas of Corsican and French authority. Later scenes must distinguish a letter, public declaration, family memory, and hostile report; no single “break” explains every person's decision.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon early years, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/1769-1793-napoleon-bonapartes-early-years/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Family property and mobility {#family-property-and-mobility}

**Status:** Work canon. Family status depends on land, dowries, offices, schooling, transport, and the ability to move between island and mainland.

A child’s education is financed through household labor and political opportunity. Settings must identify who pays, who travels, which papers authorize movement, and what relatives lose when a son leaves. Mobility is a material decision, not an abstract ambition.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon early years, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/1769-1793-napoleon-bonapartes-early-years/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Mediterranean languages and institutions {#mediterranean-languages-and-institutions}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Corsican, Italian, and French language worlds meet through school, church, military, law, and family correspondence.

Translation changes rank and meaning. A child can be fluent in household speech and uncertain in a classroom or office. Later narratives must name the language of a document, speaker, lesson, or legal act rather than flattening the island into one national voice.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon early years, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/1769-1793-napoleon-bonapartes-early-years/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Boundary to childhood settings {#boundary-to-childhood-settings}

**Status:** Work boundary. Detailed childhood, schooling, and early personality belong to the dedicated early-life settings and downstream narrative owners.

This file supplies island geography, family risk, political allegiance, property, and language. Later scenes must ground childhood action in a place, household, institution, and available knowledge rather than using retrospective greatness as present fact.
