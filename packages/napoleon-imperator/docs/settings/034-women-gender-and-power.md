# Women Gender and Power

This file owns the gendered institutions and agency surrounding Napoleon's life.

## Marriage and legal capacity {#marriage-and-legal-capacity}

**Status:** Source-supported. Marriage organizes property, inheritance, legitimacy, household authority, and diplomacy while restricting women's legal independence.

Women act within and against these structures through correspondence, patronage, property management, religious networks, salons, and family decisions. The Code's rules must be shown as lived consequences, not merely summarized.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Women as political actors {#women-as-political-actors}

**Status:** Work canon. Josephine, Letizia, sisters, queens, diplomats' wives, soldiers' families, and local women have different resources and risks.

Agency is not limited to formal office. A letter, introduction, refusal, household budget, or information exchange can alter a decision. The manuscript must not turn women into a single collective influence on Napoleon.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## War and civilian women {#war-and-civilian-women}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Occupation, requisition, widowhood, sexual violence, displacement, and care affect women differently by class, law, and territory.

Campaign settings must include women as workers, informants, refugees, property holders, caregivers, and victims without assuming that every woman occupies every role. Silence in a military report is not evidence of safety.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Boundary to dynastic women {#boundary-to-dynastic-women}

**Status:** Work boundary. Marie-Louise, Hortense, Pauline, Caroline, and other dynastic lives require separate relationship and court owners.

Each woman has a distinct marriage, property, language, political network, and exposure to state violence. No family role may be inferred from title alone.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.
