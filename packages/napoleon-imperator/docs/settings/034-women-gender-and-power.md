# Women Gender and Power

This file owns the gendered institutions and agency surrounding Napoleon's life.

## Marriage and legal capacity {#marriage-and-legal-capacity}

**Status:** Source-supported. Marriage organizes property, inheritance, legitimacy, household authority, and diplomacy while restricting women's legal independence.

Women act within and against these structures through correspondence, patronage, property management, religious networks, salons, and family decisions. The Code's rules must be shown as lived consequences, not merely summarized. Settings must identify the contract, court, household, and material property through which a legal rule operates.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Women as political actors {#women-as-political-actors}

**Status:** Work canon. Josephine, Letizia, sisters, queens, diplomats' wives, soldiers' families, and local women have different resources and risks.

Agency is not limited to formal office. A letter, introduction, refusal, household budget, or information exchange can alter a decision. The manuscript must not turn women into a single collective influence on Napoleon. Each actor needs a relationship, a channel, an audience, and a cost for action.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## War and civilian women {#war-and-civilian-women}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Occupation, requisition, widowhood, sexual violence, displacement, and care affect women differently by class, law, and territory.

Campaign settings must include women as workers, informants, refugees, property holders, caregivers, and victims without assuming that every woman occupies every role. Silence in a military report is not evidence of safety. Later scenes must identify the armed authority, household resource, and social protection or vulnerability involved.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Family labor and information networks {#family-labor-and-information-networks}

**Status:** Work canon. Households survive war and administration through correspondence, remittances, domestic labor, and decisions made outside official offices.

A wife, mother, sister, servant, or daughter may preserve papers, move money, care for dependants, or transmit news when men are absent. The setting must distinguish an actual letter from remembered advice and must show the labor that allows a household to remain connected. Domestic work is an infrastructure with time, risk, and unequal recognition.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Dynastic marriage and state strategy {#dynastic-marriage-and-state-strategy}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. A dynastic marriage is a negotiation among families, states, property, succession, ceremony, and the woman's own constrained choices.

The public ceremony cannot settle private consent or future political power. Settings must identify the marriage contract, dowry or property, language, household, diplomatic purpose, and protection available to the bride. A title does not make Marie-Louise, Hortense, Pauline, Caroline, or another woman interchangeable.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Labor rank and female education {#labor-rank-and-female-education}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Gender interacts with class, literacy, occupation, religion, age, and geography; “women” is not one social position.

A court lady, laundress, merchant, peasant, schoolgirl, nun, and officer's wife possess different access to money, law, travel, and information. Later narratives must identify work, household, language, and legal status before assigning agency or vulnerability. Education may open correspondence or employment while leaving marriage and property constraints intact.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Boundary to dynastic women {#boundary-to-dynastic-women}

**Status:** Work boundary. Marie-Louise, Hortense, Pauline, Caroline, and other dynastic lives require separate relationship and court owners.

Each woman has a distinct marriage, property, language, political network, and exposure to state violence. No family role may be inferred from title alone. Downstream owners must cite the correspondence, legal document, household, or witness that supports each relationship and must retain uncertainty where the record is mediated.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Boundary to gendered storylines {#boundary-to-gendered-storylines}

**Status:** Work boundary. This file supplies gendered institutions and material constraints; individual relationships and scenes belong to their own owners.

Later layers must show how a specific law, household, war, office, or correspondence changes a woman's available action. No storyline may use gender as a mood label or a universal explanation detached from property, kinship, labor, and risk.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
