# Josephine and Family Politics

This file owns Josephine's relationship to Napoleon and the political family network without inventing private dialogue.

## Josephine's social and family position {#josephines-social-and-family-position}

**Status:** Source-supported for Joséphine de Beauharnais as Napoleon's wife and a sustained focal relation; personal anecdotes require dated memoir or correspondence.

Josephine brings children, widowhood, colonial family connections, Parisian society, debt and patronage into Napoleon's life. She is not merely a romantic reward or a decorative court figure. Her social network can open information and access while also creating financial and reputational risk.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon Bonaparte,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, Napoleonic collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Marriage, inheritance, and legitimacy {#marriage-inheritance-and-legitimacy}

**Status:** Work canon. Marriage creates legal, emotional, financial, and dynastic consequences; it does not erase prior children or kin.

Napoleon's marriage must be staged through contracts, households, letters, guests, money, and public representation. Josephine's children remain part of the family system. Later divorce and remarriage settings must inherit the difference between affection, succession, law, and propaganda.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Court intimacy and political information {#court-intimacy-and-political-information}

**Status:** Source-supported in principle; exact conversations and bedroom confidences are disputed unless documented.

A spouse can learn through visitors, letters, servants, appointments, and mood, but proximity is not omniscience. The novel must distinguish what Josephine observes from what Napoleon hides, what a family member infers, and what a later memoir claims. Intimacy has political consequences without becoming a shortcut to every decision.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Boundary to Marie-Louise and dynastic succession {#boundary-to-marie-louise-and-dynastic-succession}

**Status:** Work boundary. The Austrian marriage, divorce, and imperial succession require later owners; this file establishes the family-politics mechanisms they inherit.

Succession combines fertility, diplomacy, law, public ceremony, and personal cost. No later marriage should be treated as a purely private replacement. The settings graph must connect each dynastic decision to institutions and affected children.

## Beauharnais and Bonaparte kin networks {#beauharnais-and-bonaparte-kin-networks}

**Status:** Source-supported for family as a political resource; exact private alliances require correspondence and household records.

Marriage links households rather than replacing them. The Beauharnais children, Bonaparte siblings, in-laws, military aides, creditors, and provincial relatives form competing channels of loyalty and information. A favour may help a sister while burdening a minister; a marriage may stabilize one branch and insult another. The settings graph must identify the beneficiary and cost of each patronage act.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Marriage contract household and children {#marriage-contract-household-and-children}

**Status:** Source-supported work canon. Marriage joins legal, financial, emotional, and dynastic households without erasing Josephine's children or the Bonaparte family.

The relationship must be staged through contracts, apartments, visitors, debts, letters, servants, and public appearances. A marriage creates claims on time and money. Josephine's children are not decorative evidence of tenderness; their education, inheritance, names, and protection affect decisions. Napoleon's siblings and in-laws likewise make the household a political network rather than a private island.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon Bonaparte,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Patronage information and social access {#patronage-information-and-social-access}

**Status:** Work canon. Josephine's social world can supply introductions, rumours, money, and access while exposing Napoleon to debt and reputational risk.

A salon visitor does not automatically become a trusted agent. Information arrives through letters, servants, relatives, creditors, diplomats, and officers, each with motive and partial knowledge. The manuscript must identify who knows a fact, who wants it believed, and what changes if it is false.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Separation absence and household strain {#separation-absence-and-household-strain}

**Status:** Source-critical. Campaign absence, infidelity rumours, illness, and delayed letters cannot be converted into omniscient marital certainty.

A letter is selected communication, not a transcript of the marriage. Absence may be military necessity, concealment, illness, or logistics. The story must show waiting, servants carrying news, physicians, money requests, and the unequal ability of husband and wife to travel or investigate.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Divorce and succession pressure {#divorce-and-succession-pressure}

**Status:** Work boundary grounded in imperial dynastic history. Divorce, Marie-Louise, the king of Rome, and later family consequences require dedicated owners.

Succession combines fertility, law, ceremony, diplomacy, and personal loss. A dynastic decision affects Josephine, her children, Bonaparte siblings, Austria, the court, and public legitimacy. It must not be narrated as a simple private replacement or as proof that earlier affection was unreal.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
