# Russian and Ottoman Diplomacy

This file owns the eastern diplomatic field that constrains French and Russian strategy.

## Empires and incompatible interests {#empires-and-incompatible-interests}

**Status:** Source-critical. French, Russian, Ottoman, Austrian, and Polish interests overlap without sharing one strategic purpose.

An alliance or treaty addresses selected questions and leaves others open. Settings must identify the state, envoy, text, audience, and implementation capacity involved. Later scenes must not infer a common plan from a ceremonial meeting.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Envoys translation and diplomatic time {#envoys-translation-and-diplomatic-time}

**Status:** Work canon. Diplomacy moves through envoys, interpreters, letters, couriers, audiences, and pauses whose timing shapes what governments know.

A proposal can be delayed, summarized, mistranslated, or received after a military event. Settings must record sender, route, language, receipt, and response where known. Silence is not a complete answer until the communication channel and waiting period are established.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Ottoman sovereignty and local agency {#ottoman-sovereignty-and-local-agency}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Ottoman rulers, provinces, cities, military leaders, and communities have interests not reducible to a European emperor's map.

A diplomatic claim about territory does not prove local control. Later narratives must identify the authority, tax, garrison, court, language, and population affected. Ottoman actors require agency, not a role as a passive object of Russian or French strategy.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Polish and Balkan questions {#polish-and-balkan-questions}

**Status:** Work boundary. Polish hopes, Balkan communities, and Ottoman borderlands require separate local owners and source chains.

The same promise can be heard as liberation, danger, or postponed sovereignty. Settings must identify whose land, law, army, and property are at stake before attributing a political meaning. Later documents must not turn imperial rhetoric into implemented policy without a route through officials and troops.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Boundary to eastern campaign owners {#boundary-to-eastern-campaign-owners}

**Status:** Work boundary. Russian campaigns, Tilsit, 1812, Ottoman diplomacy, and Polish military service require distinct campaign and relationship settings.

This file supplies diplomatic asymmetry and implementation limits. Downstream units must cite the treaty, envoy, place, language, and state actor they use and must preserve contested or incomplete information.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.
