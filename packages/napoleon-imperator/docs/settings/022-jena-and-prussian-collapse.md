## Fourth Coalition and Prussian decision {#fourth-coalition-and-prussian-decision}

**Status:** Source-supported. Prussian entry into the war reflects diplomacy, court politics, mobilization, and fear of French power, not a single battlefield impulse.

The coalition's public aims and private calculations differ. A state can mobilize while negotiating, and an army can move before its political purpose is settled. The novel must give Prussian and Saxon actors independent information and avoid treating them as a monolith.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Grande Armée concentration {#grande-armee-concentration}

**Status:** Work canon. Napoleon's army uses corps routes and concentration against a slower coalition, but roads, rivers, detachments, and staff latency remain limiting conditions.

The army's apparent omnipresence comes from separated corps converging on information. A corps commander may know only his assigned road and objective. Reports become obsolete as the enemy changes direction. Scenes must distinguish a planned concentration from troops actually closed up.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Jena Auerstedt and duplicate battle knowledge {#jena-auerstedt-and-duplicate-battle-knowledge}

**Status:** Source-required domain. The simultaneous battles at Jena and Auerstedt must remain distinct in terrain, commanders, forces, and initial information.

A report of victory at one field can coexist with crisis at the other. Headquarters, couriers, and maps do not synchronize instantly. The settings layer forbids transferring Napoleon's Jena perspective to Davout's Auerstedt command or treating the later result as present knowledge.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon, *Correspondance générale*, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/chronology-of-the-general-correspondence-of-napoleon-bonaparte-volume-one-the-learnings-1784-1797/.

## Prussia after defeat {#prussia-after-defeat}

**Status:** Work boundary. Occupation, capitulation, reform, and the 1807 peace require separate political and local owners.

Military defeat changes fortresses, tax collection, civil administration, and elite legitimacy. It does not erase Prussian society or reduce every official to a French instrument. Later settings must track reform and resentment as separate processes.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
