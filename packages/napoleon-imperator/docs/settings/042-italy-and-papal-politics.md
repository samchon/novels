This file owns Italian political geography, papal sovereignty, and the local institutions encountered by French power.

## Italian fragmentation and routes {#italian-fragmentation-and-routes}

**Status:** Source-supported. Italy contains states, cities, ports, mountains, roads, courts, and languages whose interests do not collapse into one Italian response.

An army can move through one state while negotiating with another and requisitioning from a third. Settings must identify the jurisdiction, route, local official, season, and population affected by every campaign action.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Italian campaign and political settlement {#italian-campaign-and-political-settlement}

**Status:** Work canon. Military victory becomes political power through treaties, administrations, taxes, officials, and local cooperation.

A battlefield result does not erase Venetian, Milanese, Papal, Neapolitan, or other institutional interests. Later scenes must distinguish French orders, client-state policy, municipal negotiation, and popular reception.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Papal sovereignty and religious authority {#papal-sovereignty-and-religious-authority}

**Status:** Source-critical. Conflict with the papacy is simultaneously diplomatic, territorial, religious, legal, and symbolic.

The Pope's authority is not reducible to an enemy government, and French claims are not reducible to anticlerical sentiment. Settings must identify the treaty, envoy, territory, clergy, property, and audience at issue. Ceremony and coercion can coexist without proving private belief.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Cities ports and material occupation {#cities-ports-and-material-occupation}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Italian cities and ports sustain armies through warehouses, customs, workshops, roads, and taxation.

A garrison's authority depends on stores, guards, interpreters, and local clerks. Settings must show the material cost of occupation and the people who supply or evade it. A port's strategic value is not the same as the population's experience of seizure or blockade.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Boundary to Italian campaign owners {#boundary-to-italian-campaign-owners}

**Status:** Work boundary. The 1796 campaign, Italian Kingdom, Naples, and Papal conflict require separate campaign and institution owners.

This file provides jurisdiction, religion, route, and occupation mechanics. Later units must cite the city, state, official, treaty, and source they use rather than treating Italy as a decorative landscape.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
