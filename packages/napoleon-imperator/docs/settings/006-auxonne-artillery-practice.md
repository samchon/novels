This file owns Napoleon's technical maturation at Auxonne before the Revolution's decisive military opportunities.

## Transfer to Auxonne and institutional apprenticeship {#transfer-to-auxonne-and-institutional-apprenticeship}

**Status:** Source-supported for Napoleon's La Fère service and later Auxonne artillery work; exact arrival and daily schedule remain bounded. Auxonne is where school mathematics meets a working arm, not a private laboratory.

The junior officer enters a hierarchy of directors, instructors, captains, artificers, horses, magazines, and clerks. He can propose, calculate, inspect, and learn, but he cannot command the entire school. A scenario must show signatures, requisitions, drills, and senior approval alongside technical insight.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Les institutions de l'Instruction publique du Consulat et de l'Empire,” https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/les-institutions-de-linstruction-publique-du-consulat-et-de-lempire/; Fondation Napoléon, “1769–1793: Napoleon Bonaparte's early years,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/1769-1793-napoleon-bonapartes-early-years/.

## Gun, road, horse, and powder as one system {#gun-road-horse-and-powder-as-one-system}

**Status:** Work canon grounded in artillery practice. A gun's tactical value depends on carriage condition, draught horses, powder, shot, crews, roads, and target visibility; no later prose may treat firepower as an abstract number.

An officer selecting a battery position must think about recoil, field of view, soil, elevation, ammunition route, and protection from counter-fire. A road that carries infantry may fail a limber; wet ground can immobilize a gun; a magazine can be full while the forward battery is empty. These mechanics are inherited by every later campaign.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/; Fondation Napoléon, “Les institutions de l'Instruction publique du Consulat et de l'Empire,” https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/les-institutions-de-linstruction-publique-du-consulat-et-de-lempire/.

## Officers, reputation, and written calculation {#officers-reputation-and-written-calculation}

**Status:** Source-supported for the importance of correspondence and technical education; private conversations and exact papers are contested unless preserved.

Napoleon's reputation grows through work visible to instructors and commanders: accurate calculations, useful proposals, punctual reports, and the ability to explain a technical problem in institutional language. A brilliant idea that lacks horses or authorization is not yet an operation. Letters can seek patronage or report family need, but the source edition must govern quotations.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon, *Correspondance générale* chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/chronology-of-the-general-correspondence-of-napoleon-bonaparte-volume-one-the-learnings-1784-1797/.

## Boundary to revolutionary service {#boundary-to-revolutionary-service}

**Status:** Work boundary. Auxonne supplies technical competence and professional contacts; the Revolution, leave in Corsica, and Toulon require separate owners.

The next layer of settings must show how institutional skill enters political crisis. It must not claim that artillery study automatically creates republican commitment or command genius. Leave, illness, letters, vacancies, faction, and battlefield opportunity mediate the transition.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “1769–1793: Napoleon Bonaparte's early years,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/1769-1793-napoleon-bonapartes-early-years/.

## Auxonne correspondence and leave {#auxonne-correspondence-and-leave}

**Status:** Source-supported for correspondence as a major record of the learning period; exact leave dates and private meetings require the critical letter edition.

An officer's letters connect a distant garrison to family, patrons, and political news. They travel by military courier, commercial post, or a trusted traveler, and may arrive after the situation has changed. A request for leave is an administrative act with a chain of approval, not a private wish. The settings layer preserves sender, recipient, date, place, and document status before using a letter as dialogue.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon, *Correspondance générale* chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/chronology-of-the-general-correspondence-of-napoleon-bonaparte-volume-one-the-learnings-1784-1797/.

## Technical confidence and institutional limits {#technical-confidence-and-institutional-limits}

**Status:** Work canon. Napoleon can be right about a calculation and still lack authority, materials, or a superior willing to act.

The novel must repeatedly test the difference between knowing and commanding. A junior officer submits a memorandum; a director chooses whether to fund it; an artificer reports what can be built; a clerk records the issue of powder. These checks prevent the future emperor from appearing to command a system before he has earned the relevant authority.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Les institutions de l'Instruction publique du Consulat et de l'Empire,” https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/les-institutions-de-linstruction-publique-du-consulat-et-de-lempire/.
