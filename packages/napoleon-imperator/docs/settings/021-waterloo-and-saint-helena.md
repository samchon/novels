# Waterloo and Saint Helena

This file owns the terminal campaign and captivity boundary for the twenty-volume life arc.

## Return from Elba and the Hundred Days {#return-from-elba-and-the-hundred-days}

**Status:** Source-supported for the political shock of Napoleon's return; exact local reactions and communications require dedicated owners.

The return reopens an imperial state under pressure from the Bourbon government, European powers, army loyalty, and public fear. Napoleon must reconstruct command, money, legitimacy, and administration before the coalition closes. A proclamation cannot instantly restore the 1812 army: officers must decide, depots must issue equipment, horses and guns must be found, and civil officials must choose whether to serve, wait, or resist.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Waterloo field and command uncertainty {#waterloo-field-and-command-uncertainty}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Waterloo must distinguish weather, roads, corps arrival, written orders, allied defense, Prussian movement, and contested clock traditions.

The battle cannot be reduced to one delayed attack or one subordinate's failure. Mud affects artillery; ridge lines hide reserves; couriers cross a battlefield under fire; separate corps arrive with different knowledge. Each claimed time must retain its source and event meaning. A later report from headquarters, a participant's recollection, and a staff map may describe “the attack” as order, formation, first contact, or crisis; settings must not silently merge those events.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Coalition convergence and the road network {#coalition-convergence-and-the-road-network}

**Status:** Work canon. The final campaign is a race among armies whose movements depend on roads, bridges, messengers, weather, and the interpretation of orders.

Napoleon's staff cannot see the whole coalition. A report may identify a column without proving its strength or destination; a road may be open to cavalry but blocked to artillery; a courier may arrive after the situation he describes has changed. The setting must keep French, Anglo-Allied, and Prussian information states separate and must show why a commander chooses action under uncertainty rather than granting retrospective omniscience.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Abdication and political afterlife {#abdication-and-political-afterlife}

**Status:** Source-supported for abdication and surrender as distinct political acts; legal interpretations and private motives remain contested.

Defeat changes command, family security, constitutional claims, foreign bargaining, and the fate of soldiers. Abdication is not surrender, and surrender is not immediate captivity. The novel must give each document its date, audience, and coercive context. A proclamation can claim legality while ministers, allied governments, and troops act on different assumptions; the gap between text and enforcement is part of the setting.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Surrender transport and custody {#surrender-transport-and-custody}

**Status:** Source-required domain. The interval between surrender and Saint Helena is a chain of custody, negotiations, ships, guards, and competing legal claims.

Captivity has material stages: who receives the person, who records the terms, who controls movement, what papers travel, and which government bears responsibility for security. The setting must not jump from political defeat to the island without accounting for waiting rooms, ports, shipboard confinement, provisions, weather, and the people whose labor makes custody possible. Conflicting testimonies should remain attributed to their speakers and circumstances.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Saint Helena as controlled environment {#saint-helena-as-controlled-environment}

**Status:** Source-supported for Longwood captivity and the importance of memoir, officers, governors, servants, health, and surveillance; exact final conversations require primary testimony.

Saint Helena is an island prison, household, archive, and political theatre. Access, letters, medicine, weather, boredom, staff conflict, and British regulation shape the record. Napoleon's dictated memoirs are evidence of self-fashioning as well as recollection; British reports have their own administrative interests. The setting must represent servants, officers, doctors, guards, and local residents as workers with constraints rather than as scenery around a solitary captive.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Health memory and the end of the life arc {#health-memory-and-the-end-of-the-life-arc}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. Illness, medical observation, death, burial, and later memory must be separated from retrospective myth.

Medical notes are observations made through a body, a household, and an administrative relationship; they are not omniscient diagnoses. The manuscript may render pain, weakness, treatment, and uncertainty, but must not invent a final quotation or assert a disputed cause as settled fact. Death closes the biographical chronology while opening political memory: the settings layer ends the life arc and hands reception, commemoration, and competing legends to the dedicated memory setting.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.
