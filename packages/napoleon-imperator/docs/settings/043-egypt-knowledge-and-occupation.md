# Egypt Knowledge and Occupation

This file owns the Egyptian expedition's knowledge production, military occupation, and limits of imperial understanding.

## Expedition as military and scientific system {#expedition-as-military-and-scientific-system}

**Status:** Source-supported. The expedition joins soldiers, sailors, engineers, scholars, administrators, and local populations under unequal power.

Scientific work travels with ships, instruments, translators, guards, paper, and food. A discovery is also an encounter under occupation. Settings must identify who observed, who translated, who supplied knowledge, and who controlled publication.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Local institutions and military rule {#local-institutions-and-military-rule}

**Status:** Work canon. French rule depends on taxes, councils, religious authorities, interpreters, garrisons, and force rather than an abstract imperial decree.

Egyptian officials and communities retain interests and knowledge even when French troops control a city. Settings must show the institution, language, revenue, guard, and population affected by an order. Collaboration and resistance can coexist in one household or office.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Translation and ownership of knowledge {#translation-and-ownership-of-knowledge}

**Status:** Source-critical. Maps, measurements, antiquities, texts, and descriptions pass through translators and institutions whose power is unequal.

The French scholar's notebook is not the same as local testimony. Later narratives must identify language, informant, instrument, interpreter, collector, and archive. Publication can preserve evidence while erasing the people who supplied it or benefited from it.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Disease food and environment {#disease-food-and-environment}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Heat, water, disease, food, insects, terrain, and shipping determine what the expedition can do.

Military and scientific ambition are bounded by bodies and stores. Settings must identify water source, provisions, medical knowledge, transport, and local environmental expertise. A report of disease carries the observer's classification and should not be converted into a precise diagnosis without support.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Fleet loss and strategic isolation {#fleet-loss-and-strategic-isolation}

**Status:** Work canon. Naval defeat changes communication, reinforcement, food, evacuation, and the expedition's political choices.

An army cut from France receives delayed or no orders and must improvise from incomplete reports. Later scenes must distinguish what Napoleon knows about the fleet, what local forces know, and what the expedition can physically move. Isolation is a supply and information condition, not merely a dramatic mood.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Boundary to colonial knowledge owners {#boundary-to-colonial-knowledge-owners}

**Status:** Work boundary. Detailed scholars, local institutions, battles, and antiquities require separate owners and direct source chains.

This file supplies the expedition's material and epistemic mechanics. Downstream units must preserve local agency, translation, ownership, and uncertainty rather than treating Egyptian knowledge as a free imperial resource.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
