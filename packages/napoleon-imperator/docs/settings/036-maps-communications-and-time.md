## Map accuracy and lived terrain {#map-accuracy-and-lived-terrain}

**Status:** Work canon. Maps select roads, heights, settlements, and boundaries; they do not replace reconnaissance or local knowledge.

A commander reads a map through scale, symbols, survey date, and purpose. A village may have moved, a road may be seasonal, and a contour may hide a ravine. Scenarios must distinguish map knowledge from sight and report.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Courier and postal delay {#courier-and-postal-delay}

**Status:** Source-critical rule. A letter's date is not its receipt date; routes, weather, war, censorship, and interception alter information.

The emperor can issue an order that reaches a subordinate after the tactical situation has changed. A family letter may arrive after illness or death. Every unit must record dispatch, route, receipt, and response when those facts are known.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Clocks calendars and time zones {#clocks-calendars-and-time-zones}

**Status:** Historical principle. Local time, astronomical observation, military convention, revolutionary calendars, and retrospective conversion can produce apparent contradictions.

A source's “morning,” “nine,” or date may describe observation, order, arrival, or publication. The novel must preserve the source's precision and never synchronize incompatible clocks without explanation.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology portal, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.
