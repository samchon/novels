## Rebuilding after Russia {#rebuilding-after-russia}

**Status:** Source-supported. Napoleon rebuilds an army rapidly, but new conscripts, lost cavalry, weak horses, and absent veterans change its quality.

The state can produce numbers faster than experience. Officers must train formations while campaigning, and cavalry reconnaissance suffers when remounts are scarce. Strength totals require date and arm.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## German national mobilization {#german-national-mobilization}

**Status:** Work canon. German states provide troops, territory, resentment, and competing loyalties.

French-aligned contingents may obey orders while local populations increasingly support coalition mobilization. Language, uniforms, administration, and conscription affect cohesion. The novel must distinguish state policy from popular feeling.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Leipzig as coalition scale {#leipzig-as-coalition-scale}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Leipzig's multiple armies, rivers, bridges, villages, and retreat routes demand separate fronts and timelines.

No commander has a complete view. A bridge may be a supply route, a retreat choke point, and a tactical objective at once. Orders and reports must be attached to each army's location and language.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Loss of allies and retreat west {#loss-of-allies-and-retreat-west}

**Status:** Work boundary. The collapse of the Confederation of the Rhine and 1814 invasion require later owners.

Political defection changes roads, depots, intelligence, and legitimacy before a formal treaty. Retreat is an administrative crisis as well as a military one: wounded, civilians, baggage, and bridges compete for passage.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.
