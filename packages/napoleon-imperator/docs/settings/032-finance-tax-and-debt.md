# Finance Tax and Debt

This file owns the fiscal systems that make war and administration possible.

## Revenue and collection {#revenue-and-collection}

**Status:** Source-supported work canon. Taxes require registers, collectors, courts, local cooperation, transport, and the ability to enforce payment.

An imperial budget is not cash in a single vault. Revenue arrives at different times and places, while armies need immediate pay, food, horses, and ammunition. A delayed tax payment can become a military shortage or a local revolt. Settings must identify assessment, collection, transport, accounting, and enforcement rather than treating tax as an abstract flow.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## War indemnities and occupied economies {#war-indemnities-and-occupied-economies}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Treaty payments, requisitions, confiscation, and occupation costs redistribute wealth and create resentment.

An order for supplies becomes carts seized from a town, coin removed from a treasury, or a merchant unpaid. The novel must identify beneficiaries, intermediaries, and those deprived, not describe extraction as weightless victory. A requisition has a date, quantity, authority, transport route, and consequence for the population expected to provide it.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Bank credit and confidence {#bank-credit-and-confidence}

**Status:** Work canon. State borrowing and commercial credit depend on confidence, law, collateral, and the expectation of future revenue.

A government can announce solvency while merchants refuse paper or demand security. War news changes prices and credit before an official report arrives. Financial scenes must specify document, currency, institution, and risk, and must distinguish a bank's balance from the state's ability to move cash to a distant army.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Accounting and administrative latency {#accounting-and-administrative-latency}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Fiscal authority depends on ledgers, audits, seals, messengers, offices, and the time between assessment and payment.

A ministry may know a deficit only after a campaign has begun. A forged receipt, missing register, or delayed remittance changes what a local official can prove. Settings must identify who writes, copies, verifies, stores, and transports a fiscal record and must not turn a later ledger into contemporaneous knowledge.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Soldier pay and household survival {#soldier-pay-and-household-survival}

**Status:** Work canon. Delayed pay reaches barracks, families, merchants, deserters, and local credit networks.

A soldier's nominal wage is not the same as coin received, food issued, or a family's remittance. Settings must identify arrears, deductions, substitute goods, transport, and the household's alternative labor. Fiscal policy becomes visible through boots, bread, debt, illness, and the decision to remain or leave service.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Indemnity politics and sovereignty {#indemnity-politics-and-sovereignty}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. Payments imposed after war are legal claims and political theatre as well as material extraction.

A treaty may name a sum while leaving currency, installments, collateral, enforcement, and local collection unsettled. A state can comply publicly and delay privately. Later narratives must identify the signatory, payer, collector, and people who bear the cost, rather than translating an indemnity into a single moral label.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Imperial grants and dependency {#imperial-grants-and-dependency}

**Status:** Work boundary. Estates, pensions, dowries, titles, and Bonaparte family finance require household-level owners.

An imperial grant creates obligations, visibility, and possible confiscation as well as comfort. State finance and private debt intersect but are not identical. A minister can be solvent while a soldier's family starves; a court beneficiary can be rich in title and poor in liquid cash. Later settings must preserve this scale difference.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Boundary to fiscal and household storylines {#boundary-to-fiscal-and-household-storylines}

**Status:** Work boundary. This file supplies fiscal mechanisms; local tax systems, estates, dowries, and household debts belong to dedicated owners.

Every later scene must identify the scale of the transaction, the institution that records it, the time of payment, and the person who bears the risk. The settings layer hands forward a state that can mobilize resources but cannot make money, transport, or trust instantaneous.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
