This file owns the recurring military institutions inherited by every campaign setting.

**Status:** Source-supported work canon. The corps system permits independent marches, concentration, and mutual support, but only when roads, staff, supplies, and communications work.

The Grande Armée's recurring structure combines infantry, cavalry, artillery, engineers, and health services, with staff and trains making movement possible rather than ornamental. The critical 1806 corps study identifies the permanent corps structure as created in 1803 and shows corps moving on separate roads, repairing or holding bridges, and arriving at different times. A corps commander can therefore march ahead, hold a crossing, or enter battle before the emperor arrives, but autonomy is bounded by orders, roads, supplies, staff, and information. An order-of-battle map label proves neither that every brigade is present nor that its artillery, train, and effective infantry can act together.

**Status:** Historical principle. An order has sender, recipient, route, wording, time, authority, and receipt; an intended movement is not an executed one.

The telecommunications study describes the aide-de-camp or estafette as a mounted carrier who may take a written dispatch through battlefield fire or enemy territory; handwriting, punctuation, and interpretation can themselves produce errors. Napoleon preferred experienced aides because they could report what they saw, and relay stations later structured government couriers. These are mechanisms, not a guarantee of speed. Weather, enemy action, language, distance, road, headquarters movement, and a recipient's competing orders create delay. Every campaign scenario distinguishes issue, receipt, interpretation, acknowledgment, and action, and records when one stage is merely inferred.

**Status:** Source-critical rule. Promotion combines vacancy, seniority, patronage, performance, political trust, and battlefield need; later memoirs may simplify it to genius.

The army rewards results while reproducing class, network, and political power.
