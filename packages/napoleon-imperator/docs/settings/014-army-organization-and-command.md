## Corps division and independent movement {#corps-division-and-independent-movement}

**Status:** Source-supported work canon. The corps system permits independent marches, concentration, and mutual support, but only when roads, staff, supplies, and communications work.

A corps contains infantry, cavalry, artillery, engineers, medical personnel, staff, and trains in proportions suited to its mission. A corps commander can march ahead of the army, hold a bridge, or join a battle before the emperor arrives; this autonomy is bounded by orders and information. The manuscript must not treat a map label as proof that every brigade is present.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Staff orders and receipt {#staff-orders-and-receipt}

**Status:** Historical principle. An order has sender, recipient, route, wording, time, authority, and receipt; an intended movement is not an executed one.

Aide-de-camp, courier, signal, written dispatch, or verbal relay can carry an order. Weather, enemy action, language, distance, and headquarters movement create delay. Every campaign scenario must distinguish issue, receipt, interpretation, acknowledgment, and action.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Promotion and merit narratives {#promotion-and-merit-narratives}

**Status:** Source-critical rule. Promotion combines vacancy, seniority, patronage, performance, political trust, and battlefield need; later memoirs may simplify it to genius.

The army rewards results but also reproduces class, network, and political power. A commander can be technically gifted and still need an appointment; a subordinate's work can disappear into a superior's bulletin. Settings must preserve collective labor and the documentary chain.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Boundary to individual campaigns {#boundary-to-individual-campaigns}

**Status:** Work boundary. Named corps, divisions, and battles require their own order-of-battle and sequence owners.

This file supplies institutions, not a universal tactical script. Local terrain, allies, enemy doctrine, and supply change how the same organization behaves.

Sources: Nafziger order-of-battle collections, https://www.napoleon-series.org/nafzigger/; Fondation Napoléon chronology portal, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.
