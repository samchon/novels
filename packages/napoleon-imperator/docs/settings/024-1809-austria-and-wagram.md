**Status:** Source-supported political and military setting. Austria entered 1809 with preparation and expectations shaped by earlier defeat, French overextension, and the chance to mobilize German discontent.

An army rebuilt after a treaty has doctrine, officers, recruits, and a political purpose but not unlimited experience. Mobilization can be state policy while provincial officials, officers, civilians, and French client states receive different risks. Official alliance language does not prove local enthusiasm; fear, economic need, and dynastic loyalty can point in different directions. The setting therefore identifies the institution that decides, the unit that acts, and the province that pays or resists. “National war” is a contested political description, not a single voice available to every participant.

**Status:** Source-required domain. The Danube's branches, bridges, boats, islands, flood, debris, and artillery transport made Aspern–Essling materially distinct.

Berthier's 24 May letter to Bernadotte reports that the French were in serious action on 21–22 May, that two bridges over the main Danube arms were carried away by rising water, and that cavalry, divisional parks, and the Duke of Auerstädt's corps remained on the right bank. A direct bridging account describes a pontoon bridge begun on the night of the 19th, completed before daylight on the 20th, and the withdrawal into Lobau after the battle. These sources have different purposes and precision. A bridge can be built, damaged, swept away, repaired, or overloaded; a corps on one bank cannot support another merely because a map shows proximity. Engineers, boatmen, artillery teams, and wounded compete for capacity.

**Status:** Work canon. Wagram's mass requires separate corps objectives, ammunition flow, reserves, roads, observation, and information limits.

Mass can overwhelm a point while creating congestion elsewhere. Artillery concentration requires a road, caissons, ammunition, observation, protection, and a battery able to see the target; smoke and ground can mask a formation from one observer while exposing it to another. Napoleon's headquarters cannot receive every crisis at once. A battlefield-wide narrative therefore identifies which corps, battery, or reserve a witness can see, which order has arrived, and what tactical opportunity has passed before reinforcement reaches it. Scale is an arrangement of local capacities, not a license for omniscient command.

**Status:** Work boundary. The 1809 peace and Austrian marriage require diplomatic and family owners distinct from the battlefield.

Military victory alters bargaining space but does not itself explain a marriage, succession, or legitimacy decision.
