# 1809 Austria and Wagram

This file owns the 1809 Austrian campaign's strategic and material constraints.

## Austrian rearmament and national war {#austrian-rearmament-and-national-war}

**Status:** Source-supported. Austria enters 1809 with military preparation and political expectations shaped by earlier defeat and French overextension.

An army rebuilt after a treaty has new doctrine, officers, recruits, and political purpose but not unlimited experience. Austrian mobilization interacts with German nationalism and French client states. The setting must separate official alliance language from local enthusiasm or fear, and must distinguish a state decision from the conduct of every province, officer, and civilian.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Danube crossings and Aspern Essling {#danube-crossings-and-aspern-essling}

**Status:** Source-required domain. The Danube, bridges, boats, islands, flood, and artillery transport make the 1809 battles materially distinct.

A bridge can be built, damaged, swept away, or overloaded. A corps on one bank cannot support a corps on another merely because a map shows proximity. Every crossing scene must identify water level, construction, guards, boats, and the messenger route. Engineers, boatmen, pontoon stores, artillery teams, and wounded men all compete for the crossing's capacity.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Wagram mass and artillery {#wagram-mass-and-artillery}

**Status:** Work canon. Wagram's scale requires separate corps objectives, ammunition flow, reserves, and information limits.

Mass can overwhelm a point while creating congestion elsewhere. Artillery concentration requires roads, ammunition, observation, and protection. A battlefield-wide narrative must not give Napoleon a simultaneous view of every crisis. Settings must establish which batteries can see, which caissons can reach them, which formations are masked by smoke or ground, and which orders arrive after the tactical opportunity has passed.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon's Grande Armée” (1), https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Schönbrunn and dynastic consequence {#schonbrunn-and-dynastic-consequence}

**Status:** Work boundary. The 1809 peace and Austrian marriage require diplomatic and family owners.

Military victory alters treaty terms, territorial transfers, and Napoleon's dynastic strategy. The battlefield result does not itself explain the marriage; diplomacy, succession, legitimacy, and European balance must be staged separately. A treaty's signature, ratification, implementation, and local cost are different events.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology portal, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Campaign roads and imperial overstretch {#campaign-roads-and-imperial-overstretch}

**Status:** Source-required domain. French concentration in 1809 depends on roads, garrisons, allied contingents, river crossings, and the time needed to convert occupation into a field army.

The army's strength at headquarters is not strength at the point of contact. Detachments guard depots and cities; allied troops have separate political constraints; rain and damaged roads delay guns and food. Later scenes must preserve the order in which reports, reinforcements, and supplies arrive, rather than treating the campaign map as an instantaneous command display.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Aspern loss and operational learning {#aspern-loss-and-operational-learning}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. Aspern-Essling's shock can inform later preparations without being reduced to a single lesson or guaranteed correction.

Commanders may attribute failure to bridges, enemy fire, timing, terrain, or subordinate decisions, and different institutions may learn different things. The settings layer must distinguish a contemporary report from a later explanation. The narrative may show a revised bridge plan or artillery practice only when a source or explicit work decision connects it to the earlier experience.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Treaty transfer and civilian administration {#treaty-transfer-and-civilian-administration}

**Status:** Work boundary. Territorial transfer changes taxes, military recruitment, property, courts, and local allegiance over time.

A peace document names governments, but soldiers, clerks, landlords, clergy, and villagers experience the transfer through new seals, uniforms, levies, and claims. Settings must not treat a line on a treaty map as immediate social integration. Each later storyline should identify the official who administers a change and the people who bear or negotiate its cost.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon chronology portal, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.
