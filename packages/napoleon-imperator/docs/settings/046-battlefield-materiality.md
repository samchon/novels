# Battlefield Materiality

This file owns the physical mechanics that make campaigns executable and perceptible.

## Ground weather and visibility {#ground-weather-and-visibility}

**Status:** Work canon. Terrain, season, light, mist, rain, smoke, and roads determine what units can see, hear, carry, and reach.

A map does not provide a witness's horizon. Settings must identify slope, surface, water, buildings, vegetation, weather, and the observer's position before assigning movement or knowledge. Visibility can be limited without limiting sound, and sound can be confused without being absent.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Formation movement and congestion {#formation-movement-and-congestion}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Infantry, cavalry, artillery, wagons, ambulances, and prisoners move at different speeds and require space.

A road or bridge can become a tactical obstacle when units, guns, civilians, and wounded compete for it. Later scenes must identify formation, frontage, turning space, order, halt, and physical obstruction rather than treating an army as a moving symbol.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon's Grande Armée” (1), https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Fire ammunition and protection {#fire-ammunition-and-protection}

**Status:** Work canon. Musketry, artillery, powder, shot, cover, ammunition wagons, and resupply govern what a formation can sustain.

Settings must distinguish a unit's nominal armament from serviceable ammunition, line of fire, command, and reload conditions. Smoke, walls, villages, and friendly troops can mask a target or create danger. Precision ammunition or casualty claims require direct support.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon's Grande Armée” (1), https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Wounded prisoners and aftermath {#wounded-prisoners-and-aftermath}

**Status:** Source-critical. Battle consequences include evacuation, capture, burial, medical triage, missing persons, and civilian labor.

A casualty count depends on category, time, source, and whether missing men are later found. Settings must identify who collects wounded, which route remains open, who guards prisoners, and what report counts. The field does not end when firing stops.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Boundary to campaign-specific battle owners {#boundary-to-campaign-specific-battle-owners}

**Status:** Work boundary. Austerlitz and each later battle require their own terrain, order, source, and sequence settings.

This file supplies executable physical mechanics. Downstream units must cite the ground, unit, weapon, weather, time, and observer they use, and must not borrow precise tactical facts from generic battlefield language.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
