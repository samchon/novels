**Status:** Source-supported for the army's shortages and Napoleon's use of speed and supply; exact strengths vary by return.

The Army of Italy begins exhausted, short of uniforms, ammunition, food, horses, and reliable transport. A proclamation promising glory and resources does not fill a magazine. Every march consumes shoes, animals, cartridges, and time; requisition changes civilian relations and can produce resistance. Scenes must give units uneven readiness rather than a uniform “French army.”

Speed is a system of roads, guides, staff work, food, bridges, weather, and marching discipline. A column that arrives first may be hungry or dispersed. The army's success yields captured material and negotiating power, but extraction can alienate civilians and allies. Napoleon's dispatches frame victories for the Directory; soldiers and local populations experience them differently. Later settings must preserve this dual ledger and avoid presenting bulletin language as neutral fact.
