## Prefects, ministries, and information flow {#prefects-ministries-and-information-flow}

**Status:** Source-supported for centralized administration; exact departmental implementation requires local records.

Napoleon's state governs through ministries, prefects, police, courts, tax offices, military administrations, and published decrees. Information travels upward in reports and downward in orders, with delay, selection, and distortion. A central decision is only effective when a local official has staff, money, coercive capacity, and a road or courier network.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Civil Code and equality before law {#civil-code-and-equality-before-law}

**Status:** Source-supported for codification and legal centralization; the claim of equality must be shown alongside exclusions and enforcement.

The Civil Code standardizes property, contract, family authority, and civil status while entrenching gender hierarchy and limiting women's legal independence. The novel must not describe codification as either total liberation or mere fraud. A legal article has different effects in a notary's office, a peasant household, a merchant dispute, and a colonial territory.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic legal collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Concordat, religion, and state control {#concordat-religion-and-state-control}

**Status:** Source-supported for the Concordat's institutional settlement; local religious practice and resistance remain separate owners.

Religious reconciliation supplies public order and clerical administration while preserving state control through appointments and articles. A church scene must distinguish belief, ceremony, property, and political obedience. The Concordat does not erase non-Catholic citizens or revolutionary memory.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Police, censorship, and the cost of order {#police-censorship-and-the-cost-of-order}

**Status:** Work canon grounded in administrative centralization. Stability and repression must remain simultaneous states.

Police files, censorship, surveillance, prison, and controlled newspapers allow the regime to manage threats and public narratives. A clean order report may conceal fear or noncompliance. Scenes must show who records information, who is watched, who can appeal, and what happens when the state misclassifies a person.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Property contract and household authority {#property-contract-and-household-authority}

**Status:** Source-supported for codified civil administration; local disputes require notarial and court records.

The Code operates through documents: identity, marriage, inheritance, contract, title, debt, and court judgment. Its abstraction becomes physical when a widow loses control of property, a child inherits, a creditor seizes goods, or a notary translates law into a household's future. The novel must show who can read, pay, sign, appeal, and enforce.

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.
