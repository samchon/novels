**Status:** Source-critical. Historical medicine contains observation, theory, practice, hierarchy, and uncertainty that must not be rewritten as modern diagnosis.

A doctor records symptoms through contemporary categories and limited instruments. Settings must distinguish observation, treatment, prognosis, and later interpretation. The manuscript may render bodily experience without asserting a disputed cause as settled fact.

**Status:** Work canon. Care depends on sites, surgeons, orderlies, water, instruments, transport, shelter, and command protection.

A wounded soldier can be treated, moved, abandoned, captured, or returned to duty. Settings must identify the route, staff, supplies, triage, and authority at each stage. A hospital is an institution with queues and capacity, not a timeless refuge.

The settings graph must include dependants, pensions, prostheses, domestic care, and social stigma where sources support them. A survivor's body is not merely a symbol of heroism or loss; it has changed capabilities and costs.

**Status:** Work canon. Water, food, crowding, insects, season, hygiene, and movement shape disease independently of battle.

Reports may classify an epidemic differently from modern medicine. Settings must identify location, symptoms as recorded, available prevention, and the institution responsible. Later scenes must not turn a population's illness into a moral judgment or a precise diagnosis without evidence.
