# Tilsit and Russian Partnership

This file owns the 1807 encounter and its unstable Franco-Russian partnership.

**Status:** Source-supported. Friedland's military outcome created leverage but did not dictate every treaty clause or erase separate Russian and French information.

The defeated army, diplomatic representatives, French command, and courts received different reports. A battlefield report arrived before the treaty; a victory could be framed to strengthen bargaining, while the legal authority to sign belonged to plenipotentiaries and ratifying governments. The setting preserves the sequence from battle result to report, armistice, meeting, article, signature, and implementation. Military reputation is leverage, not a signed clause.

**Status:** Source-critical. The Tilsit meeting is documented as ceremony and diplomacy, while later accounts heighten its theatrical intimacy.

The Niemen raft, guards, interpreters, protocol, and public spectacle create a controlled encounter. The 7 July 1807 secret France–Russia treaty contains its own alliance commitments, while the 9 July France–Prussia treaty records a different public legal instrument and territorial conditions.
