# Tilsit and Russian Partnership

This file owns the 1807 encounter and its unstable Franco-Russian partnership.

## Friedland and negotiation leverage {#friedland-and-negotiation-leverage}

**Status:** Source-supported. Friedland's military outcome creates leverage but does not dictate every treaty clause.

The defeated army, the emperor's diplomatic representatives, and the French command possess different information. A battlefield report arrives before the treaty; a victory can be exaggerated to strengthen bargaining. The novel must preserve that sequence and keep military reputation separate from the legal authority to sign.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## The raft meeting and staged intimacy {#the-raft-meeting-and-staged-intimacy}

**Status:** Source-critical. The Tilsit meeting is documented as ceremony and diplomacy, while later accounts heighten its theatrical intimacy.

The river, raft, guards, interpreters, protocol, and public spectacle create a controlled encounter. A private exchange must be sourced; proximity does not prove agreement. The setting distinguishes gesture, treaty promise, secret article, and later legend, and it records whose memoir or official account supplies each detail.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Treaty text and secret arrangements {#treaty-text-and-secret-arrangements}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Public articles, private understandings, ratification, and implementation are separate evidence objects.

An emperor's spoken promise cannot be treated as a signed article, and a signed article does not specify every local consequence. Later documents must identify the text, language, signatory, date, and audience involved. Where the record is incomplete, the narrative should show negotiation pressure and uncertainty rather than inventing a precise private bargain.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Partition and dependent states {#partition-and-dependent-states}

**Status:** Work canon. Tilsit reorganizes territory and creates dependent political arrangements whose populations experience new administrations.

Maps and treaties assign sovereignty, but officials, taxes, armies, and local elites implement it. A client state is not a puppet with no agency; it has institutions and interests constrained by French power. Settings must identify the new ruler, the administrative language, the garrison or military obligation, and the people who collect revenue or resist the transfer.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Partnership and competing imperial interests {#partnership-and-competing-imperial-interests}

**Status:** Source-supported. The Franco-Russian partnership contains cooperation against Britain and incompatible continental interests.

Diplomatic warmth can coexist with economic friction, Polish questions, Ottoman interests, and mutual military suspicion. The relationship must deteriorate through accumulated decisions, not one invented betrayal. Each government maintains its own army, court, treasury, and information system even while an official communiqué describes friendship.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Economic enforcement and Russian exposure {#economic-enforcement-and-russian-exposure}

**Status:** Work boundary. Russian participation in the Continental System creates domestic costs that require commercial, maritime, and political owners.

The policy reaches ports, merchants, customs officials, consumers, and nobles differently. A Russian promise at the summit does not prove that every port can enforce it or that every class benefits. Later settings must establish which cargo, route, law, and local authority turn a diplomatic commitment into a household price or a smuggling opportunity.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Polish and Ottoman questions {#polish-and-ottoman-questions}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. Polish hopes and Ottoman interests constrain the partnership without reducing either to a French or Russian instrument.

Diplomatic actors may hear the same treaty as promise, threat, or postponement. The settings graph must identify whose sovereignty is being discussed, which document carries the claim, and which government can implement it. Later narratives must avoid giving Napoleon, Alexander, Polish representatives, or Ottoman officials a single shared intention merely because they stand near the same negotiation.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Partnership's contradiction and 1812 boundary {#partnerships-contradiction-and-1812-boundary}

**Status:** Work boundary. The Franco-Russian breakdown requires its own diplomatic and campaign setting; this file supplies the relationship's promises and structural contradictions.

No later scene may locate the rupture in one invented betrayal. The partnership hands forward signed arrangements, economic strain, territorial ambiguity, military precaution, and incompatible imperial interests. Storyline owners must show which actor learns what, when, and through which channel before assigning motive or inevitability.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.
