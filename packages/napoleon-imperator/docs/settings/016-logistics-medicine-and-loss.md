# Logistics Medicine and Loss

This file owns the recurring material systems beneath campaign spectacle.

## Food water forage and magazines {#food-water-forage-and-magazines}

**Status:** Work canon. Armies move on grain, meat, water, forage, carts, magazines, and requisition; captured territory is not an infinite warehouse.

Staff officers must locate supplies, protect them, issue them, and account for spoilage or theft. A river can transport grain and block a column; a town can feed an army once and starve afterward. Every campaign inherits this resource state.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Horses artillery and transport {#horses-artillery-and-transport}

**Status:** Historical principle. Cavalry, guns, ambulances, and baggage compete for horses and roads.

Loss of horses can disable a battery without losing a cannon. Mud, snow, bridges, gradients, and enemy fire determine whether a train follows. Scenarios must track limbers, caissons, remounts, and repair rather than teleporting artillery into a battle.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Wounded disease and hospitals {#wounded-disease-and-hospitals}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Wounds, epidemics, exposure, and medical evacuation alter effective strength and family life.

A casualty is not merely a number: he may lie on the field, reach a dressing station, be moved by cart, die in a hospital, return to duty, or disappear from records. Surgeons need instruments, water, assistants, shelter, and time. Campaign writing must avoid clean casualty transitions.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Loss reports and uncertainty {#loss-reports-and-uncertainty}

**Status:** Source-critical rule. Strength and casualty totals use incompatible definitions across returns, bulletins, memoirs, and modern historians.

Every number needs unit, date, category, and source. “Present,” “effective,” “under arms,” “missing,” and “dead” are not synonyms. If a source gives only an estimate, preserve the estimate and disagreement rather than manufacturing precision.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.

## Food water forage and magazine distance {#food-water-forage-and-magazine-distance}

**Status:** Work canon. An army's operational strength depends on where supplies are, when they arrive, and who can guard and issue them.

Grain, meat, water, forage, powder, shoes, and carts move at different speeds. A magazine can be full while a forward battalion is hungry; a requisition can feed an army once and impoverish a town. Every campaign must show depot, road, issue, and consumption rather than calling territory an unlimited supply.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Horses guns and repair labor {#horses-guns-and-repair-labor}

**Status:** Source-supported material constraint. Artillery and cavalry lose effectiveness through horse sickness, broken harness, damaged wheels, and absent artificers before they lose a formal unit designation.

Drivers, wheelwrights, smiths, armorers, and veterinary workers are part of combat power. A gun stuck behind a bridge cannot support a battalion that has already crossed. Scenarios must track limbers, caissons, remounts, repair time, and the road capacity that connects them.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Wounded evacuation and medical hierarchy {#wounded-evacuation-and-medical-hierarchy}

**Status:** Source-required domain. A casualty moves through field dressing, ambulance, hospital, convalescence, return, death, or disappearance from the record.

The route requires orderlies, carts, water, surgeons, instruments, shelter, and protection. A commander may know a general is wounded while not knowing how many enlisted men remain in a ditch. Medical care changes the army's next-day strength and families' futures; it is not a decorative aftermath.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Casualty arithmetic and report conflict {#casualty-arithmetic-and-report-conflict}

**Status:** Source-critical. Returns, bulletins, memoirs, and modern estimates use different definitions of killed, wounded, missing, prisoner, and present strength.

Every number must retain unit, date, category, and source. “Losses” may combine categories that cannot be added safely. If two sources disagree, the setting records both standings and chooses a bounded narrative claim rather than inventing a precise total.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.
