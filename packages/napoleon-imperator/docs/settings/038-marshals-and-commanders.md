**Status:** Work canon. A marshal combines legal appointment, military command, household wealth, reputation, and a personal relationship with the emperor.

The title does not guarantee competence in every task. A marshal may command troops, negotiate, report, patronize, or fail under a different pressure. Settings must name the office, force, mission, authority, and date rather than turning a reputation into a permanent trait.

**Status:** Source-critical. Personal courage, tactical skill, administrative reliability, and political loyalty are different capacities.

A marshal may comply while reserving an objection, delay a movement, or report a fact in a politically safe form. Settings must establish the communication channel and the cost of disagreement. A later memoir's claim that a commander “knew” the emperor's intention cannot replace the order, report, or observed action.
