# Marshals and Commanders

This file owns the command personalities, professional roles, and limits of Napoleon's senior officers.

<!-- principles/common.md#purpose-fit principles/common.md#layer-boundary principles/common.md#work-language -->

## Marshal as office and person {#marshal-as-office-and-person}

**Status:** Work canon. A marshal combines legal appointment, military command, household wealth, reputation, and a personal relationship with the emperor.

The title does not guarantee competence in every task. A marshal may command troops, negotiate, report, patronize, or fail under a different pressure. Settings must name the office, force, mission, authority, and date rather than turning a reputation into a permanent trait.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Berthier and the staff machine {#berthier-and-the-staff-machine}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Berthier's value lies in staff organization, order transmission, maps, schedules, and the conversion of imperial intention into executable instructions.

The chief of staff is neither a passive clerk nor an omniscient commander. He receives, edits, copies, prioritizes, and dispatches information under time pressure. Later scenes must distinguish Napoleon's thought, Berthier's written order, an aide's carriage, and a subordinate's receipt.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Corps command and mission change {#corps-command-and-mission-change}

**Status:** Work canon. A corps commander acts through subordinate divisions, roads, attachments, artillery, reports, and a mission that can change before the original order is complete.

The setting must identify what force is present, what ground is assigned, what the commander knows, and what can be changed without a new order. A corps may be nominally strong while detached units, fatigue, terrain, and ammunition reduce its usable power.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon's Grande Armée” (1), https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Technical competence and courage {#technical-competence-and-courage}

**Status:** Source-critical. Personal courage, tactical skill, administrative reliability, and political loyalty are different capacities.

A commander can be brave and poor at logistics, loyal and slow, brilliant in defense and weak in negotiation. Later narratives must avoid the single adjective that explains every decision. Each competence requires a material test: a march, bridge, report, formation, supply problem, or political exchange.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Rivalry obedience and correction {#rivalry-obedience-and-correction}

**Status:** Work canon. Senior command depends on obedience, rivalry, trust, fear, and the ability to correct an order without destroying the chain of command.

A marshal may comply while reserving an objection, delay a movement, or report a fact in a politically safe form. Settings must establish the communication channel and the cost of disagreement. A later memoir's claim that a commander “knew” the emperor's intention cannot replace the order, report, or observed action.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Command under defeat and regime change {#command-under-defeat-and-regime-change}

**Status:** Source-required domain. A commander's loyalty and authority change when supplies fail, the capital falls, the emperor abdicates, or a new government issues orders.

The same officer can be legally commissioned, politically exposed, and physically unable to control troops. Later settings must distinguish oath, action, survival, property, and retrospective justification. Defeat does not make every prior decision treasonous, and restoration does not erase the officer's earlier institutional obligations.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Boundary to commander-specific settings {#boundary-to-commander-specific-settings}

**Status:** Work boundary. Individual marshals, generals, aides, and allied commanders require separate historical owners and dated source chains.

This file provides command mechanics. Downstream units must identify the commander, office, force, mission, information state, and material constraint they use; they may not import a generalized marshal personality into a scene without evidence.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon correspondence, https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.
