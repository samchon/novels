## Revolutionary religious rupture {#revolutionary-religious-rupture}

**Status:** Source-supported. Revolution transformed church property, clergy allegiance, worship, and local identity; no single French experience represents all regions.

Confiscation and constitutional clergy create legal and personal choices. A parishioner may obey a non-juring priest, attend a public ceremony, hide a priest, or accept a new civil official. The novel must distinguish belief from institutional obedience and must identify who owns a building, records a marriage, collects a tithe, or enforces a decree.

Sources: Gallica, revolutionary collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Concordat and Organic Articles {#concordat-and-organic-articles}

**Status:** Source-supported for the Consular settlement; local implementation and non-Catholic treatment require separate owners.

The Concordat restores public religious structures while preserving state supervision. Appointment, salary, ceremony, property, and police oversight remain intertwined. A church reconciliation is therefore both spiritual and administrative. Settings must keep the signed settlement, the implementing regulations, and parish-level practice as separate evidence.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Clergy property and civil records {#clergy-property-and-civil-records}

**Status:** Work canon. Religious policy becomes visible through appointments, salaries, buildings, registers, schools, marriage, burial, and confiscated property.

A bishop's public obedience does not settle a village's practice. Officials must translate policy into registers, repairs, ceremonies, and police decisions. The settings graph must identify which institution can certify a marriage, bury a body, teach children, or claim a building, and which families lose or gain property through the change.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, revolutionary collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Religion in army and empire {#religion-in-army-and-empire}

**Status:** Work canon. Chaplains, rites, burial, oaths, and local religious law affect soldiers and occupied populations.

An army's religious practice varies by regiment and territory. Catholic restoration does not make Jewish, Protestant, Muslim, Orthodox, or local communities identical subjects. The settings graph must identify the institution and the people subject to it, and must not infer private belief from a public oath.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Non-Catholic communities and legal pluralism {#non-catholic-communities-and-legal-pluralism}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Religious toleration, civil equality, communal authority, and local custom must be specified for the community and jurisdiction involved.

An empire-wide legal phrase can meet different courts, languages, and inherited privileges. Settings must name who interprets the rule, who keeps communal records, and whether the policy changes property, marriage, education, or public office. No generic religious reaction may stand in for Jewish, Protestant, Muslim, Orthodox, or local evidence.

Sources: Gallica, revolutionary collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Concordat as political compromise {#concordat-as-political-compromise}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. A public reconciliation can coexist with surveillance, resistance, private dissent, and unresolved property claims.

The state may use religious ceremony to stabilize authority while retaining appointment and policing powers. Clergy may accept office without accepting every imperial aim; parishioners may attend without believing; former owners may litigate without restoring a building. Storyline owners must preserve these mixed motives and the limits of the sources.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Sacred space occupation and local worlds {#sacred-space-occupation-and-local-worlds}

**Status:** Work boundary. Egypt, Iberia, Italy, and the German territories each require localized religious and legal settings.

The empire's policy travels through officials but meets different clergy, sacred spaces, languages, and customary authority. A church, mosque, synagogue, chapel, cemetery, or shrine is a material place with custodians, objects, schedules, and jurisdiction. Later scenes must show who can enter, alter, guard, or close it rather than using a generic “church reaction.”

Sources: Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Boundary to localized religious settings {#boundary-to-localized-religious-settings}

**Status:** Work boundary. This file supplies policy mechanics; regional religious histories belong to their campaign and occupation owners.

Every later use must identify the local authority, community, law, and evidence chain. The settings layer hands forward the tension between public settlement and private belief without declaring that one Concordat made every region identical.
