# Court Ceremony and Household

This file owns court as an institution of access, rank, gender, and performance.

## From Consular residence to imperial court {#from-consular-residence-to-imperial-court}

**Status:** Source-supported. Court develops through offices, apartments, audiences, uniforms, calendars, and a hierarchy of access.

The emperor's household is an administrative machine as well as a social scene. An invitation, an usher, a chamberlain, a guard, and a written appointment determine who can approach power. Proximity does not guarantee influence.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Ceremony as political technology {#ceremony-as-political-technology}

**Status:** Work canon. Coronations, receptions, marriages, medals, and military displays make hierarchy visible and invite public interpretation.

Ceremony allocates precedence, money, costume, space, and witness. A slight in rank can have diplomatic consequences; a splendid display can conceal fiscal strain. Scenarios must stage servants and preparation alongside the public image.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Household staff and information {#household-staff-and-information}

**Status:** Source-critical. Secretaries, valets, ladies, physicians, cooks, guards, and visitors know different fragments of the sovereign's life.

Staff are not invisible scenery. They carry letters, hear quarrels, manage rooms, and shape access, but their knowledge is partial and their testimony later mediated. The novel must not make a servant an omniscient narrator merely because he is nearby.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.
## Boundary to private intimacy {#boundary-to-private-intimacy}

**Status:** Work boundary. Bedroom, medical, and family scenes require individual primary or critical sources.

Court ceremony may reveal public status while hiding private feeling. Later memoirs and gossip need attribution, and a scene must identify whether a statement is witnessed, remembered, or invented for the fiction.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The correspondence of Napoleon,” https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/.
