# Continental System and Economic War

This file owns the blockade as an administrative, commercial, and coercive system.

## Blockade decree and enforcement gap {#blockade-decree-and-enforcement-gap}

**Status:** Source-supported. A decree prohibiting British commerce requires customs officials, ports, patrols, courts, informers, and cooperation from allies and occupied states.

The Continental System is not a wall. Smuggling, neutral shipping, forged papers, corrupt officials, local scarcity, and diplomatic exceptions create gaps. A central order can be obeyed publicly and evaded privately. The novel must show who profits and who pays, and must distinguish the legal text from the enforcement capacity available at a particular port or frontier.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic legal collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Ports merchants and contraband {#ports-merchants-and-contraband}

**Status:** Work canon. Economic war is lived in warehouses, customs houses, ships, credit networks, and household prices.

A merchant facing confiscation may bribe an inspector, reroute cargo, appeal to a consul, or stop trading. A port town experiences unemployment and scarcity alongside official patriotism. Scenes must track paperwork, currency, ships, and the physical risk of concealment. Contraband is not an atmosphere: it requires a buyer, a carrier, a route, a document, and a person willing to accept the penalty.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Allied compliance and political resentment {#allied-compliance-and-political-resentment}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Allies enforce French economic policy unevenly because their own states, merchants, and consumers bear the cost.

Coercion can produce compliance and make an ally less reliable. The policy affects Russia, Spain, the German states, Italy, and the Netherlands differently. A diplomatic dispute over commerce can become a military crisis without a single dramatic insult. The setting must separate an ally's treaty promise, its customs practice, its local exemptions, and the central government's ability to discover or punish noncompliance.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Customs bureaucracy and coercive routine {#customs-bureaucracy-and-coercive-routine}

**Status:** Work canon. A blockade becomes real through forms, seals, inspections, warehouses, confiscation records, courts, and paid personnel.

The state needs classifications for legal cargo, neutral cargo, seized cargo, and perishable goods. Inspectors work under pressure from merchants, superiors, soldiers, and hunger. A seizure changes a household or company before a distant ministry hears of it. Later scenes must identify who has authority to inspect, who can appeal, where goods are stored, and which ledger records the loss.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic legal collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/.

## Credit prices and civilian scarcity {#credit-prices-and-civilian-scarcity}

**Status:** Source-required domain. Economic warfare alters credit, wages, prices, employment, and household nutrition unevenly across regions and classes.

An official ban does not explain a loaf's price or a worker's unemployment by itself. Settings must establish supply, transport, substitute goods, seasonal harvests, currency, and the power to compel or evade purchase. A wealthy merchant and a dock laborer's family meet the same policy through different risks. The manuscript may render shortage through queues, substitutions, debt, and illness only when those material links are established.

Sources: Archives nationales, https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Neutral flags and maritime pressure {#neutral-flags-and-maritime-pressure}

**Status:** Work boundary. Neutral shipping, naval power, licenses, and seizures require distinct maritime owners rather than a land blockade shorthand.

A neutral flag can be legal, disputed, forged, or politically tolerated. A port official may face a ship's papers while a naval patrol faces its cargo at sea. Settings must identify jurisdiction, weather, escort, cargo, and the document that claims legality. Continental policy cannot be described as complete control of the sea when enforcement depends on vessels the French do not command.

Sources: Gallica, Napoleonic legal collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte; Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/.

## Enforcement failure and diplomatic rupture {#enforcement-failure-and-diplomatic-rupture}

**Status:** Source-critical boundary. The system's failure is cumulative and political: revenue loss, smuggling, allied resentment, naval limits, and divergent strategic aims interact over time.

No later scene may state that one decree alone caused a war. A ministry may describe compliance while local records show evasion; a ruler may protest commerce while preparing military action; a French reprisal may solve one port's problem and deepen another state's hostility. Storyline owners must preserve the separate chains of economic pressure, diplomatic communication, and military preparation.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.

## Boundary to the 1812 rupture {#boundary-to-the-1812-rupture}

**Status:** Work boundary. The Franco-Russian breakdown requires its own diplomatic and campaign setting; this file supplies economic pressure and enforcement mechanics.

The settings graph hands forward the effects without deciding every motive: Russian commercial interests, French strategic demands, allied obligations, customs practice, and public costs may converge without one actor possessing the whole causal picture. Later documents must cite which pressure they use and must not retroactively make the Continental System a single inevitable cause.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, biography, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/.
