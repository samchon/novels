## Expedition purpose and secrecy {#expedition-purpose-and-secrecy}

**Status:** Source-supported for the expedition's military, commercial, and imperial objectives; exact private motives remain contested.

Egypt offers a route toward British India, a base in the eastern Mediterranean, and an arena for scientific and cultural claims. Secrecy is operationally limited by ship movement, provisioning, officers, foreign observers, and the need to issue orders to a large force. A plan can be secret at Paris while obvious to a port worker or enemy scout.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon Bonaparte,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, *Correspondance de Napoléon Ier*, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Fleet, transports, and disease {#fleet-transports-and-disease}

**Status:** Work canon grounded in expedition logistics. Troops, horses, guns, water, food, engineers, scientists, and animals compete for ship capacity; disease and heat alter effective strength.

An embarkation scene must track manifests, loading order, fresh water, ventilation, horses, powder safety, and weather. At sea, a unit may be present on paper but weakened by sickness, separation, or lost equipment. On land, wells, shade, grain, local transport, and medical care determine whether a victory can be exploited.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “The life of Napoleon I: a timeline for 6 years old +,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/chrono-consulat-1er-empire/; Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon’s ‘Grande Armée’ (1),” https://www.napoleon.org/en/young-historians/napodoc/napoleons-grande-armee-1/.

## Science, archaeology, and colonial power {#science-archaeology-and-colonial-power}

**Status:** Source-supported for the expedition's scholars and scientific work; contested imperial claims must not erase military occupation or Egyptian voices.

The expedition carries scholars and instruments as well as weapons. Scientific observation can produce valuable records while serving imperial possession and propaganda. The setting gives Egyptian residents agency, religious institutions, merchants, and local authorities; they are not scenery for French discovery. Any scientific scene must identify who funds, permits, controls, and benefits from the work.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon Bonaparte,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, Napoleonic collections, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.

## Isolation and return decision {#isolation-and-return-decision}

**Status:** Work boundary. The fleet's defeat, Syrian operations, and Napoleon's return to France require separate event owners; the strategic state is isolation from metropolitan decision-making.

Messages travel slowly or not at all. Napoleon must make decisions with incomplete news from the Directory, Europe, the fleet, and local opponents. Returning to France is a political gamble, not merely a voyage. The later storyline must show who knows of the decision and what authority is left behind.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon chronology, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/; Fondation Napoléon, *Correspondance générale*, https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/chronologies/chronology-of-the-general-correspondence-of-napoleon-bonaparte-volume-one-the-learnings-1784-1797/.

## Mamluk power and local negotiation {#mamluk-power-and-local-negotiation}

**Status:** Source-required domain. French descriptions of Egypt must be cross-checked against Egyptian institutions, Ottoman authority, and local actors.

The expedition enters an existing political and commercial world. Mamluk elites, Ottoman officials, religious scholars, merchants, rural communities, and urban residents possess different interests and information. A French proclamation cannot make those interests disappear. The scenario must show interpreters, taxes, hostages, markets, religious authority, and the limits of French knowledge.

Sources: Fondation Napoléon, “Napoleon Bonaparte,” https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/biographies/napoleon-bonaparte/; Gallica, https://gallica.bnf.fr/html/und/histoire/napoleon-bonaparte.
