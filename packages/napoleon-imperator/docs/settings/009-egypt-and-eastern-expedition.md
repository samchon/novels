**Status:** Source-supported for the expedition's military, commercial, and imperial objectives; exact private motives remain contested.

Egypt offers a route toward British India, a base in the eastern Mediterranean, and an arena for scientific and cultural claims. Secrecy is operationally limited by ship movement, provisioning, officers, foreign observers, and the need to issue orders to a large force. A plan can be secret at Paris while obvious to a port worker or enemy scout.

**Status:** Work canon grounded in expedition logistics. A manifest lists intended men and matériel; it does not prove that every person, horse, gun, scientist, or crate landed together.

Loading order reflects danger and priority. Powder needs protection from heat and sparks; horses need ventilation and water; artillery requires carriages, tools, shot, and trained crews; scholars need instruments and cases. A storm, delay, illness, or transfer can separate an item from its unit before the first landing.

Once maritime support is lost or threatened, every decision changes: reinforcements cannot arrive on schedule, wounded cannot be evacuated easily, and news from Europe becomes stale. Napoleon's later return is a political gamble made under isolation, not a routine transfer of command.
