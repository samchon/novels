# Health and First Aid

This file fixes ordinary health, prevention, first aid, referral, and recovery constraints around school and civic work. It is not medical advice and does not diagnose any character.

## Health information boundary

**Status:** Work decision — privacy canon.

The club records functional needs—water, rest, medication timing, quiet, seating, food restriction, communication aid—rather than diagnoses unless an authorized host requires a specific field. A participant may explain what helps without explaining why. Health information is restricted to the responsible owner.

Peers do not speculate, photograph treatment, or share a disclosure as group context. An emergency contact receives only the information needed for the immediate handoff. A school or medical professional owns clinical interpretation.

## Prevention before activity

**Status:** Work decision — safety canon.

Before an activity, the host checks weather, route, duration, food and water, rest, equipment, adult coverage, communication, access, and stop conditions. The assessment is proportionate: a paper survey and a night route require different checks, but neither is exempt from basic safety.

Participants can ask questions and decline. Prevention is revised when the actual room, crowd, temperature, or route differs from the plan. A signed form does not make a dangerous condition safe.

## Water, food, and rest

**Status:** Work decision — embodied resource.

Water stations, toilets, shade, seating, and food access follow `050` and the weather conditions in `070`. Long tasks include pauses before symptoms or exhaustion become a crisis. A participant may eat privately, carry a personal item, or decline shared food.

The host does not force water, food, or disclosure. If someone becomes unwell, the task stops and the responsible adult assesses next steps. The club does not turn endurance into a competition.

## Heat and humidity response

**Status:** Work decision — source-backed safety application.

The Korea Meteorological Administration’s current heat safety guidance is the official public reference for heatwave precautions: [KMA heat safety guide](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do). It is public safety guidance, not a fictional diagnosis or a substitute for a medical professional.

Haneul’s work decision is to shorten outdoor tasks, provide shade and water, monitor participants, and stop when a person reports heat illness signs or the responsible adult judges conditions unsafe. A forecast or warning is checked through the current official channel; a hot feeling alone is recorded as an observation, not a diagnosis.

## Cold, rain, and wet clothing

**Status:** Work decision — environmental health canon.

Cold, wind, rain, wet socks, slippery surfaces, and dark early evenings change comfort and route safety. The host supplies a dry waiting option, reasonable layers or rescheduling, and a way to protect paper and devices. No one is required to remain outdoors to prove reliability.

A participant who becomes chilled or unwell is moved to a safe indoor host and referred to an adult. Wet clothing is not photographed for humour. The route assessment is repeated after rain because surface and visibility conditions change.

## First-aid kit and trained response

**Status:** Work decision — equipment boundary.

The host identifies first-aid kit location, expiry checks, responsible trained adults, emergency contact route, and replenishment. Students may fetch a kit or call an adult but do not improvise treatment beyond their training. A kit is not a medical facility.

The record states observable injury, time, location, action taken, handoff, and follow-up owner. Medication is handled by its responsible person or authorized process. A peer’s consent and privacy are respected while immediate danger receives prompt adult response.

## Emergency call and handoff

**Status:** Work decision — immediate safety canon.

When emergency help is needed, the caller gives location, entrance, access obstacle, number of people, observable condition, and callback. One person meets responders if safe; another stays with the affected person within their competence. The founders do not move an injured person unless directed or immediate danger requires it.

The school, youth centre, city facility, or family adult owns the next handoff according to location. Afterward, the incident record is restricted and factual. Public updates omit identity and medical detail unless the authorized owner requires otherwise.

## Medication and personal aids

**Status:** Work decision — privacy and safety rule.

Medication, inhalers, epinephrine, glucose tools, hearing devices, glasses, mobility aids, and sensory supports remain with their owner or authorized custodian. The club does not store or administer them casually. A participant may request a reminder or accessible location without naming a diagnosis.

If an aid is lost or damaged, the host protects the person, contacts the responsible adult, and records the handoff. Borrowing an aid without permission is not helpfulness. Emergency procedures follow the authorized school or medical guidance.

## Infection, air, and smoke

**Status:** Work decision — environmental health boundary.

Poor ventilation, smoke, dust, strong scent, pollen, illness, or an indoor crowd can make a room unusable for some people. The host increases ventilation where safe, moves the activity, provides a no-penalty absence route, and follows current official or school guidance. `070` owns environmental observation; this file owns participant care.

The 2022 fire legacy remains a school facilities record, not a general claim that smoke is harmless after reconstruction. A smell, cough, or irritation is reported without diagnosing its cause. The group does not pressure a person to enter a smoky or poorly ventilated room.

## Mental distress and supportive referral

**Status:** Work decision — safeguarding canon.

A person may show panic, shutdown, grief, overload, confusion, exhaustion, or distress without a peer knowing the cause. The listener offers space, clear choices, reduced audience, and an adult referral. They do not promise secrecy about immediate danger, provide therapy, or force a disclosure.

The record uses the person’s words and observable request rather than a diagnosis. A participant may choose a quiet route, leave, contact family, or pause the project. The club’s task is safety and referral, not a dramatic cure.

## Accessibility and health accommodation

**Status:** Work decision — cross-system canon.

Health accommodation can include seat, lighting, pace, break, route, food, communication, attendance, or deadline adjustment through the responsible school or host. The request is functional and confidential. A legal dimension from `170` and physical dimensions from `050` are checked separately from the person’s preference.

An accommodation is reviewed under actual weather, noise, crowd, and fatigue. A successful trial with one person is not universal proof. The host records outcome and unresolved barrier, not a spectacle of gratitude.

## Incident review and recovery

**Status:** Work decision — continuity rule.

After an incident, the owner checks immediate care, notification, equipment, route, supervision, privacy, and whether the activity must pause. The review asks what condition allowed the risk and what practical change is needed. It does not begin with blame or a demand for a perfect memory.

Recovery may require rest, medical care, counselling, replacement equipment, route redesign, apology, or no further participation. A task resumes only under the responsible owner’s decision and current conditions. The incident record remains distinct from gossip.
