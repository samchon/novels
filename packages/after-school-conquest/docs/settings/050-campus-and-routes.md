# Campus and Routes

This file fixes the physical envelope through which Haneul Public High School, its adjacent civic spaces, and the ordinary routes between them can be imagined and later blocked. It owns surfaces, dimensions, thresholds, sight lines, noise, circulation, accessibility, and physical uncertainty. It does not grant authority, schedule rooms, approve a club, alter the 2019 flood legacy or the 2022 fire record, or decide what happens in a narrative unit. Those matters remain with `030-chronology-baseline.md`, `150-school-institutions.md`, and the game files.

All dimensions below are fictional Haneul measurements or conservative operating choices. Current Korean legal and administrative standards are evidence for the distinction between a legal minimum, a maintenance duty, and a work choice; they are not evidence that Haneul is a real campus. The principal and facilities office own daily access under 150. A later scene may use any listed space only after its physical path, threshold, supervision, and current closure condition are respected.

## Campus boundary and adjacent parcels

**Status:** Work decision for Haneul's fictional physical site, informed by current school-facility and accessibility frameworks.

Haneul Public High School occupies a roughly rectangular 180-metre east–west by 120-metre north–south parcel. The north edge meets a public two-lane street; the east edge meets a narrow municipal service lane; the south edge borders the school's fenced athletic field and a drainage swale; and the west edge meets the separate west annex yard and a low-rise residential block. The fence is a physical boundary, not a statement that the school owns the street, the market, the bus loop, or the youth centre. The main school parcel, the west annex, and the sports field are contiguous in the reader's mental map but have separate doors, surfaces, locks, and custodial responsibility.

The main three-storey school block occupies the central and eastern portion of the parcel. The west annex is a one-storey rebuilt structure connected by a covered external walkway with a 1.8-metre clear width. The gymnasium sits south-east of the main block; the auditorium occupies the north-east corner and has its own public-facing entrance. A service yard sits behind the east lane and is reached by a gate that is never part of the ordinary student route. The campus map uses north at the top, the north street as the reference edge, and metres rather than steps as its base unit.

The property line is visible through a 1.8-metre fence with two pedestrian gates and one service gate. A fence does not make every adjacent route safe: the north sidewalk, bus loop, market passage, and Seongnae flood channel are municipal or public contexts described in 030. A person can stand outside the fence and still be within the school's visual field, while a person inside the fence can lose sight of a public route behind a wall or parked vehicle. Downstream blocking must therefore name both parcel and side of boundary.

The legal school-facility frame is the School Facilities Project Promotion Act, which defines school facilities to include the school site, school buildings, gymnasium, dormitory, and meal facilities; the official National Law Information Center text is effective 2023-04-18 at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsPdfPrint.do?ancYnChk=0&bylChaChk=N&efGubun=Y&efYd=20230418&joAllCheck=Y&joEfOutPutYn=on&lsiSeq=249969&mokChaChk=N, Article 2. That source establishes scope, not Haneul's measurements. The Education Facilities Safety and Maintenance Standard, effective 2022-01-14, treats school gates and boundary facilities as maintainable safety objects at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2100000208251. Haneul's parcel, fence, and adjacency are Work decisions.

## Main gate, side gate, and public thresholds

**Status:** Work decision using source-verified access dimensions as a design floor, not a claim that the fictional campus has been legally certified.

The north main gate separates the public sidewalk from a 2.4-metre-wide pedestrian forecourt. A vehicle gate is set 4 metres east of it and is physically separated by a low kerb and a fixed bollard line; pedestrians do not share the vehicle opening as the default route. The forecourt widens to 3 metres in front of the main entrance, allowing a wheelchair user, a person carrying a box, and a person waiting for a door to pass without standing in the vehicle lane. The gate attendant's booth is west of the pedestrian path and has a clear view of the threshold without projecting into the route.

The main door is a pair of doors with one leaf providing a 0.9-metre clear opening when the other leaf is closed. The approach is level; the door swing leaves 1.5 metres of clear turning space outside and 1.4 metres inside. A short queue cannot be allowed to become a wall across the accessible line. A door held open by a wedge changes the fire and supervision condition, so it is not an assumed convenience. The side gate on the east lane has a 1.5-metre clear pedestrian opening but no public waiting area; it is a staff or escorted route and cannot substitute for the main entrance's visible threshold.

The official Act on the Promotion of Convenience for Persons with Disabilities, Older Persons, and Pregnant Women requires convenience facilities to be installed and maintained according to statutory categories; its current framework is at https://www.law.go.kr/법령/장애인ㆍ노인ㆍ임산부등의편의증진보장에관한법률#제3조 and #제8조. The official Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, amended 2023-12-11, gives the detailed door and threshold rules: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913, Appendix 1, items 6 and 7. It specifies a 0.9-metre clear door width in the relevant public-building standard, no ordinary threshold, and a 0.3-metre tactile or texture change before a main entrance. Haneul adopts those values or larger as a Work decision; the source does not certify a fictional inspection.

The threshold has three simultaneous readings. Physically, it is the line between sidewalk and school. Administratively, 150's principal or facilities process decides whether a person may cross. Narratively, a later unit can block a character at the gate, in the forecourt, at the door, or inside the lobby without pretending those are the same location. No game seal or G-P01 can turn the public threshold into club property.

## Building wings and circulation spine

**Status:** Work decision for the main block's physical layout, bounded by current egress and accessibility standards.

The main block is 72 metres east–west by 36 metres north–south, with three occupied floors over a service basement that is not an ordinary student route. A 2.4-metre-wide east–west circulation spine runs through the middle of each floor. Classrooms and seminar rooms open primarily to the north and south sides of the spine; stair cores sit near the east and west ends; the elevator and main lobby are slightly east of centre. A person entering at the north lobby can reach the central spine without crossing a classroom threshold, while a person approaching the west annex exits the main block before reaching the west stair.

The spine is not a straight sight line for its whole length. A fire door at the east third, a shallow service offset near the elevator, and a daylight break at the central stair divide it into readable segments. Each segment remains at least 1.8 metres clear in ordinary condition, leaving a 0.6-metre passing margin beyond the 1.2-metre accessibility minimum. Display boards may occupy designated wall recesses only; a chair, cart, instrument case, or wet umbrella left in the clear width is a physical obstruction, not scenery that can be ignored.

The north corridor has classroom doors and high windows; the south corridor receives mechanical-room noise near the east end and gym noise at the ground-floor south junction. A closed classroom door muffles ordinary speech, while an open door makes the spine acoustically public. The lobby's hard floor reflects footsteps and wheel noise farther than the carpeted library threshold. Later blocking must distinguish what can be seen from what can be heard: a person at the west stair cannot automatically hear a conversation at the elevator, even though both are on the same floor.

The Building Act Enforcement Decree's current Article 34 requires direct stairs or ramps so that walking distance from parts of a room to the nearest stair is within the applicable limit, and Articles 35 and 39 address refuge stairs and exits; the official National Law Information Center locator opened with the 2026-07-28 text is https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000990378. Haneul's conservative geometry keeps ordinary occupied-room paths within 30 metres of a protected stair or the lobby route, but the fictional design decision does not replace a future fire review. 150 owns the daily facilities response, not this physical description.

## Classroom wing and ordinary-room geometry

**Status:** Work decision for ordinary teaching rooms and their physical access; no class timetable or storyline assignment is owned here.

The typical classroom is 8 metres east–west by 7 metres north–south, approximately 56 square metres, with its door opening toward the circulation spine and its windows facing either the north street or the south field. A 1.2-metre clear strip runs from the door to the front teaching wall; movable desks occupy the remaining field in rows or small groups. The room's capacity is set by the school schedule and safety review, not by the fictional game. For physical blocking, the door zone, teacher wall, window line, rear storage, and any designated wheelchair position are distinct places.

Each ordinary room has one inward-facing observation panel in the door and a high window that admits daylight without giving a standing passer-by a full view of seated students. The door can be heard from the nearest two metres of spine but not reliably from the far stair when the corridor is occupied. A teacher at the front wall can see the door only by turning or using the panel reflection; a student seated behind a raised display cannot see the entire room. These limits prevent a settings description from granting omniscient physical awareness.

The floor is level with the corridor. The door's clear width is 0.9 metres, the handle centre sits between 0.8 and 0.9 metres above the floor, and a 1.2-by-1.2-metre approach zone remains free of permanent furniture. A temporary bag, instrument, or project board can reduce that zone and must be treated as a real access change. The room's 1.5-metre turning zone is reserved at the designated accessible position; two parked desks cannot be added there without changing the room's usable geometry.

The detailed accessibility source is the official Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, items 6 (doors) and 7 (corridors), amended 2023-12-11, https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. Its public-building standard specifies level thresholds, clear door width, handle height, and at least 1.2 metres for an accessible corridor, with stricter local arrangements where both sides carry rooms. Haneul chooses a 1.8-metre spine and the stated 0.9-metre doors as Work decisions. No later writer may infer that every classroom has an accessible toilet, a second exit, or a free desk unless another H2 establishes it.

## Administrative and service-side physical boundary

**Status:** Work decision separating public circulation from staff, delivery, and maintenance space; authority remains in 150.

The administrative wing occupies the east end of the ground floor. Its public counter faces the lobby through a 2.4-metre waiting bay, while records, staff workrooms, electrical rooms, and the service corridor lie behind controlled doors. The public side has two chairs, a standing counter, a 1.5-metre turning area, and a clear line to the elevator. The service side is narrower, 1.2 metres at its least, and is not an accessible public shortcut. A person waiting at the counter can see the receptionist and the notice panel but cannot see the records shelves behind the staff door.

The east service lane reaches a 3-metre loading apron outside the service entrance. Deliveries stop there; carts do not cross the main lobby unless the facilities process has cleared the route. A 1.2-metre fire-door clear width is kept around the service threshold, but the door is not a public entrance and is not assumed unlocked. The electrical and mechanical rooms are physically adjacent to the service corridor but separated by solid doors and labelled panels. Their adjacency creates noise and heat, not permission to enter.

The west side of the administrative wing has the counselor's interview room and a small accessible quiet room. The interview room's door opens to the public side but its interior is not visible from the counter; the quiet room has a frosted panel and a 1.5-metre turning space. These physical arrangements support privacy without declaring a legal confidentiality rule here. 150 owns the records officer, counselor, principal, and appeal routes. This file only prevents a later scene from placing a private meeting in the middle of the public queue or treating the service corridor as a neutral shortcut.

The Education Facilities Safety and Maintenance Standard, effective 2022-01-14, defines boundary facilities such as school gates and fences and frames inspection and maintenance obligations: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2100000208251. The standard is an administrative rule, not a fictional floor plan. Haneul's waiting bay, service apron, door separations, and clear widths are Work decisions selected to keep the public, staff, delivery, and emergency readings physically legible.

## West annex and audiovisual-booth envelope

**Status:** Work decision physically consistent with 030's 2022 fire legacy and 150's twelve-seat, two-breaker, key, and supervision constraints.

The rebuilt west annex is a one-storey rectangle 18 metres east–west by 12 metres north–south. Its covered connection to the main block meets the annex at the east wall; the public entrance is on the north side, and a facilities-only service door is on the south-west corner. The audiovisual booth occupies a 6-by-5-metre room in the north-east quarter. Its wired-glass door opens to a 1.8-metre annex corridor, with a 1.5-by-1.5-metre turning area outside. The room has a twelve-seat maximum as already fixed in 150, not twelve seats plus standing viewers or equipment cases.

The two circuit breakers are in a labelled, locked cabinet in the south service wall, outside the seat field. Batteries and charging equipment are kept on the designated north-west equipment bench, separated from the fire-rated paper archive cabinet in the west storage alcove. No charging layout may block the 0.9-metre door, the 1.2-metre internal clear strip, or the 1.5-metre turning space. The wired-glass panel gives a partial view of movement and occupancy but not a readable view of every paper or screen.

The booth has two acoustic conditions. With the door shut, ordinary speech is contained enough that a person in the annex corridor hears only a softened voice or equipment tone; with the door open, the corridor becomes a shared sound field. The west exterior wall receives less pedestrian noise than the north entrance, while rain on the covered connection can mask a quiet request. A person standing outside can see whether the room is lit and whether bodies cross the glass, but cannot infer consent, key custody, or what is displayed.

The physical fire history remains the 2022-11-04 electrical fire in the old audiovisual store, damage to the west annex, smoke-sealing of the original club room, and education-office-funded reconstruction; city hall did not own the school building. The current room's inspection, key log, adult-present charging, and closure decisions belong to 150 and 030. The official Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, item 6, supports a 0.9-metre clear door and level threshold; item 7 supports the corridor floor and width principles: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. Haneul's booth dimensions and acoustic envelope are Work decisions, not an engineering certificate.

## Gymnasium, auditorium, and high-capacity spaces

**Status:** Work decision for physical envelopes and bottlenecks; booking, supervision, and approval remain with 150.

The gymnasium is a 30-by-18-metre south-east hall with a 3-metre clear perimeter strip around the marked floor. Its main public door opens north toward the south corridor junction; a second exit opens east toward the athletic field. The equipment closet is south-west and reduces the usable wall there; balls, folding goals, or mats cannot be treated as invisible when they occupy the perimeter. The floor amplifies footfall and voices, so a person at the north door can see the central floor but may not hear a quiet speaker at the far end when the space is occupied.

The auditorium is an 18-by-12-metre room in the north-east wing. A 1.8-metre central aisle and two 1.2-metre side aisles divide its fixed seating field; the stage is 0.6 metres above the floor and is reached by a ramp at the west edge and stairs at the centre. The public foyer is 6 metres deep and has two 0.9-metre door leaves. The rear exit leads to the north-east exterior court, not to the service lane. A person in the foyer cannot see the stage when the doors are closed, and a person on stage cannot see the rear exit without turning.

Both spaces have a defined accessible position without claiming that every event arrangement is accessible. In the gym, a level field position beside the north aisle leaves 1.5 metres for a turn and 1.2 metres to the nearest exit route. In the auditorium, two removable seats at the rear-left bay create a 1.5-metre turning space and a clear line to the foyer; their removal is a physical reconfiguration that must be recorded by the facilities process. The stage ramp is 1.2 metres clear and has a level landing at its foot and top.

For high-capacity or assembly conditions, the Building Act Enforcement Decree's Articles 34, 35, 38, and 39 address direct stairs, refuge stairs, room exits, and outward exits; the official current locator opened at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000990378, with the displayed text effective 2026-07-28. Those provisions are legal building requirements whose exact application depends on classification and floor area. Haneul's two gym exits, auditorium foyer, aisles, and ramp are Work decisions designed so a later scenario can block movement honestly without claiming a code inspection.

## Stairs, lifts, ramps, and accessible route geometry

**Status:** Source-verified design floor plus Work-decision campus measurements.

The main block has two enclosed stairs: East Stair at the east end and West Stair at the west end. Each stair is 1.5 metres clear between handrails, has uniform 0.28-metre treads and 0.17-metre risers in the fictional design, and opens to the outside or a protected landing at ground level. The stairs are not wheelchair routes. The elevator is beside the lobby, linking all three occupied floors; its interior clear floor is 1.6 metres wide by 1.8 metres deep, with a 0.9-metre door and a 1.5-by-1.5-metre landing. It is larger than the detailed minimums and still has a finite capacity, a wait time, and a door that cannot be held indefinitely.

The north-lobby accessible route is level from the pedestrian forecourt to the elevator. The west-annex route rises 0.6 metres over a 12-metre ramp, a 1:20 slope, with 1.5-by-1.5-metre landings at the bottom, top, and turn. The ramp has 1.2 metres of clear width, handrails, a 5-centimetre edge protection, a non-slip surface, and a covered section. The route's 1:20 choice is a Haneul design decision; it is not a claim that every Korean ramp has that slope. The route cannot be shortened by crossing the service yard.

The official Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, item 9, specifies an accessible-lift landing activity space of at least 1.4 by 1.4 metres, a floor gap no greater than 3 centimetres, and lift dimensions and controls; item 12 specifies a ramp clear width of 1.2 metres, landings at each 0.75-metre rise, 1.5-by-1.5-metre activity spaces at starts, ends, turns, and landings, and a 1:12 maximum slope with a limited existing-building exception. Direct official PDF: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913, Appendix 1 items 9 and 12, amended 2023-12-11. Haneul adopts the larger elevator and gentler ramp as Work decisions.

The route has physical failure modes. A wet mat can reduce the clear width, a queue can fill the elevator landing, a door can swing into a turning space, and a delivery cart can make a service-side route unavailable. The existence of an elevator does not make the stairs disappear or guarantee immediate travel. During an emergency, the legally and operationally designated evacuation plan controls; this H2 supplies geometry only and does not invent a refuge procedure or transfer authority from 150.

## Toilets, rest points, shade, and water stations

**Status:** Work decision applying source-verified accessibility dimensions to Haneul's physical support spaces.

An accessible toilet is located on the ground floor beside the central spine, 14 metres east of the lobby, and another is in the west annex beside the covered connection. Each has a 0.9-metre clear outward-opening or sliding door, a 1.6-by-2.0-metre new-build internal envelope, a 0.75-metre side approach to the pan, and a 1.4-by-1.4-metre forward turning zone. The existing-building alternative in the official standard is not used for these fictional rebuilt or designated rooms. The corridor before each door remains 1.8 metres clear; a person waiting outside cannot block the approach without changing the route for everyone.

The main ground-floor rest point is a 3-by-2.4-metre alcove on the west side of the lobby, with one fixed bench, one armrest seat, and a 1.5-metre turning zone. It is not a private counseling room and does not promise quiet: the lobby doors and elevator chime remain audible. The west-annex rest point is smaller, 2.4 by 2.4 metres, beside the covered connection and outside the audiovisual booth's door swing. Neither rest point is a storage area. A bag or folded chair left in the turning zone is a physical obstruction and must be moved before the route is considered clear.

Water stations are at the lobby and west-annex junction. The lower bottle-filling control is within reach from a seated position; the surrounding 1.2-metre route remains clear. Shade is provided by the north forecourt canopy and the west covered connection. The canopy protects from ordinary sun and rain but does not make the municipal flood passage safe during a warning. The 2019 gauge, closure threshold, pump response, and youth-centre basement restriction remain exactly as 030 defines them.

## Lighting, signage, and wayfinding

**Status:** Work decision informed by official tactile, visual, audible, and accessible-route requirements.

The campus uses a three-part wayfinding system: large high-contrast zone letters, room numbers readable from the corridor, and tactile or Braille identifiers at public-room doors. The main lobby has a raised campus diagram oriented to the north gate, with the elevator, East Stair, West Stair, gym, auditorium, and west annex marked as physical points rather than game territories. The diagram does not show private residences, student names, or a live task ledger. A person can understand the route without knowing the game's vocabulary.

The main entrance has a tactile or texture change 0.3 metres before the door, a sign at approximately 1.5 metres beside public-room entrances, and a visible emergency direction marker that is not the same as a club arrow. Corridor signs alternate between ceiling-mounted zone signs and wall-mounted room signs; a low sign cannot project into the 1.2-metre minimum route. The elevator gives visual and audible floor indication. A sign can be physically present but functionally unusable when glare, rain, smoke, crowding, or language complexity defeats its reader.

Lighting is zoned rather than uniform. The north forecourt and main lobby are bright enough to read a notice without standing in the door path; the west covered connection has low-glare fixtures at each bend; the service lane has separate security lighting; the auditorium foyer can dim for use while its exit signs remain visible. Haneul treats 150 lux at elevator controls and the immediate accessible approach as a design floor informed by the official appendix's elevator lighting requirement, but this is not a measurement of every campus fixture. An outage changes the physical condition and must not be solved by pretending the sign is still readable.

Appendix 1, items 6, 9, and 13 of the official Enforcement Rule address tactile door cues, Braille signs, elevator visual and audible signals, and accessible toilet signs and surfaces: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. The legal standard applies according to facility category and current law; Haneul's English-readable labels, Korean room codes, high contrast, and campus diagram are Work decisions. ## Bus loop, market passage, and school-side approach

**Status:** Work decision for spatial relationships that preserves 030's municipal flood and authority boundaries.

The ordinary school approach begins at the bus-loop ramp, continues along the lower market passage, reaches the north public sidewalk, and then enters the campus forecourt through the main gate. In dry, open conditions, the school-side segment from the north sidewalk to the gate is 90 metres and takes approximately two minutes at an ordinary walking pace; the route from the bus stop to the gate is longer and depends on the municipal layout, not the school's fence. The bus loop and market passage are outside school property. A school map may show them as approach context but cannot issue a road closure or alter a municipal barrier.

The 2019-07-27 severe monsoon flood legacy remains exactly as 030 defines it: Seongnae flood channel, lower market passage, bus-loop ramp, yellow gauge, three raised electrical cabinets, pumps, 1.2-metre closure, narrow accessible detour, and 35-minute walking alternative. This file adds only the ordinary geometry of the school-side approach. During a warning, the alternative route may be longer, wetter, or more tiring; a student adviser may cancel a task, while the municipal flood-response office owns the gauge and closure. No route line here converts city infrastructure into school authority.

The north sidewalk widens to 2 metres at the school frontage, narrows to 1.5 metres beside the older residential wall, and crosses the service-lane mouth at a marked, level crossing. The crossing is a sight-line problem: a parked delivery vehicle can hide a pedestrian from a turning driver without changing the legal ownership of the road. The school gate attendant can observe the forecourt but cannot see around the market corner. The accessible detour is not a single magical line; its continuity must be checked from the municipal side to the school threshold.

The current Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, item 2, requires accessible approach routes to be separated from vehicle movement where applicable, kept level and slip-resistant, and protected from dangerous gaps; item 1 of the same appendix addresses approach-route and threshold principles: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. ## Campus measurements, map scale, and physical uncertainty

**Status:** Work decision establishing the measurement protocol and limits of physical canon.

Haneul's canonical site plan uses a local coordinate grid with origin at the inside face of the north pedestrian gate. East is positive x, south is positive y, and all horizontal measurements are in metres. The main lobby threshold is approximately (72, 18), the elevator centre approximately (78, 35), the west-annex booth centre approximately (18, 45), the gym centre approximately (128, 92), and the auditorium centre approximately (142, 18). These coordinates are map anchors, not GPS claims. They let a scenario place a person, obstruction, route, or line of sight consistently without pretending fictional coordinates are a real cadastral survey.

The canonical map has three precision levels. Structural dimensions—parcel edges, building footprints, room sizes, stairs, elevator, ramp, and clear widths—are fixed to the values in this file unless the earliest owning H2 is revised. Operational measurements such as walking time, queue length, sound carry, and daylight are bounded ranges: the dry school-side approach is 80–110 metres depending on the gate opening, an elevator wait is 0–4 minutes in ordinary conditions, and a closed classroom door reduces but does not eliminate corridor sound. Impressionistic details such as “the west wall feels close” are not measurements and cannot contradict the structural layer.

Every route check records origin, destination, surface, width at its narrowest point, rise, door state, obstruction, weather condition, observer position, and whether the measurement was planned or observed. A planned 1.8-metre corridor can become 1.1 metres when a cart is left across it; an accessible ramp can become unusable when water, ice, or a barrier changes its surface. The record must preserve both the design value and the temporary condition. A later revision changes the earliest owner and propagates to every dependent map, not merely the sentence that noticed the problem.

The official Education Facilities Safety and Maintenance Standard defines school boundary facilities and ongoing safety/maintenance concepts at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2100000208251. The Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1 supplies the dimensional reference points for routes, doors, lifts, ramps, and toilets at https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. Those sources support the measurement discipline and technical floors; the Haneul grid, coordinates, tolerances, sound ranges, walking ranges, and uncertainty protocol are Work decisions. No coordinate, map mark, or measurement transfers property, consent, or institutional authority.
