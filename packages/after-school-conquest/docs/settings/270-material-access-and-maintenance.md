# Material Access and Maintenance

Access is sustained by surfaces, doors, furniture, signs, storage, cleaning, inspection, and repair. This file fixes the maintenance layer beneath the routes in `050` and the emergency states in `260`.

## Surface and threshold condition

**Status:** Work decision — physical canon.

Floors, thresholds, mats, drains, ramps, kerbs, and tactile surfaces are checked for level, slip, water, glare, debris, and edge visibility. The legal and technical accessibility references are cited in `050` and `170`; Haneul’s measured envelope remains a fictional design decision with current inspection still required.

A route marked accessible in a plan can fail under rain, mud, ice, crowding, or a shifted mat. The owner records location, condition, time, action, and recheck. Students do not tape over a structural defect and call it repaired.

## Doors, locks, and closers

**Status:** Work decision — access boundary.

Doors have clear approach, swing, handle reach, threshold, closer force, sign, lock state, and emergency behaviour. A door may be physically wide enough yet unusable if a bin blocks the approach or a closer defeats a person’s reach. The 050 envelope remains authoritative for fictional dimensions.

Keys and access codes belong to named custodians. A propped door changes security, fire, privacy, and supervision conditions. A broken closer or lock is reported to the owner; a student does not wedge it open indefinitely.

## Furniture and reach

**Status:** Work decision — material canon.

Tables, chairs, counters, shelves, coat hooks, charging points, and bins are arranged so a person can approach, turn, reach, sit, and leave. A seat reserved for access need is labelled through the host’s policy, not occupied as a permanent personal claim unless that policy says so.

Furniture can move only within the host’s permission and after checking egress, lift landing, toilets, fire equipment, and sight lines. A temporary arrangement has owner, duration, and restoration plan. Heavy tables are not moved by a single student for speed.

## Signage and tactile information

**Status:** Work decision — wayfinding canon.

Signs state destination, direction, restriction, date, issuer, and alternative when needed. Text, contrast, mounting height, tactile or audible cues, and viewing distance are considered together. A beautiful sign that cannot be read or reached has not solved wayfinding.

Temporary notices carry version and removal date. A student-made arrow cannot override a safety barrier or official closure. When signs conflict, the competent current issuer is contacted and the conflict remains visible until corrected.

## Lighting, glare, and shadow

**Status:** Work decision — environmental access.

Lighting is checked for entry, stairs, ramps, signs, faces, screens, reflective floors, and night transit. Glare from wet paving, glass, displays, or low sun can erase a route even when nominal light is high. A person may request lower brightness, a different position, or a printed alternative.

Outdoor lights belong to the school, city, market, or facility owner according to location. The founders report a dark or dazzling point with time and direction. They do not open a fixture or infer a crime from one failed lamp.

## Acoustic environment

**Status:** Work decision — sensory canon.

Announcements, bells, traffic, HVAC, doors, crowds, and construction create different acoustic loads. A room that is quiet at 08:00 may be unusable after lunch. The host offers captions, written instructions, a quieter position, or another channel where possible.

Noise complaints identify source, time, affected space, and requested remedy. A person needing quiet is not required to prove a diagnosis. A microphone can amplify a barrier if it distorts, feeds back, or hides the speaker’s face.

## Cleaning and hygiene

**Status:** Work decision — maintenance canon.

Cleaning schedules identify surfaces, products, responsible worker, ventilation, wet-floor warning, waste, and reopening condition. Food, bodily fluid, smoke residue, mould, and flood water require the host’s trained process. Students may wipe an ordinary shared surface only when authorized and protected.

Cleaning cannot erase a maintenance defect, incident record, or person’s property. Strong scent or residue can make a room inaccessible. The host records a missed clean and assigns a new owner rather than relying on a student’s goodwill.

## Toilets, water, and rest points

**Status:** Work decision — access resource.

The toilet, water, shade, and rest-point envelope is described in `050`; this file governs inspection, supplies, signage, privacy, and reopening. A toilet can be physically present yet unusable if locked, wet, occupied by storage, or missing a functioning call mechanism.

Rest points are not punishment seats. A person may pause without explaining. The host checks routes to the point and from it to an exit. Water stations are maintained and labelled; shared cups, allergies, and sanitation are handled by the host.

## Lift, ramp, and stair maintenance

**Status:** Work decision — vertical access canon.

The 050 lift, ramp, stair, landing, and corridor geometry is a fictional physical baseline aligned with the official technical references in `170`. Daily usability still depends on power, door operation, obstruction, wetness, lighting, and notice. An out-of-service lift requires a safe alternate or a changed activity.

The facilities owner inspects and tags a defect. Students do not bypass a lift interlock, carry someone up stairs without consent and competence, or place equipment on a landing. A ramp’s slope does not authorize use during a flood closure.

## Storage and accessible clearance

**Status:** Work decision — housekeeping canon.

Storage has owner, item class, shelf height, fire separation, expiry, access, and retrieval rule. Corridors, door approaches, lift landings, toilets, ramps, and evacuation routes remain clear. A box that is “only there for ten minutes” still changes access during those minutes.

The audiovisual paper archive remains in its fire-rated cabinet separate from batteries. A student may report a blocked route and move only lightweight personal items when safe and authorized. Institutional storage is changed by its owner.

## Repair ticket and priority

**Status:** Work decision — accountability canon.

A repair ticket records location, symptom, date, user impact, immediate control, owner, priority, materials, contractor or staff, and recheck. Priority considers immediate danger, access loss, fire or flood risk, security, and ordinary wear; it is not simply whoever complains loudest.

The host communicates an interim route or closure. A ticket number is not a repair. If a defect remains, the founders may revise the activity rather than repeatedly testing it.

## Inspection and reopening

**Status:** Work decision — facilities boundary.

Inspection checks the relevant structure, electrical state, surface, access, signage, equipment, and records under current conditions. The inspector and owner are named. Public use resumes only when the competent host confirms the scope and communicates it.

An inspection may clear one room while leaving a route, archive, or adjacent wing restricted. A student observation supplements but does not replace certification. Reopening has date, time, conditions, and next review.

At term change, graduation, renovation, or owner change, the host audits keys, furniture, signs, equipment, cleaning, tickets, inspections, and user-facing maps. The handoff lists open defects and the next responsible person. A former club memory is not a maintenance record unless adopted by the owner. Material access remains available only through upkeep. A route, room, or device that once worked is historical evidence, not a present guarantee. The settings therefore keep maintenance visible as a continuing obligation rather than a one-time construction fact.
