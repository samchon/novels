# Peer Network and Consent

This file defines the social interfaces around the founders. It fixes what a peer relationship can authorize, what a club request must disclose, and how refusal, translation, privacy, and repair remain materially real. It does not assign meetings or plot turns.

**Status:** Work decision — fictional school canon.

The founders' grade contains ordinary classmates with different schedules, family obligations, abilities, languages, friendships, and willingness to participate. The class is not a single audience and cannot be represented by the loudest group chat. A request to “the class” identifies the channel, date, response method, and whether silence means no response, not consent.

Names and personal details are used only for a stated school purpose. A seating chart, attendance list, phone number, health note, or family circumstance is not a general club resource. A peer may participate under a nickname, anonymously in a survey, or not at all where the task permits. The absence of a reply cannot be converted into endorsement.

## Invitation, consent, and withdrawal


**Status:** Work decision — executable social rule.

An invitation states the activity, location, expected duration, physical demands, audience, recording or publication plan, adult presence, cost, and a contact for questions. Consent must be specific enough for the invitee to understand those conditions. It may be written, spoken, or expressed through an approved school process, but the inviter records the method rather than pretending that attendance answers every question.

Withdrawal is allowed before or during participation. A person can ask for a pause, a different route, removal of a quote, or deletion of an unpublished image when the applicable owner can do so. Withdrawal may create logistics—unused materials, a smaller team, a rescheduled task—but cannot be punished as betrayal. Safety or statutory retention duties may limit deletion; the holder explains that limit.

## Peer roles without forced representation


**Status:** Work decision — role boundary.

A peer may serve as a tester, witness, translator, photographer, route companion, note-taker, equipment borrower, or audience member only for the agreed scope. A disabled student is not automatically the accessibility representative; a multilingual student is not automatically the translator; a migrant student is not automatically the cultural explainer. The founders ask for expertise and compensate burdens through time, credit, accommodation, or a smaller request.

When a peer speaks for a group, the record states whether the person is speaking personally, as an elected representative, or under a formal appointment. A friend’s confidence does not authorize a public statement. If a peer changes position, the record preserves the change rather than treating it as inconsistency to hide.

## Group chat and message discipline


**Status:** Work decision — communication canon.

Group chats can coordinate ordinary logistics but do not replace a school office's notice, a facilities inspection, a city closure, or an emergency dispatch. Every consequential message identifies issuer, timestamp, intended audience, action, deadline, and correction path. A forwarded screenshot without provenance is a lead, not an authoritative record.

The chat owner sets membership, moderation, retention, and removal rules. Private messages remain private unless the sender authorizes sharing or an immediate safety obligation requires referral. Jokes, stickers, and reaction counts are not votes. A correction is linked to the original message and explains what changed, so a late reader cannot mistake an obsolete route or room status for current permission.

## Conflict, apology, and repair


**Status:** Work decision — relationship maintenance rule.

Conflict is recorded at the level of action and effect: what was said or done, who was affected, what access or trust changed, and who can repair it. The setting rejects a compulsory confession scene or a single apology as universal repair. Repair may require correcting a notice, returning property, paying a cost, removing an image, reopening a decision, or giving the affected person authority over the next step.

A mediator may facilitate but cannot erase a safety report, force private disclosure, or decide that harm is over because the group is uncomfortable. The person affected may accept, reject, delay, or define the repair. A later relationship can be civil, distant, conditional, or ended while the institutional record remains accurate.

## Privacy and identifiable media


**Status:** Work decision — data boundary.

The founders may make a map, photograph, audio note, or video only after identifying the purpose, subjects, storage location, access list, retention period, and deletion or correction route. Faces, voices, names, uniforms, room numbers, family documents, medical details, and precise routines can identify a person even when a caption omits the name.

Public visibility is not blanket permission to record. A school-approved event may have its own notice and consent mechanism; the founders still follow its scope. Blurring, cropping, pseudonyms, delayed release, and private review are design options, not substitutes for asking when asking is possible. Any minor's image is handled under the responsible adult and school process, never by popularity poll.

## Translation between peers


**Status:** Work decision — language interface.

Peer translation distinguishes literal wording, intended action, social register, and unresolved ambiguity. A translated request preserves a refusal, deadline, fee, safety warning, and appeal route. Lila or another bilingual peer may explain in conversation, but an official school or city decision remains issued by its named office and should be requested in an accessible form when misunderstanding could affect rights or safety.

No peer is pressured to translate family conflict, immigration paperwork, health information, or disciplinary material. If confidentiality, competence, or power imbalance makes peer translation unsafe, the group refers to an authorized service or adult. The English novel may render Korean speech in English while retaining the institutional distinction between source and translation.

## Access needs and ordinary accommodation


**Status:** Work decision — inclusion canon.

An access request may concern mobility, hearing, vision, sensory load, language, fatigue, medication timing, anxiety, reading, or the need for a support person. The request need not disclose a diagnosis. The responsible host asks what barrier must be removed, what alternative works, how long it is needed, and who must know; it does not demand a biography as the price of entry.

Accommodation is tested under actual conditions: rain can change a ramp, a crowd can erase a sight line, and a noisy room can defeat a written instruction. The request owner records what was tried and what remained difficult. “We have a ramp” or “the notice is online” is not a complete access assessment.

## Money, gifts, and reciprocal pressure


**Status:** Work decision — peer economy boundary.

Participation cannot depend on an undisclosed fee, required purchase, or gift to a founder. Borrowed money, food, transit fare, printing, and donated materials are logged according to the owner and returned or converted into a transparent shared cost. A free item can still create social pressure or a privacy expectation.

The group does not trade access to rooms, records, translation, or friendship for money. A peer who cannot pay receives the same information about no-cost participation or may decline without public explanation. School or municipal grants, if available, belong to their named institution and conditions, not to a private friendship ledger.

## Adult contact and safeguarding referral


**Status:** Work decision — bounded escalation.

Each peer-facing activity names an adult contact who can stop it, a backup contact, and the emergency route appropriate to the location. The founders are not a confidential crisis service. If a peer reports immediate danger, abuse, serious illness, or inability to remain safe, the listener stays with the person when safe, limits promises, and contacts the responsible adult or emergency service.

The record avoids interrogating or testing a disclosure. It preserves exact words only when necessary and stores them with restricted access. A founder may tell the peer what referral will happen and what cannot remain private. No group vote can cancel a safeguarding duty.

## Participation ledger and minimum necessary record


**Status:** Work decision — operational canon.

The peer ledger contains only the minimum needed to run the activity: consent scope, access condition in functional terms, role, contact route, equipment, cost, and withdrawal or correction status. It does not collect speculative labels, diagnoses, family gossip, or popularity rankings. Each entry has an owner, creation date, review date, and deletion or archival rule.

When the activity ends, the holder closes the ledger by returning property, settling costs, deleting temporary media, and recording unresolved obligations. A participant can ask what was recorded about them and how to correct it through the responsible host. Historical records retained for school reasons remain separated from informal club notes.

## Non-participation as a valid social state


**Status:** Work decision — consent canon.

Non-participation may mean no, not now, not in this location, not with recording, not without an adult, or no explanation offered. The group does not rank these answers by moral worth. A person can remain a friend, classmate, translator, critic, or beneficiary without joining the club.

The founders may redesign the task so that fewer people carry fewer risks, but cannot redesign a refusal into a hidden yes. Publicly thanking participants never names nonparticipants. A route, resource, or notice intended for general access must remain useful to people who never consented to the founders' project.
