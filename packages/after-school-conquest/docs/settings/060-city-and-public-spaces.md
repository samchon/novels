
# City and Public Spaces

This file owns the physical city-side environments that meet Haneul's campus: streets, market edges, bus facilities, the Seongnae channel corridor, the youth centre, civic buildings, and public wayfinding. It does not replace 030's flood chronology, 050's school parcel geometry, 150's institutional authority, or 020's permission procedure. A city space is public in the spatial sense only; a person still needs the responsible host's permission to enter a restricted room, use equipment, or perform a task.

Haneul is fictional. External sources below establish current Korean legal categories, accessibility floors, sign conventions, and safety duties. Exact distances, widths, building footprints, and civic names are Work decisions. Every public-space description retains the distinction between municipal property, school property, youth-centre property, and privately operated market frontage.

## Metropolitan districts and physical orientation


**Status:** Work decision for fictional Haneul's urban morphology.

Haneul's built-up area is organized around a north civic street, the east–west Seongnae channel, the central market district, and a school-and-library civic campus north of the market. Apartment towers occupy the north-west ridge; older low-rise shops occupy the channel's south bank; the bus interchange sits east of the market; and the youth centre stands west of the channel on a raised municipal lot. These are map relationships, not claims about a real Korean city.

The school is reached from the bus loop by a route that passes a public market edge, a channel crossing, a north sidewalk, and the school forecourt. The route crosses administrative boundaries before reaching the gate. A person can be within sight of the school while still being on city property, and a city notice can be visible from school grounds without becoming a school notice. The map therefore uses a boundary line and an owner label on every segment.

The city grid is not uniformly flat. The market sits in the lower channel terrace; the school parcel rises slightly northward; the apartment ridge rises farther north; and the east interchange ramp descends toward the channel. The ordinary accessible route chooses a longer, gentler grade. A short staircase may be visually direct but is not a substitute for a continuous route. Haneul's 35-minute flood detour remains the exact 030 condition when the lower route closes.

The Act on Promotion of Transportation Convenience for Mobility Disadvantaged Persons defines transportation-disadvantaged people and mobility facilities including accessible sidewalks, lifts, and tactile blocks; the current official text displayed as effective 2026-02-01 is https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1028650975, Article 2. The law defines a framework, not Haneul's districts. The district map, ridge, terrace, market, and civic campus are Work decisions.

## North civic street and school frontage


**Status:** Work decision informed by current pedestrian-safety and accessibility standards.

The north civic street is 7 metres curb-to-curb with one lane each way and a 2-metre sidewalk at the school frontage. The school fence sits 0.6 metres behind the sidewalk's outer edge, leaving a narrow planting strip but no second pedestrian lane. At the market-side corner the sidewalk narrows to 1.5 metres for 18 metres because of an older retaining wall. That narrowing is a physical defect to be recorded, not an invitation to move into the road.

The school frontage has a marked crossing 22 metres east of the main gate. It is level with a refuge-free 4-metre crossing width. A separate service-lane mouth crosses the sidewalk 38 metres west of the gate; its turning vehicle can obscure a pedestrian, so the route has a stop line and tactile contrast. The city owns the street and markings. The school can report a blocked route but cannot repaint a crossing through club procedure.

The Road Traffic Act Enforcement Rule's official Appendix 6, amended 2024-11-14, defines sign and marking applications for crosswalks and pedestrian-priority roads: https://www.law.go.kr/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=146448457. The current text includes crosswalk conditions, pedestrian-priority markings, and child-protection indications; it does not require Haneul's fictional exact placement. The Mobility Convenience Act's current Article 2 framework is at https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1028650975. Haneul's street width, crossing position, and sidewalk bottleneck are Work decisions.

Daylight and traffic change what can be seen. A person approaching from the market sees the school gate after passing the service-lane mouth; a driver turning into that mouth may see the gate only after the crossing. Rain on the painted surface, a delivery van, or a crowd under the school canopy changes sight distance. Later blocking must specify sidewalk side, crossing state, and municipal obstruction rather than saying simply “outside school.”

## Market edge and lower passage


**Status:** Work decision preserving 030's flood legacy and municipal ownership.

The market edge follows the south bank of the Seongnae channel for 260 metres between the west pedestrian bridge and the east bus-loop ramp. Stalls occupy private or leased frontage under a continuous 2.2-metre covered arcade; the lower market passage is a separate 2.4-metre pedestrian corridor below the arcade. The covered arcade remains above the lower passage at the west end but descends toward the channel near the east ramp. A person can walk under cover while still approaching a flood-prone lower level.

The lower passage has three visual interruptions: a market loading recess, a bridge support, and a utility cabinet line. The clear width is 2 metres in ordinary condition and can narrow when a stall's permitted goods or a rain barrier extends into the arcade. The market operator controls stall frontage; the municipal flood-response office controls the passage closure under 030. A stallholder cannot reopen a city barrier, and a student cannot treat a public arcade as a club workspace without host agreement.

The 2019-07-27 flood remains the owner of the gauge, pumps, three raised electrical cabinets, 1.2-metre closure, narrow detour, and 35-minute alternative. This file owns only current spatial adjacency and the difference between arcade and lower passage. A warning removes the lower passage from the accessible route even when the arcade appears dry. The city record and a family's soaked paper notice can disagree in time without changing the physical boundary.

The official Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, item 2, requires accessible approaches to be level, slip-resistant, and protected from dangerous gaps; direct PDF, amended 2023-12-11: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. The rule is a technical standard whose application depends on facility type. Haneul's arcade width, loading recess, supports, and flood-prone lower level are Work decisions.

## Bus interchange and ramp


**Status:** Work decision for municipal transport geometry; no school or club authority is assigned.

The east bus interchange has three bays arranged around a 36-by-22-metre loop. The school-facing stop is Bay 2, with a 2-metre platform and a 1.8-metre sheltered waiting strip. The bus-loop ramp descends 1.2 metres from the upper east approach to the lower market passage over 24 metres, but its surface is not the same as the accessible school ramp in 050. The lower ramp closes at the 030 threshold; the upper platform may remain open while the lower connection is barred.

The platform has a 1.5-metre clear boarding line beside the curb, a 1.2-metre tactile edge strip, and a 2-metre shelter depth. A queue can fill the shelter without blocking the boarding line only if people stand along the marked rear zone. Wheelchair boarding requires the bus operator's ramp or lift and a level enough interface; the existence of a shelter does not guarantee a boarding position. A person waiting at Bay 2 can see the school gate across the street but cannot safely cross outside the marked crossing.

The Mobility Convenience Act's current framework identifies buses, passenger facilities, roads, lifts, and tactile blocks as mobility concerns; official direct text effective 2026-02-01: https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1028650975. The Enforcement Rule Appendix 1 for mobility facilities is an official technical PDF amended 2019-02-08 at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?flNm=%5B%EB%B3%84표+1%5D+이동편의시설의구조ㆍ재질+등에+관한+세부기준%28제2조제1항+관련%29%0A&flSeq=40424119; its displayed road standard includes a 2-metre sidewalk floor with an existing-road exception. Haneul's bus geometry is fictional and does not assert compliance certification.

Transport delays, a full queue, or a closed lower ramp change a route's actual duration. City transport staff own bay operations; the school adviser may cancel a student task but cannot hold a bus, move a barrier, or declare the ramp safe. A route plan that starts at the upper platform must name the street crossing and school-side threshold separately.

## Seongnae channel corridor


**Status:** Work decision for present physical channel geometry; 030 owns the 2019 disaster record and warning threshold.

The Seongnae channel is a concrete-lined open corridor averaging 12 metres from retaining edge to retaining edge through the market district. A 1.8-metre inspection ledge runs along the north side, separated from the public lower passage by a 1.1-metre guardrail. The ledge has access hatches and a maintenance stair at the west bridge; it is not a pedestrian shortcut. The south retaining wall carries the market arcade above, while the east ramp approaches the lower passage.

The yellow gauge is fixed to the north retaining wall beside the west pedestrian bridge at reader height. It can be read from the public side without entering the channel, but glare, rain, and crowding can obscure it. Pumps connect through screened inlets near the east ramp. Three raised electrical cabinets stand outside the channel's lower flood line. These are 030's fixed physical facts; this H2 adds their spatial relationship only.

The channel's ordinary barrier has no gate that volunteers may operate. The municipal flood-response office owns the gauge and closure, and volunteers are barred from the channel during warning. A school map may draw the corridor as a hazard edge but may not treat it as a traversable territory. The youth-centre basement is a separate facility condition, not a lower extension of the channel.

The Disaster and Safety Management Basic Act is current official law displayed effective 2026-02-01 at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?ancYnChk=0&chrClsCd=010202&efYd=20260611&lsiSeq=283851&urlMode=lsInfoP; it frames disaster prediction, warning, and safety responsibilities, while the exact city trigger remains the fictional 030 rule. The channel dimensions, ledge, guardrail, gauge placement, pump inlets, and cabinet positions are Work decisions.

## Youth-centre parcel and basement access


**Status:** Work decision for a separate municipal youth-centre physical site, preserving 030's authority boundary.

The youth centre stands west of the channel on a 42-by-30-metre raised parcel. Its public entrance faces north onto a 2-metre sidewalk and a 1.5-metre covered apron. The main hall, staff desk, and multipurpose room are at grade; the basement storage is reached by an internal stair and a goods lift, not by the public entrance. A south service door opens toward the channel-side maintenance lane but is not an ordinary youth or school entrance.

The basement has a 2.4-metre-high ceiling, a 1.5-metre central aisle, and storage cages on both sides. A floor drain sits at the lowest south-east corner, while paper and electrical material are stored on raised shelves along the north wall. The storage restriction after a flood warning is physical and administrative: the room can be dry at the public entrance while the channel-side access remains unsafe. A volunteer cannot infer safety from the smell, lighting, or absence of visible water.

The youth-centre coordinator owns the facility response plan; the municipal flood-response office owns the public channel and road closure; a school adviser may cancel a student task but cannot issue or revoke a city closure. These are 030 boundaries, not a new authority grant. The public hall's clear path is not permission to enter the basement, and a school booking cannot convert the centre's service door into a school door.

The Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, items 7, 9, and 13, supports level routes, lift activity spaces, and accessible toilet connection: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. The youth-centre parcel, basement dimensions, raised shelving, drain, doors, and separation of public and service paths are fictional Work decisions.

## Library and civic-service frontage


**Status:** Work decision for adjacent public buildings and their physical interfaces.

The Haneul Public Library occupies a 54-by-28-metre building north-west of the school, separated by a 6-metre public street and a 12-metre planted setback. Its main entrance faces south and has a 2.4-metre covered forecourt. A civic-service building sits 40 metres west of the library, with a public reception entrance and a separate records delivery door. Neither building is part of Haneul Public High School, and neither can be used as an unofficial school office.

The library forecourt has a 1.8-metre clear route between planting beds, a 1.5-metre turning area at the door, and a tactile change before the threshold. The reading room is visually open from the lobby but acoustically separated by a 6-metre vestibule. A person can see the service desk from the entrance yet cannot hear a quiet desk conversation across the vestibule. The civic-service building's public queue occupies a 3-by-5-metre bay; the records door is west-facing and never opens into that queue.

The public library and civic-service frontage offer indoor alternatives during rain, heat, or darkness only when their current opening and access conditions permit. A school student cannot assume that an open public door authorizes a club task, photograph, interview, or record request. 020's host agreement and 150's records boundary remain upstream. This file gives later blocking enough physical information to distinguish foyer, lobby, reading room, public counter, and service door.

The official School Facilities Project Promotion Act is not a source for library ownership; the civic buildings are Work decisions. Accessibility principles and technical dimensions are drawn from the official Enforcement Rule Appendix 1, items 6, 7, and 9, amended 2023-12-11: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. The library and civic-service footprints, setbacks, vestibule, and queue bays are fictional.

## Public toilets, rest areas, and shelter


**Status:** Work decision for city-side support spaces, distinct from 050's school toilets and rest points.

The market district's public toilet is in a 12-by-7-metre municipal kiosk beside the upper arcade, 55 metres west of Bay 2. Its accessible stall is reached through a 1.8-metre corridor and has a 0.9-metre door, a 1.6-by-2.0-metre internal envelope, and a 1.4-by-1.4-metre turning zone. The bus interchange has a separate accessible toilet beside the staff room; the youth centre has its own ground-floor facility. A person's physical need can therefore be met in several places, but the nearest door changes with closure, opening hours, and route conditions.

Rest areas are distributed rather than concentrated: a 3-metre bench under the market arcade, two seats inside the bus shelter, a 2.4-metre library vestibule bench, and a covered youth-centre apron. These spaces have different noise and privacy profiles. The market bench hears deliveries and stall calls; the bus shelter hears engines; the library bench is quieter but visible; the youth-centre apron is exposed to the channel-side route. None is a private counseling room.

The city's shelter roofs stop ordinary rain but not wind-driven water. During a flood warning, the lower market bench may be physically dry yet downstream of the closure line. The municipal response plan, not the presence of a roof, decides whether it remains an available location. A student can request a different public space, but 020's host consent is still required for work or publicity.

The official Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1 item 13 supports accessible toilet connection, level non-slip floors, 0.9-metre doors, and turning/side-approach dimensions: https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913. These standards apply by facility category and current legal scope. Haneul's public toilet locations, benches, shelter dimensions, and sound profiles are Work decisions.

## Crossings, kerbs, and pedestrian sight lines


**Status:** Work decision informed by current road-marking and mobility-accessibility standards.

The school route crosses three distinct vehicle interfaces: the north civic street, the east bus-loop ramp entrance, and the youth-centre service lane. Each has a level pedestrian continuation, a marked crossing or priority treatment, and a sight-line risk. The north street crossing is 4 metres wide; the service-lane crossing is 2.4 metres; the bus-loop crossing uses a 2-metre refuge strip between the upper platform and the civic sidewalk. The lower ramp is excluded when 030's 1.2-metre closure trigger applies.

Kerbs are cut at each planned accessible crossing, but a cut is not a continuous route if a drain grate, parked vehicle, advertising board, or puddle occupies the landing. The fictional standard keeps a 1.5-by-1.5-metre landing on each side and a 2-metre nominal sidewalk where the public geometry allows. The older market wall's 1.5-metre narrowing requires slower passage and single-file movement; it is not silently widened by a map symbol.

Sight lines are measured from a 1.1-metre seated eye-height reference and a 1.6-metre standing reference. A delivery van at the service lane can hide a seated wheelchair user; a market sign can hide a child's approach; a bus at Bay 2 can obscure the crossing from a driver. These are spatial facts, not claims of blame. A later scenario must place the observer, vehicle, weather, and crossing state before assigning what was visible.

Road Traffic Enforcement Rule Appendix 6, amended 2024-11-14, gives official crosswalk and pedestrian-priority marking conditions at https://www.law.go.kr/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=146448457. The Mobility Convenience Act's current Article 2 and the official mobility-facility appendix frame accessible sidewalks and road facilities at https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1028650975 and https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?flNm=%5B별표+1%5D+이동편의시설의구조ㆍ재질+등에+관한+세부기준%28제2조제1항+관련%29%0A&flSeq=40424119. ## Civic signs and public route information


**Status:** Work decision for physical wayfinding and information access; no public authority is transferred by a sign.

The city-side route uses three sign scales. Street-level signs identify the market, bus bays, library, youth centre, and school direction at a distance; entrance signs identify the building owner and open threshold; low tactile or high-contrast signs identify a specific door or toilet. A municipal flood gauge and a school notice board are not interchangeable: the former carries the city trigger fixed in 030, while the latter carries school communication under 150.

The bus interchange has a large route board behind the shelter, a tactile bay marker at platform height, and a high-contrast emergency panel. The market arcade uses hanging signs so stall boards do not reduce the 2-metre nominal route. The library uses quiet wall signs rather than overhead announcements in the reading zone. A sign can be blocked by a queue, glare, rain film, or an unfamiliar language even when its text is technically visible.

The club may draw a simplified public map only with the host's agreement and without private addresses, protected records, or a false claim of ownership. A game territory code does not replace a municipal street name, a bus bay number, or the school gate. If a public sign is wrong, the correction belongs to the issuing authority; 020's G-C01 can record the club's map correction without editing city infrastructure.

Official Road Traffic Enforcement Rule Appendix 6 provides current sign and marking categories, including crosswalk, pedestrian-priority, and protection-zone signs, at https://www.law.go.kr/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=146448457. The official Accessibility Enforcement Rule Appendix 1 provides tactile and Braille sign placement principles at https://law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=135226913, items 6 and 9. Haneul's sign text, heights, colors, and map conventions are Work decisions.

## Municipal service lane and loading geometry


**Status:** Work decision for the city-side service boundary, preserving school and market ownership distinctions.

The east municipal service lane runs north–south between the bus interchange and the school’s east fence. It is 5 metres wide at the straight section and widens to a 7-by-7-metre turning court beside the civic-service building. The school service gate opens west from this lane, while the market delivery recess opens east through a separate curb cut. A person walking from the bus loop cannot use the service lane as a sheltered shortcut without sharing a space designed for vehicles and deliveries.

The lane has a 1.5-metre pedestrian strip on its west side, interrupted by the school service gate threshold and two drain grates. The grates sit flush with the pavement and have openings no wider than the current accessibility design floor used in 050; a temporary leaf mat can still make them slippery. The turning court has a sight-line break at the civic building's south-west corner. A box truck can occupy the court for unloading without legally occupying the school forecourt, but it can block the west pedestrian strip and change the route condition.

Loading is time-bounded by the city or property operator, not by the student club. The market operator controls its delivery recess, the civic-service building controls its records door, and Haneul facilities controls its service gate. A driver may wait in the court without receiving a school key; a school custodian may open the service gate without authorizing access to the market or civic records. This physical separation keeps 150's authority and 020's host agreement intact.

## Public-space measurement and ownership ledger


**Status:** Work decision establishing city-side measurement and boundary control.

Every city route is recorded as a sequence of segments with owner, surface, clear width, slope or step, crossing, shelter, sight-line obstruction, lighting, and closure condition. The route from Bay 2 to the school gate has seven segments: platform, upper ramp, civic crossing, market-side sidewalk, lower-passage approach, north sidewalk, and school forecourt. The lower-passage segment is conditionally unavailable under 030; the school forecourt is controlled by the school; the other segments are city or market-side spaces.

Measurements use the same metre-based discipline as 050 but a separate city coordinate prefix `C-`. `C-01` begins at the upper Bay 2 shelter, `C-02` at the north market corner, `C-03` at the west pedestrian bridge, and `C-04` at the school-side sidewalk. These are map labels, not game territory codes and not legal parcel identifiers. A measured 1.5-metre sidewalk can become 0.9 metres after a permitted stall extension or temporary barrier; the ledger retains the design and observed values separately.

The municipal flood-response office owns gauge and road-closure records; the youth-centre coordinator owns its facility response plan; market operators own their frontage within their agreements; the school and education office own school-side records. This is an ownership ledger for physical information, not an appeal route. 150 and 020 supply institutional escalation. No city map can authorize a volunteer to enter a basement, channel, service lane, or school room.
