# Campus and Civic Technology

Technology in Haneul is layered over physical rooms, authority, and maintenance. This file fixes common systems, permissions, failure states, and user-facing access without inventing a magical network or assigning plot functions.

## School network boundary

**Status:** Work decision — fictional infrastructure canon.

The school network serves instructional devices, office systems, approved student accounts, printers, and safety communications under separate permissions. A club account may access its own files without accessing grades, counselling records, facilities controls, or another club’s archive.

Network access is conditional on current student or staff status, host policy, device security, and maintenance. A familiar login is not perpetual permission. On graduation, student roles expire or transfer through the school’s documented process.

## Civic public network boundary

**Status:** Work decision — municipal interface.

City offices, library terminals, youth-centre systems, public Wi-Fi, traffic signs, flood gauge reporting, and school systems are separate networks and authorities. A public webpage may publish a notice without offering an interface for changing it. A city employee’s account cannot be used to update a school booking.

The founders identify which system owns a status and which merely displays it. A cached closure notice is labelled by capture time. Public Wi-Fi is treated as observable transport, not a trusted place for sensitive records.

## Display boards and public screens

**Status:** Work decision — communication canon.

Electronic boards, printed boards, classroom displays, bus signs, and civic screens have issuers, update owners, display periods, and removal or correction routes. A screen showing “open” may be stale if its feed failed; a handwritten barrier notice may be current if issued by the competent office.

The club does not place a notice on a public screen without host permission. Text is legible, high contrast, dated, and paired with a nonvisual route where needed. A student-created graphic cannot mimic a city or school seal so closely that its origin is unclear.

## Sensors and measured status

**Status:** Work decision — evidence boundary.

Sensors, gauges, timers, cameras, and counters report a bounded observation under calibration, location, and maintenance conditions. The yellow flood-level gauge has the municipal ownership and threshold in `030`; a phone photograph of water is a separate observation.

The founders record source, timestamp, units, location, calibration or uncertainty where known, and who may act on the reading. A sensor output does not decide a closure unless its competent authority adopts it. A missing reading remains a gap.

## Power, charging, and electrical safety

**Status:** Work decision — inherited safety canon.

Charging uses approved equipment, suitable outlets, supervision, and a clear surface. The rebuilt audiovisual booth’s two circuit breakers, adult-present charging rule, and separate fire-rated paper archive remain fixed in `030` and `050`. Students do not reset unknown breakers or daisy-chain damaged cords.

The owner isolates heat, smell, sparks, exposed wiring, or repeated trips and calls facilities. A fully charged battery is not evidence that a room is safe. Charging schedules include overnight, absence, rain, and evacuation contingencies.

## Account recovery and continuity

**Status:** Work decision — operational canon.

Every essential system has a current admin, backup, recovery method, data owner, and offline fallback. Recovery data is protected separately from ordinary club notes. A student’s personal phone is not the only recovery route for a school or civic asset.

The handoff record includes active accounts, integrations, publication access, storage, deletion, and review date. An alumni contributor may retain personal copies but not a school administrator role without current authorization.

## Assistive technology and compatibility

**Status:** Work decision — access canon.

Screen readers, captions, magnification, hearing loops, tactile labels, alternative keyboards, translation tools, and mobility devices are part of the access environment. Compatibility is tested with the actual file, room, browser, lighting, noise, and user preference. A tool listed on a procurement sheet is not proof it works.

The host owns maintenance and training; the user chooses whether and how to use an aid. Data in an assistive device is not copied into a club ledger without purpose. A fallback may be paper, human explanation, a different format, or a rescheduled task.

## Privacy, logs, and retention

**Status:** Work decision — data boundary.

System logs can include account, timestamp, location, device, edit, access, or error information. The owner states purpose, retention, audience, correction route, and incident response. A log is not a diary and should not be mined for a student’s personality.

The founders request aggregate or redacted logs when individual identity is unnecessary. A screenshot preserves only what is visible and may expose unrelated private data. Logs used for safety or audit remain separate from public storytelling material.

## Failure, outage, and manual mode

**Status:** Work decision — resilience canon.

Systems fail through power loss, network outage, weather, maintenance, account lock, device breakage, or human error. Each essential process has a manual mode: printed route, phone contact, paper attendance, physical key register, or staff confirmation. Manual mode does not mean bypassing safety.

The outage record states start, affected function, fallback, owner, user notice, and restoration test. A cached map or old spreadsheet is labelled stale. No student is blamed for not seeing a notification during an outage.

## Public technology and dignity

**Status:** Work decision — social boundary.

Cameras, speaker announcements, access gates, public charging points, and online forms affect people who did not choose the club’s project. The founders minimize collection, offer alternative interaction, and identify the responsible owner. Convenience is not a sufficient reason to make a person’s face, voice, or disability visible.

A public device may be used only for its posted purpose. A lost phone is reported through its owner, not unlocked by curiosity. A technology demonstration cannot use a real student record as sample data.

## Maintenance and replacement

**Status:** Work decision — asset lifecycle.

Technology has installation, inspection, update, repair, replacement, accessibility, and disposal costs. The owner tracks warranty, spare parts, secure deletion, battery condition, and environmental disposal. A new device is not automatically better if users cannot access it or the host cannot maintain it.

Replacement preserves necessary data only through an authorized migration. A broken display, lift call panel, printer, or sensor is reported with exact location and observable symptom. Students do not repair an institutional system with a tutorial and a screwdriver.

## Handoff and audit

**Status:** Work decision — continuity canon.

At each term, owner change, closure, or graduation, the host audits accounts, devices, media, permissions, notices, backups, and offline fallbacks. The audit records missing owners and stale access. A system is not handed off merely because a password was sent.

The current owner publishes the user-facing contact and effective date. Historical files retain provenance without keeping unnecessary personal data. Alumni access is a new grant with an expiry and revocation route.
