**Status:** Work decision — user-confirmed scope.

This package delivers one complete English novel covering the fixed fictional interval March 2027 through February 2031 in twenty numbered volumes. The delivery is not a first volume plus optional sequels: every volume is required, remains in causal order, and receives its own storyline, scenario, and finished manuscript. The school interval is three Korean high-school years (Volumes 1–17); Volumes 18–20 cover the founders’ first year after graduation. The work contains no supernatural system, lethal combat plot, or sexual content involving minors.

Each volume must end with a changed obligation, resource, relationship, or authority that the next volume inherits. A volume may contain local closure, but it cannot erase its predecessor’s ledger, fatigue, public commitments, or institutional memory. The final volume closes the founders’ school-era arc while leaving ordinary civic work unfinished; “complete” means the reader receives a designed ending, not a claim that Haneul has become perfect.

**Status:** Work decision — owner-adopted interpretation of the title.

The novel promises comic pleasure from a grand strategy vocabulary colliding with school bells, wet umbrellas, room bookings, missing receipts, and people who did not consent to be conquered. It also promises the emotional seriousness of watching a harmless game acquire real constituencies and costs. The governing question is whether young people can turn the desire to possess a place into the harder practice of maintaining relationships without possessing them.

Comedy may expose vanity, bureaucracy, and the founders’ overconfidence; it may not make consent, disability, poverty, migration, or grief itself the joke. Dramatic consequences must arise from earlier choices and material limits. A late villain, miraculous grant, sudden legal loophole, or romantic confession cannot substitute for accumulated work.

**Status:** Work decision — owner-adopted access rule.

Finished prose uses close third person with one dominant focal character per scene. The principal rotation is Minseo Han, Jae-won Park, Lila Reyes, and Do-yun Choi; a volume may grant a scene to a successor or adult only when that viewpoint reveals a materially different responsibility. The narrator can describe observed action, remembered experience, documents, and dialogue, but cannot state an absent person’s unspoken thought.

Perspective changes occur at an H4 scene boundary or a clearly marked document boundary. A focal character may misunderstand a ledger, translate a phrase incorrectly, or misread silence; the prose must later earn correction through evidence or conversation. No omniscient sentence may secretly solve a mystery that the current viewpoint has not encountered.

Physical danger is handled through prevention, evacuation, first aid, and adult referral rather than tactical spectacle.

Marginalized characters retain ordinary wants, comic agency, expertise, and the right to refuse the club. Disability is not a metaphor for moral purity; migration is not a shortcut to exposition; poverty is not a quaint aesthetic. Any scene that makes a person’s vulnerability public must show who authorized the disclosure and what repair is owed.

If a real-world rule is uncertain, the owner either researches it directly or chooses an explicitly fictional rule before downstream staging.
