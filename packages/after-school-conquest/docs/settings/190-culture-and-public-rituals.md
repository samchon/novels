
# Culture and Public Rituals

Haneul’s shared life contains formal ceremonies, ordinary etiquette, food, music, sport, mourning, celebration, and public memory. This file fixes usable cultural context without treating one custom as universal or assigning it to a plot event.

## School ceremonial register


**Status:** Work decision — fictional school canon.

Assemblies, orientation, examinations, awards, safety drills, and graduation administration use formal registers, timed movement, assigned seating, notices, and adult roles. A ceremony can be emotionally important while still being a logistical operation with microphones, ramps, queues, interpretation, and an exit plan.

Students distinguish an official announcement from a peer tradition. Wearing a uniform, standing, bowing, or receiving a certificate may be required in one setting and optional in another. A ritual does not erase disability, religious practice, grief, or a person’s right to ask what participation means.

## Everyday greetings and respect


**Status:** Work decision — social-language canon.

Greetings vary by age, role, familiarity, urgency, and language. A bow, nod, wave, handshake, title, first name, or brief text can be respectful in context. The novel shows the relation and setting rather than treating one gesture as a universal code.

Politeness does not mean agreement. A student can speak formally while refusing a request; an adult can use a warm tone while issuing a binding instruction. The founders ask when a register matters instead of making etiquette a test of ethnic authenticity.

## Food sharing and hospitality


**Status:** Work decision — material culture canon.

Food carries care, celebration, economy, allergy risk, religion, labour, and memory. Shared food is labelled with ingredients and preparation conditions when safety requires. A host states whether eating is expected, optional, paid, or unavailable.

The founders accept that a person may decline food without explaining. A dish is not a symbol for an entire culture, and a recipe is not public property because it appears at a school event. Food waste, refrigeration, serving tools, and cleanup are part of the setting’s physical reality.

## Music, sound, and performance


**Status:** Work decision — cultural resource.

School and civic spaces host recorded music, rehearsal, karaoke, street sound, prayer, speeches, and ordinary noise. Volume, copyright, hearing access, acoustic reflection, and neighbours’ rest all affect permission. A performance plan names equipment owner, duration, audience, and stop condition.

The founders offer captions, printed lyrics, visual cues, or a quiet alternative where needed. Music can carry memory without revealing a character’s private history. A song title or lyric is not reproduced at length as exposition; its social use and listener response do the work.

## Sport and embodied play


**Status:** Work decision — inclusion canon.

Sport can be competitive, social, therapeutic, or simply a way to occupy a field. Rules, equipment, warm-up, surfaces, weather, changing access, and refusal are stated before participation. A person may be skilled at strategy, pacing, or support without being the fastest player.

The gym and field capacities remain those in `050`. The founders do not use a disabled participant as a demonstration or make injury a proof of commitment. A score records the game’s result, not a person’s value.

## Mourning and remembrance


**Status:** Work decision — dignity canon.

Mourning may be public, private, religious, secular, delayed, collective, or expressed through ordinary maintenance. A school or civic office may issue a notice while a family controls personal details. No person is required to perform grief for an audience.

A memorial, photograph, name, or donation has an owner and permission boundary. The founders do not turn a loss into a convenient moral lesson or disclose cause of death without authorization. Support can mean silence, practical help, translation, transport, food, or referral.

## Celebration and gift etiquette


**Status:** Work decision — social economy.

Birthdays, exam completion, team milestones, holidays, and ordinary good news may be marked with food, cards, messages, small gifts, or no ceremony. A gift is not a contract and does not purchase access, forgiveness, or public praise.

The giver states whether return is expected; the receiver may decline or accept without a performance. Group celebrations account for cost, allergy, sensory load, transport, and people who do not want photographs. A date in `030` supplies chronology, not a required celebration.

## Clothing, uniform, and weather


**Status:** Work decision — embodied context.

Uniform, work clothes, sportswear, religious clothing, rain gear, and personal style can coexist. Weather and household budget affect what is practical. A wet coat, overheated classroom, muddy shoe, or borrowed umbrella is a material condition, not a character shorthand.

The school’s uniform or safety rule is issued by the school authority, while a person’s comfort and religious or access need must be handled through the applicable accommodation route. The founders do not police one another’s bodies or infer identity from clothing.

## Public art, symbols, and borrowing


**Status:** Work decision — civic culture boundary.

Murals, banners, school emblems, market signs, memorial plaques, and community art have owners, histories, and maintenance conditions. A photo or map can show a public symbol without implying endorsement. Borrowing an image, phrase, or design requires permission or an applicable licence.

The founders ask who made the work, who maintains it, and whether the image is safe to publish. A symbol may mean different things to different users. The setting resists treating a contested sign as a single secret code.

## Holidays and ordinary calendar knowledge


**Status:** Work decision — chronology interface.

Public holidays, school recess, family observances, and business closures can change availability, but the package does not invent a public-holiday date where `030` has not specified one. A plan records the actual office or family schedule it relies on.

The legal semester and instructional windows remain separate. A holiday-like atmosphere cannot move an exam, graduation boundary, or city closure. When the calendar is uncertain, the founder checks the issuing school, employer, or office rather than using cultural expectation as fact.

## Public memory and local history


**Status:** Work decision — civic memory canon.

Residents may remember floods, fires, redevelopment, migration, closures, or a market’s former route differently. An official plaque, family account, newspaper item, and lived memory have distinct sources and purposes. The 2019 flood and 2022 school fire remain the fixed records in `030`.

The founders preserve disagreements about dates, responsibility, and meaning without assigning a culprit or discovery in settings. Commemoration can include infrastructure maintenance and changed access, not only speeches. A past injury does not make every later institution guilty, and a repaired building does not erase memory.

## Cultural consultation and compensation


**Status:** Work decision — ethical practice.

When the founders need cultural or linguistic expertise, they ask a named person or group for a defined contribution, time, audience, and compensation or credit. They do not mine a friend’s family life for free authenticity. A consultant can correct a detail without approving the whole work.

The record states what was learned, what remains uncertain, and what the consultant asked not to publish. A single conversation cannot stand for a nation, religion, neighbourhood, or generation. Consultation changes a design only within the owner’s authority.
