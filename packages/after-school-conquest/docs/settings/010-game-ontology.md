**Status:** Work decision - fictional civic-maintenance game.

Conquest is the founders' name for a voluntary process in which students help a host maintain one small public-facing condition and receive a dated seal when the host confirms the work. A “conquest” is therefore a completed relationship among a host, a task, a responsible student, evidence, and a review date. The word is theatrical: it describes the founders' exaggerated map language, not a transfer of land, a person, a service, or government authority.

Minseo may draft a proposal; the host or delegated adult must agree before work begins; the assigned student records the task; and the host may cancel it without penalty. The game cannot recruit a student through grades, access to food or transport, romantic pressure, or public embarrassment. Its success condition is maintained usefulness, not a rising score. A proposal that lacks a willing host, safe task, or review date is a map mark only, never a conquest.

The fictional rule is designed around youth voice and voluntary participation. The Convention on the Rights of the Child Article 12 protects a child's opportunity to express views in matters affecting them, with due weight given to age and maturity; it does not authorize the founders to govern other people. Source: [UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 12](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-child#art12).

A territory is a bounded service zone, route, object, or recurring condition that a host can identify without exposing a person's home or private life. Examples are the library's charging shelf, the market's covered notice board, or the accessible route between the bus loop and the school gate. A territory has a name, physical boundary, owner or responsible office, access hours, hazard note, and review date in Ledger Form G-L01.

Only a person with authority over the place may define the boundary. A student may suggest a boundary but cannot draw a neighbour's apartment, a classmate's body, a family circumstance, or a public office into the game without consent. The territory is a description of work, not a deed. Two overlapping territories must name separate tasks or merge into one host-approved record; overlapping seals never create priority.

The map uses a paper grid and an encrypted digital copy with coarse public coordinates. The public version omits private addresses and exact movement patterns. If a route becomes unsafe, the host marks it suspended in red ink and the digital record shows the suspension timestamp; no player may “defend” it by entering a closed space.

The physical seal stays in the ledger sleeve rather than on a person or building. A digital scan is a convenience copy and cannot replace the host signature. The club may publish a count of valid seals, but not a name or photograph merely because a seal exists. The stamp is an invitation to inspect maintenance, not a medal that makes the holder superior.

**Status:** Work decision - fictional noncash labour record.

A token is a paper square printed in denominations of one, two, and four completed volunteer-hours. It is a non-transferable acknowledgment of time already worked; it is not money, wages, academic credit, a raffle ticket, a material-donation receipt, an expense authorization, or a claim on another person. The treasurer issues it only against a dated shift sheet, and the worker signs whether the record is accurate. Material donations, printing costs, and emergency transport belong to receipts and the separate budget ledger, not to tokens.

Tokens cannot be spent, traded, pooled, redeemed, or used to obtain grades, favours, food, transport, or access. They may be corrected or cancelled only by the treasurer and adviser together, with the original serial and reason retained. The public display cap is forty hours per student so the map cannot create a prestige ranking; hours above forty remain in the private provenance record and appear only in an anonymous aggregate. The cap does not delete, transfer, or confiscate labour.

Counterfeit or duplicated tokens are quarantined, not publicly mocked. The treasurer and adviser compare the shift sheet to the ledger within three school days. A proven error receives a correction or cancellation mark; the completed labour remains historical evidence and no fictional cash is created.

The stop is entered on the task card with time, person category, immediate safety action, and whether follow-up is invited. A student who leaves a voluntary shift keeps membership, existing credit for work actually completed, and access to the club's ordinary information. No founder may ask a teacher, parent, council, or host to punish refusal.

The only exception is an immediate danger in which an adult or emergency service directs people away; that direction is safety action, not game consent. A host may later invite a revised proposal, but the club cannot reopen a declined task until the host initiates contact. Public summaries state only that a task was declined or stopped, never who refused or why.
