# Chronology Baseline

This file fixes the calendar that every later setting, storyline, scenario, and manuscript must use. It distinguishes the legal school-year container from Haneul's fictional instructional calendar, and it records the pre-story conditions that make dates materially consequential. It does not allocate scenes, reveals, chapters, or volume beats; those belong to later layers and to `040-volume-time-boundaries.md`.

## Opening date

**Status:** Source-verified legal baseline plus Work decision for Haneul's fictional delivery.

The delivery opens on Tuesday, 2027-03-02. The date is the first instructional day experienced by the four founders—Minseo Han, Jae-won Park, Lila Reyes, and Do-yun Choi—at Haneul Public High School. The legal academic year has already begun on Monday, 2027-03-01, but that Monday is a school-year boundary rather than a student-facing first day in this work. The one-day distinction prevents a calendar label from falsely implying that a school must teach on the first day of its academic year.

The work's opening interval runs from Tuesday, 2027-03-02 through Friday, 2031-02-28. Every dated document, roster, receipt, weather note, and remembered appointment falls inside or explicitly outside that interval. The ending date is Friday, 2031-02-28; it is the last day of the founders' post-graduation year in this chronology, not a claim that all later civic work ends. Dates are written in ISO order when a record needs precision, with the weekday written out when a missed or moved day matters.

The first instructional day has ordinary operational conditions: morning transit must reach the school before the first bell, daylight is present but still winter-short, and family work or care cannot be treated as invisible slack. A late bus, a wet crossing, an early-shift parent, or an inaccessible route changes what a person can actually do that day even when the date remains fixed. The school can publish a timetable, but it cannot manufacture a student's travel time, energy, money, or permission to stay after hours. Those constraints are chronological facts for downstream planning, not scene instructions.

The opening date is a Work decision because the future Haneul and its founders are fictional. The legal container is not fictional: Article 24 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act states that a school's academic year begins on March 1 and ends at the end of February. That article also leaves school-term, instructional-day, closure-day, and curriculum-operation details to the Enforcement Decree and related administration. The source was opened directly as the National Law Information Center document “Elementary and Secondary Education Act, Article 24 (Classes, etc.),” effective 2026-08-20; its direct locator is https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1032276043. The law is a current-law baseline at 2026-08-21, not a prediction that Korean law will remain unchanged through 2031.

## School-year rule

**Status:** Source-verified legal rule with a Work-decision calendar application.

Each Haneul academic year begins on March 1 and ends on the last day of the following February. The three relevant containers are 2027–28 (2027-03-01 through 2028-02-29), 2028–29 (2028-03-01 through 2029-02-28), and 2029–30 (2029-03-01 through 2030-02-28). The final post-graduation interval is not a fourth high-school year: it runs 2030-03-01 through 2031-02-28 while the founders are alumni or other young adults under the access rules owned by the school-institutions settings.

The container is continuous even when instruction is not. A legal school year includes weekends, public holidays, examination gaps, summer recess, winter recess, graduation administration, weather closures, and other lawful non-instructional days. A record dated during recess still belongs to the academic year whose interval contains it. A record dated at midnight across February and March crosses into a new academic year even if the same people, room, project, or ledger continue without interruption.

Haneul's fictional year labels use the opening March year: “2027–28” means 2027-03-01 through 2028-02-29, never the calendar year 2027 alone. A school form that says “Year 1, spring” must therefore be resolved against both the academic-year label and its ISO date. City-hall records may use a calendar year, a budget year, or a disaster anniversary; the Haneul Metropolitan Office of Education and the school use the academic-year container for student and instructional records. The two authorities may describe the same day differently without creating two dates.

The March 1 rule does not itself require a first class on March 1. In this work the first instructional dates are 2027-03-02, 2028-03-02, and 2029-03-05. The choice leaves room for the lawful scheduling of instructional days, closure days, orientation, staff preparation, and the public-holiday/weekend pattern while preserving a continuous legal year. A later document cannot treat the delayed first instruction as a delayed academic-year start, and it cannot treat March 1 as an invented student event merely because it is the statutory boundary.

The direct source is the National Law Information Center document “Elementary and Secondary Education Act, Article 24 (Classes, etc.),” effective 2026-08-20, at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1032276043. Its legal date is current-law evidence; the specific Haneul instructional windows below are Work decisions.

## Semester and term boundaries

**Status:** Source-verified statutory semester rule plus Work decision for all Haneul dates.

Every academic year has two legal semesters with no gap. The first semester begins on March 1 and ends on the date selected by the school principal after considering instructional days, closure days, and curriculum operation. The second semester begins the next day and ends on the following February's last day. This is the governing membership rule even when the school is on vacation, closed for a holiday, or preparing records. A recess is a condition inside a semester, not an unassigned third semester.

Haneul adopts these boundaries:

| Academic year | Semester 1 | Semester 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2027–28 | 2027-03-01–2027-08-20 | 2027-08-21–2028-02-29 |
| 2028–29 | 2028-03-01–2028-08-20 | 2028-08-21–2029-02-28 |
| 2029–30 | 2029-03-01–2029-08-19 | 2029-08-20–2030-02-28 |

These boundaries classify every date once and only once. For example, 2027-08-20 is the final day of semester 1 even if no student is in a classroom, while 2027-08-21 is the first day of semester 2 even if it is a weekend or an administrative closure. A weather closure does not move the boundary; it changes the day's operation and may create a later make-up decision under the school's authority.

The 2029–30 first semester ends one day earlier because the 2029–30 second semester begins on 2029-08-20. This is deliberate fictional scheduling, not an arithmetic accident. The 2029 first instructional day is Monday, 2029-03-05; Friday, 2029-03-02 is fictional office preparation, while Saturday and Sunday follow as weekend dates. The first semester therefore contains administrative preparation and non-instructional days before classroom instruction begins, yet remains legally assigned from March 1.

The source is the National Law Information Center document “Elementary and Secondary Education Enforcement Decree, Article 44 (Semesters),” effective 2026-05-26, direct locator https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1002280340. Article 44 states the two-semester structure and the first-semester/second-semester adjacency. The three Haneul date ranges, the choice of boundaries, and the distinction between membership and instruction are Work decisions bounded by that rule.

## Examination periods

**Status:** Work decision for Haneul's published calendar; weekday audit completed.

Haneul publishes four Monday-to-Friday examination windows in each of the three high-school years. The windows are protected assessment periods: routine club activity, recruitment, unsupervised room use, and voluntary labour cannot be scheduled as though the students had ordinary after-school capacity. A school notice can assign a room, paper, digital system, accommodation contact, and collection procedure; it cannot erase transport, illness, disability access, family work, or the recovery time needed after an examination.

The dates are:

| Academic year | Examination windows (all Monday–Friday) |
| --- | --- |
| 2027–28 | 2027-04-26–30; 2027-06-28–07-02; 2027-10-25–29; 2027-12-13–17 |
| 2028–29 | 2028-04-24–28; 2028-06-26–30; 2028-10-23–27; 2028-12-11–15 |
| 2029–30 | 2029-04-23–27; 2029-06-25–29; 2029-10-22–26; 2029-12-10–14 |

The first two windows in each row occur in semester 1 and the last two in semester 2. The June–July 2027 window crosses a calendar-month boundary but not a semester boundary: 2027-07-02 remains in semester 1. The same principle applies to any future record that crosses midnight, a month, or a named holiday. “Exam week” is a five-day date range here, not permission to invent extra examination days on the adjacent weekend.

The dates are fictional Haneul scheduling decisions. The legal baseline comes from the school-year and semester rules already identified, not from a claim that national law mandates these exact examination weeks. The school examination authority remains separate from the student council, game club, and city government. If a later layer needs a moved examination, it must record the original window, the authorized reason, the replacement dates, and the affected semester membership; it may not silently replace one date with another.

## Holidays and vacations

**Status:** Work decision for recess and closure windows, with legal semester membership preserved.

Instructional and non-instructional windows are fixed as follows:

| Academic year | Instructional window 1 | Summer recess | Instructional window 2 | Winter recess / graduation administration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2027–28 | 2027-03-02–07-20 | 2027-07-21–08-20 | 2027-08-21–2028-02-08 | 2028-02-09–02-29 |
| 2028–29 | 2028-03-02–07-19 | 2028-07-20–08-20 | 2028-08-21–2029-02-08 | 2029-02-09–02-28 |
| 2029–30 | 2029-03-05–07-19 | 2029-07-20–08-19 | 2029-08-20–2030-02-07 | 2030-02-08–02-28 |

These windows describe the school's planned operation, not a promise that every building is open every hour. Weekends and public holidays remain inside whichever semester contains them. The 2029–30 winter interval includes graduation administration because graduation records, return of keys, archive transfer, and alumni-access boundaries need a date range rather than a fictional single instant. It remains semester 2 through 2030-02-28.

Weather closures, transport disruption, illness, maintenance, and family emergencies can reduce a planned instructional day or voluntary activity. They do not authorize a student leader to move an examination, open a locked room, or count an absent person as present. The school records the closure and its issuing authority; the education office and city government retain separate records for their own responsibilities. Daylight and weather affect safe travel and the amount of work possible after the final bell, while the calendar still records the same date.

The first instructional day in 2029–30 is Monday, 2029-03-05. Friday, 2029-03-02 is a fictional office-preparation day, and 2029-03-03 and 2029-03-04 are weekend dates. The calendar does not call the preparation day a class, nor does it call the weekend a vacation chosen by the school. These distinctions matter for attendance, supervision, building access, and family scheduling. Exact public-holiday names are not needed in this baseline; a later institutional document may identify them when a downstream rule genuinely depends on one.

## Founder age arithmetic

**Status:** Work decision calculated from fixed birth dates using completed international age.

The founders' birth dates are Minseo Han — 2011-11-18; Jae-won Park — 2011-06-02; Lila Reyes — 2012-01-27; and Do-yun Choi — 2011-09-09. Age is calculated by completed years followed by months and days, not by the former Korean age convention and not by rounding a birth year. On a birthday the year count increases; before that birthday it does not. A school record may display a grade and an age field differently, but the underlying date arithmetic remains this canon.

At the opening on 2027-03-02, Minseo is 15 years, 3 months, 12 days; Jae-won is 15 years, 9 months; Lila is 15 years, 1 month, 3 days; and Do-yun is 15 years, 5 months, 21 days. On the first instructional day of the second year, 2028-03-02, they are respectively 16 years, 3 months, 13 days; 16 years, 9 months; 16 years, 1 month, 4 days; and 16 years, 5 months, 22 days. On the first instructional day of the third year, 2029-03-05, they are 17 years, 3 months, 15 days; 17 years, 9 months, 3 days; 17 years, 1 month, 6 days; and 17 years, 5 months, 24 days.

At the graduation boundary, 2030-02-28, Minseo is 18 years, 3 months, 10 days; Jae-won is 18 years, 8 months, 26 days; Lila is 18 years, 1 month, 1 day; and Do-yun is 18 years, 5 months, 19 days. At the final delivery date, 2031-02-28, they are 19 years, 3 months, 10 days; 19 years, 8 months, 26 days; 19 years, 1 month, 1 day; and 19 years, 5 months, 19 days. Leap-day handling follows ordinary calendar arithmetic: 2028-02-29 exists, but none of the four birth dates is February 29.

Age does not itself grant room keys, school records, spending authority, or permission to represent Haneul. Those powers belong to the institutional settings and their procedures. Conversely, a person reaching a birthday does not erase an existing commitment, fatigue, injury, or family obligation. Downstream authors must use the exact date when age changes legal access, consent procedure, travel feasibility, or a person's understanding of their own time.

## Grade progression

**Status:** Work decision aligned with the three-year high-school structure; no volume or scene allocation is owned here.

All four founders enter Haneul Public High School as first-year students in the 2027–28 academic year, continue as second-year students in 2028–29, and are third-year students in 2029–30. “First-year” means the grade attached to the academic-year record, not the number of instructional days that have elapsed. The grade changes at the school's authorized progression boundary, not on an individual's birthday and not when a club ledger reaches a token or hour threshold.

The progression is:

| Academic container | Grade | Legal year | Instructional first date | Grade boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2027–28 | First year | 2027-03-01–2028-02-29 | Tue 2027-03-02 | 2028-02-29 |
| 2028–29 | Second year | 2028-03-01–2029-02-28 | Thu 2028-03-02 | 2029-02-28 |
| 2029–30 | Third year | 2029-03-01–2030-02-28 | Mon 2029-03-05 | 2030-02-28 |

The table is a record classification, not an instruction to stage a promotion ceremony or a reveal. A student's official record, attendance, course completion, and graduation eligibility are held by the school. The game club can observe workload and voluntary participation but cannot promote, retain, certify, or alter a grade. The city government cannot borrow school authority merely because a civic project occurs in a city building.

The three-year high-school term is supported by the Elementary and Secondary Education Act, Article 46, while the March-to-February year and official records/graduation operations are bounded by Articles 24–26. ## Graduation boundary

**Status:** Work decision bounded by current-law record and graduation provisions.

The third academic year ends on Thursday, 2030-02-28. That date is the final day on which the founders are treated as current Haneul students for the chronology. Graduation administration occupies 2030-02-08 through 2030-02-28 inside semester 2; it may include record checking, certificate preparation, key return, archive transfer, accessibility review, and notices about alumni access. The boundary is a date range for administrative work and a precise end date for student status, not a claim that a ceremony must occur on a particular hour.

Eligibility is determined from official academic records and the applicable school rules, not from game tokens, club reputation, volunteer hours, a council vote, or a promise made to a friend. The school records completion and issues any certificate under its authority. On 2030-03-01, the founders may still be invited to Haneul as alumni, volunteers, contractors, or visitors only through the then-applicable access and safeguarding rules; they no longer receive the automatic access of enrolled students. A former student's familiarity with a room, teacher, route, or archive is not a key, a pass, or consent from current students.

The boundary also separates institutional ledgers. The school closes its student record according to its authority; the Haneul Metropolitan Office of Education retains its education-side oversight records; city hall retains municipal records; and a club or successor organization retains only the records it is authorized to hold. A file created before graduation is not automatically public after graduation. A later request must identify the record owner, purpose, lawful access, redaction, and retention condition.

Article 46 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act provides the three-year high-school term, while Articles 24–26 cover the academic year, student records, and progression/graduation framework. These are official National Law Information Center anchors in the document effective 2026-08-20: https://www.law.go.kr/법령/초ㆍ중등교육법#제24조, https://www.law.go.kr/법령/초ㆍ중등교육법#제25조, https://www.law.go.kr/법령/초ㆍ중등교육법#제26조, and https://www.law.go.kr/법령/초ㆍ중등교육법#제46조. The 2030 boundary and Haneul procedures remain fictional applications.

## Pre-story Haneul city era

**Status:** Work decision establishing fictional pre-story continuity; not a historical claim about a real city.

Haneul is a fictional metropolitan city whose civic records and education records must not be merged. Before the founders' first instructional day, the city was already an inhabited system of apartment districts, older low-rise streets, a stream corridor, a market, bus interchanges, public libraries, schools, clinics, repair shops, and families whose work begins before school and ends after it. The school sits within the city but is not city hall. The Haneul Metropolitan Office of Education supervises the public-school side; city hall manages municipal roads, drainage, public facilities, and civic records within its lawful boundary.

The pre-story era establishes three practical layers. First, the built layer: stairs, curb cuts, covered paths, drainage grates, bus shelters, lighting, elevators, locks, and the distances between them. Second, the administrative layer: dated work orders, procurement records, inspection notes, school notices, weather alerts, and archive retention. Third, the household layer: shift work, care for siblings or elders, rent pressure, language access, disability accommodations, and the cost of staying late. These layers often disagree in time. A repaired drain may be recorded before a family trusts it; a new bus route may exist on a map before a driver or wheelchair user can depend on it; a school notice may be posted before every household receives it.

The city begins the story with accumulated memory rather than a blank map. People know which underpass floods, which stairwell loses light, which archive room smells of smoke, and which office answers a complaint. That knowledge is unevenly distributed and may be wrong, outdated, or inaccessible. A later author must not treat a map, a rumor, a city spreadsheet, and a student's lived route as interchangeable evidence. Each record needs an issuing authority, date, scope, and limitation.

No pre-story event is a hidden plot solution. The flood and fire below are standing constraints with surviving records, repairs, habits, and disagreements. The chronology permits downstream narratives to make choices under those conditions, but does not prescribe which character discovers which record or when a reader learns it.

## 2019 flood legacy

**Status:** Work decision for a fictional municipal flood and its continuing material consequences.

On 2019-07-27, a severe monsoon event overtopped Haneul's fictional Seongnae flood channel. Water entered the lower market passage, the bus-loop ramp, and the youth-centre basement storage. These are three related but separately managed spaces: the passage is a public pedestrian route, the ramp is a municipal transport approach, and the basement belongs to the youth centre's facility operation. The flood did not erase the city or make every route impassable. Its continuing significance is the durable safety system and the uneven human cost of the detours that followed.

The city replaced and maintains the pumps serving the affected passage and ramp, raised three electrical cabinets above the former water line, and installed a yellow flood-level gauge where responders can read it without entering the channel. At 1.2 metres, the affected covered passage and the bus-loop ramp close. The gauge is a trigger for closure, not a forecast of how much water is safe to walk through. Pumps can lower standing water after a warning; they do not authorize entry into the channel, the ramp, or the youth-centre basement while the warning remains active.

The residual route is narrower and slower. An accessible detour avoids the lower passage and ramp, but its verified walking alternative takes thirty-five minutes in ordinary conditions. Heavy rain can lengthen that time, reduce sight lines, soak paper notices, and make a nominally accessible surface difficult for a wheelchair, cane, stroller, or person carrying materials. A student arriving through the detour may therefore be late or exhausted without having ignored a posted schedule. A family member leaving a shift, a volunteer carrying a box, and a person using the route for the first time experience different costs even on the same date.

Records retain different kinds of truth. The municipal flood-response office owns gauge readings, the 1.2-metre closure decision, pump maintenance, cabinet work, and the road or passage notice. The youth-centre coordinator owns the facility response plan, basement access restriction, storage inspection, and volunteer briefing. Haneul Public High School owns its own attendance and student-safety communication. The school adviser may cancel a student task or tell students not to travel, but cannot issue or revoke the city's passage or ramp closure. A city notice does not prove that every household received it; a student's soaked paper notice does not establish the official gauge time. These records and lived experiences must remain distinct.

During a flood warning, volunteers are barred from the channel and basement. Storage remains restricted until the youth-centre coordinator records inspection and reopening. The school may substitute a remote or above-ground task, but no club leader may turn the yellow gauge into a challenge, move a barrier, or count a cancelled shift as completed. This event and all its repairs, detour costs, notices, and authority boundaries are fictional canon; it carries no claim about a real city.

## 2022 fire legacy

**Status:** Work decision for a fictional school fire and its continuing safety controls.

On 2022-11-04, an electrical fire began in Haneul Public High School's old audiovisual store. The fire damaged the west annex and smoke-sealed the original club room. The building is school property: city hall did not own it. The Haneul Metropolitan Office of Education funded reconstruction, while the school's principal and facilities office retained responsibility for daily access, inspection coordination, keys, and public-use conditions. The date is pre-story history, not a hidden culprit, discovery, or future reveal.

The rebuilt audiovisual booth is a constrained facility rather than a symbolic replacement. It has a wired-glass door, two circuit breakers, and a twelve-seat capacity. A key log records issue, return, and responsible adult; possession of a key does not prove that a person operated equipment or remained in the room. Charging is prohibited when no adult is present, and no unsupervised charging is allowed as a workaround for a crowded schedule. Public use begins only after the facilities inspection is recorded. A room booking therefore needs a named supervisor, a headcount within twelve seats, an accessible route, equipment condition, and a closing check.

The archive controls are separate from the electrical controls. Fire-rated paper archive cabinets hold records separately from batteries and charging equipment. A fire-service incident record establishes response time and physical conditions; a facilities inspection establishes whether the room may reopen; a key log establishes access history; and an archive chain-of-custody record establishes which papers were moved, smoke-affected, scanned, or retained. None of these records alone proves intent, motive, or the complete contents of a damaged file. A reconstructed copy must say that it was reconstructed rather than silently filling a missing page.

Education-office funding does not make the education office the daily room operator, and city hall cannot approve a school key or charging practice merely because municipal staff visit the campus. The principal and school facilities office decide ordinary access and safety closure. The statutory School Governance Committee may deliberate the applicable school-rule and budget framework within its lawful role, but it does not inspect wiring, issue keys, certify the booth, or substitute for facilities authority. A safety stop remains in force until the responsible school office records the condition for reopening.

The booth's twelve-seat limit includes the people actually inside, not merely the students listed on an invitation. Equipment may be staged only after the supervisor checks the route and breaker condition. If a key is missing, a breaker trips, smoke is detected, or a charging device is found without adult presence, the room closes and the facilities office records the stop before anyone resumes work. A club may move its meeting elsewhere, but it may not solve a capacity or electrical problem by concealing a person, bypassing a breaker, or leaving a battery charging after the supervisor departs.

The fire legacy is therefore a standing chronology constraint: every later date involving the booth carries the rebuilt room's capacity, supervision, charging, key, inspection, and archive boundaries. Students can remember smoke or a sealed door, but memory does not replace the inspection record; an inspection record does not describe every person's fear or lost work. This event, its reconstruction, and its records are fictional Work decisions.

## Post-graduation interval

**Status:** Work decision defining the final chronological container after student status ends.

The post-graduation interval begins on Friday, 2030-03-01 and ends on Friday, 2031-02-28. It is one continuous calendar year bounded by the same March-to-February convention, but it is not an additional high-school academic year for the founders. The founders are alumni or young adults with changing work, study, family, money, transport, and access conditions. Their exact occupations, living arrangements, and obligations belong to later settings owners; this H2 fixes only the time container and the institutional consequences of leaving school.

From 2030-03-01 onward, access to Haneul requires a defined basis: invitation, alumni program, approved volunteer role, contractor arrangement, public event, or another current rule. A former student may know a room's history and still need a key request. A current student may refuse a photograph, interview, or archive request. The school must distinguish an alumni contact list from a student roster, and city hall must distinguish a municipal volunteer record from an education record. Employment or university schedules can constrain availability without becoming school attendance.

The interval has its own ordinary chronology: pay periods, application deadlines, transport timetables, weather, daylight, public holidays, rent or family-care obligations, and the renewal or expiry of permissions. The same project can therefore acquire a different cost after graduation even if its map and vocabulary remain familiar. A bus journey made after a paid shift is not equivalent to the same journey after a school day; a civic-room booking is not equivalent to a school club booking; and an alumni promise is not a current student's obligation.

The final date, Friday 2031-02-28, closes the delivery window. It does not close the city, the school, the founders' relationships, or every civic problem. Later narrative layers may allocate the twenty-volume work across the three school years and this post-graduation year, but they must preserve the chronological boundary and show any cross-boundary commitment as an explicitly carried record. No post-graduation interval may be smuggled into the school years by an undated flash-forward, and no school authority may be assumed after graduation without a new access basis.
