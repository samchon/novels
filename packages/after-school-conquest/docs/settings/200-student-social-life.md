
# Student Social Life

This file fixes the ordinary social systems that surround schoolwork: clubs, study, meals, movement, competition, leisure, and reputation. It keeps social life from becoming a single popularity hierarchy or an invisible plot reward.

## Classroom neighbourhoods


**Status:** Work decision — school-life canon.

Students form temporary desk partnerships, friendship circles, study groups, sports teams, language pairs, and quiet routines. Seating and timetable create contact without creating intimacy. A classmate may know where someone sits and still not know their home life.

The founders describe a social group by its current activity, members, and access boundary. They do not call a whole class a faction because several students share a joke. A student can belong to more than one group or none.

## Club membership and attendance


**Status:** Work decision — inherited club canon.

Club recognition, room booking, attendance, and adviser authority follow `150` and the game procedure in `020`. A student can attend a public introduction, trial task, regular meeting, or approved sub-group under different conditions. Membership does not authorize keys, records, spending, or public representation.

Attendance records presence and relevant absence status without ranking enthusiasm. A late arrival may have a family or transport reason; a student who leaves early remains a participant for the portion completed. The club provides a safe exit and a current contact.

## Study and peer tutoring


**Status:** Work decision — learning canon.

Peer tutoring identifies subject, scope, time, language, access need, and whether the work is collaborative or assessed. A tutor explains and checks understanding; they do not complete graded work or access another student’s submission account. A student can be excellent at one topic and need help in another.

Study spaces account for noise, lighting, seating, devices, food, and transport home. A quiet student is not automatically focused; a talkative student is not automatically disruptive. The founders ask the learner what form of help works.

## Examinations and social pressure


**Status:** Work decision — chronology-linked canon.

The fixed examination windows in `030` alter sleep, room use, tutoring demand, family work, and emotional pressure. The exam authority remains the school. The club does not turn exam results into a public leaderboard or promise that a civic project will raise a grade.

Students may study together, share permitted resources, or ask for accommodation through the school route. Rumours about questions, grades, or accommodations are not circulated as strategy. A failed test changes a record and support need, not a person’s social worth.

## Lunch, break, and informal routes


**Status:** Work decision — physical-social canon.

Breaks are short resource windows for food, toilets, messages, quiet, peer contact, and movement. The route from classroom to dining area can be crowded, wet, noisy, or inaccessible; `050` physical geometry and `070` environmental states apply.

The founders do not claim a table, corridor corner, or market seat as permanent territory without the owner’s rule. A student may spend break alone. A group invitation includes where to meet and what to do if the designated place is occupied.

## Rumour and school reputation


**Status:** Work decision — social information rule.

Reputation is a circulating interpretation, not a reliable record. A student may be known for punctuality, jokes, translation, repair, absence, or a rumour that is false. The founders verify claims with the person or competent record owner and avoid forwarding private detail.

Correction does not require repeating the harmful rumour to a larger audience. A public error receives a proportionate public correction; a private misunderstanding is repaired privately when safe. The club cannot guarantee that reputation will reset after a correction.

## Online and offline presence


**Status:** Work decision — student communication canon.

A profile, group chat, school notice, face-to-face conversation, and printed poster have different audiences, speed, permanence, and correction paths. A message sent after midnight may reach a student during sleep or family work. Online presence does not prove physical availability.

The founders identify the authoritative channel for each action and offer a non-digital route where needed. They do not screenshot a private message for a public argument without authorization. A deleted message may still have been read; the setting treats digital harm as material.

## Dating, affection, and boundaries


**Status:** Work decision — minor-safety canon.

Students may experience friendship, affection, crushes, dating, jealousy, or no romantic interest. The setting does not make romance compulsory, eroticize minors, or use a confession to resolve institutional work. Consent, privacy, age-appropriate boundaries, and the right to end contact remain explicit.

A club request cannot demand a partner’s location, phone, schedule, or public declaration. Affection does not authorize recording, borrowing, or representation. A breakup may alter transport or group logistics but does not become a public spectacle.

## Leisure, games, and the title’s vocabulary


**Status:** Work decision — game-culture interface.

The founders use strategy vocabulary as a playful model for rooms, routes, resources, and obligations. A game term is not a claim that people or neighbourhoods are property. Leisure play has its own consent, rules, turn-taking, accessibility, and stop condition.

A student may dislike the game, prefer another hobby, or participate only as an observer. The club explains terms to readers and peers without making prior game knowledge a membership test. Real consequences require real institutional consent and cannot be smuggled in as a joke.

## Peer leadership and quiet leadership


**Status:** Work decision — social authority.

Leadership can be elected, delegated, technical, caring, logistical, or situational. A student who speaks rarely may hold essential knowledge or a veto over their own participation. A charismatic student does not automatically coordinate the group.

The club records role, term, authority, support, and handoff. Leadership is reviewed after absence, conflict, graduation, or changed task. No student is made the permanent note-taker, translator, access tester, treasurer, or emotional buffer by inertia.

## Absence, exclusion, and return


**Status:** Work decision — social continuity.

Absence may follow illness, family duty, work, transport, punishment, grief, access failure, or choice. The group sends the minimum useful update and does not demand a public explanation. A returning student receives current context and can decline the old role.

If a person was excluded, the record states rule, decision owner, reason, duration, appeal route, and repair. An apology or new invitation does not force return. The club redesigns the task when exclusion was caused by its space, channel, language, or schedule.

## Social closure and alumni contact


**Status:** Work decision — transition canon.

Graduation changes school access and daily contact while friendships may continue. Alumni contact uses current channels, chosen availability, and a defined request. A former club leader cannot summon current students or enter a school room by memory.

The group keeps a consent-based contact list with expiry and deletion rules. A farewell, archive, or reunion is optional and accessible. Social continuity is maintained by present consent, not by pretending the student years never ended.
