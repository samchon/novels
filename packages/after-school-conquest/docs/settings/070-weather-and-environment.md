# Weather and Environment

This file fixes how weather and environmental conditions alter Haneul's physical world without inventing real-world climate averages or changing the chronology in 030 and 040. It owns daylight as a usable resource, wet and dry surfaces, heat, cold, wind, visibility, indoor conditions, air quality, material exposure, and observation records. It does not own the 2019 flood event, the 2022 fire, city closure authority, school cancellation authority, or any plot assignment.

Weather entries use three standings. A `Source-verified framework` is an official warning or safety category actually supported by the cited current source. A `Work decision` is a fictional Haneul threshold or physical response chosen for consistent blocking. A `Contested or unresolved` condition is not left as an invisible guess: the later owner must either research it or use the bounded work range recorded here.

## Seasonal daylight and usable time

**Status:** Work decision for fictional daylight and exposure conditions; no real-world average is asserted.

Haneul measures daylight operationally rather than by a claimed sunrise table. The school forecourt, north street, bus shelter, and west covered connection are the four reference zones. A route is “daylit enough for ordinary visual wayfinding” when a person can distinguish the high-contrast sign, curb edge, and another person's approach without relying on a phone light. This is a physical observation condition, not a legal time or a promise that every person's vision is the same.

The March opening has winter-short mornings and a brighter afternoon; late April and May provide longer useful light; June and July leave the greatest daylight margin but add heat and storm exposure; August and September shift from bright evenings toward earlier darkness; October through February reduce the safe margin after school. These are seasonal Work decisions, not regional climatology. A later scene may use a measured sky condition, a streetlight, or a building shadow, but it cannot infer an exact clock time from “dark” alone.

The north gate canopy creates a 4-metre shadow band at low sun; the west annex connection has artificial fixtures at its bends; the market arcade blocks direct sky view; and the bus shelter's roof leaves the platform edge exposed. A person can therefore be in daylight at the curb and in shadow at the sign ten metres later. Family work and transport are resource limits: a route that is technically open may be unusable after a student's required departure time.

The Korea Meteorological Administration's public hazard guidance distinguishes forecast and safety response rather than supplying Haneul's fictional route times. Its heatwave safety guide is https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do, and its live warning page is https://www.weather.go.kr/weather/warning/status.jsp?printable=true. These are current official information pages accessed 2026-08-21; Haneul's seasonal daylight descriptions and visual-wayfinding test are Work decisions.

## Monsoon rain and saturated surfaces

**Status:** Source-verified hazard framework plus Work decision for Haneul surface states.

Rain changes a route through water film, reduced friction, obscured markings, soaked paper, roof runoff, and the time needed to open or close an umbrella without blocking another person. Haneul records four surface states: damp, actively wet, pooled, and unsafe. “Wet” does not automatically mean closed, and “open” does not mean equally accessible to a wheelchair, cane, stroller, or person carrying material.

The north civic sidewalk uses textured paving but becomes noisy under heavy rain. The school forecourt drains toward the east kerb; the west covered connection sheds water at two downspouts that must not discharge across the ramp landing. The market arcade protects overhead but can collect wind-driven rain at the lower passage edge. The bus shelter roof keeps the waiting strip dry only when wind is below the Work-defined transport threshold; otherwise a person must stand under cover without blocking boarding.

Paper notices swell at the market edge and youth-centre apron. A laminated sign remains readable but can glare; a digital notice can be inaccessible when a phone is wet or shared. The club cannot solve a wet route by adding volunteers to it. The school adviser may cancel a student task under 030, while the municipal flood-response office controls the public closure.

KMA's hazard guidance and live warning pages identify rain and other dangerous weather as conditions requiring public response: https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do and https://www.weather.go.kr/weather/warning/status.jsp?printable=true. The official pages are current guidance, not a direct source for Haneul's fictional surface thresholds. Haneul's four surface states, downspout placement, paper failure, and route responses are Work decisions.

## Flood warning as a physical state

**Status:** Work decision strictly preserving 030's fixed flood canon and authority.

The flood-warning state is entered when the municipal flood-response office's yellow Seongnae gauge reaches the 1.2-metre closure threshold specified in 030. At that point the lower market passage and bus-loop ramp are physically excluded from the available route, the channel and youth-centre basement are barred to volunteers, and the school adviser may cancel a student task. The adviser cannot issue or revoke the municipal closure. Nothing in this H2 changes the 2019-07-27 date, the three raised electrical cabinets, the pumps, or the 35-minute accessible detour.

The warning has three spatial readings. The municipal closure line is the barrier and notice at the lower passage and ramp. The school-side response is the adviser’s task cancellation or alternate above-ground instruction. The youth-centre response is the coordinator's basement and storage restriction. A person may be safe on the school parcel while the public route below is closed; a dry youth-centre lobby does not make its basement or channel-side door available.

The warning record carries gauge time, issuer, affected segment, last safe inspection, barrier location, alternate route, and reopening authority. A volunteer photograph of water is not the closure record; a school message is not the gauge; a family memory is not a pump log. These records may report different times because they serve different owners.

The Disaster and Safety Management Basic Act is current official law displayed effective 2026-02-01 at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?ancYnChk=0&chrClsCd=010202&efYd=20260611&lsiSeq=283851&urlMode=lsInfoP. ## Heat and humidity

**Status:** Work decision informed by current KMA heat-safety guidance; no local temperature average is asserted.

Heat affects the body, equipment, pavement, and the amount of work that can be completed safely. Haneul uses a heat state based on the KMA warning visible to the responsible office plus observed conditions at the forecourt, bus shelter, and west connection. The work states are ordinary, caution, and stop-for-outdoor-task. They are not national legal temperature thresholds and cannot be presented as such.

The south athletic field and asphalt bus loop retain heat after direct sun; the north forecourt has canopy shade; the library setback has tree shade that moves during the day; and the west connection is shaded but poorly ventilated on still afternoons. A person carrying a box reaches a different physical limit from a person walking empty-handed. A wheelchair user may experience ramp surface heat and a longer detour. Water stations in 050 and 060 are available only when their buildings are open and the route is safe.

Heat changes materials. Adhesive strips soften, ink fades, batteries warm, metal handles become uncomfortable, and a sealed audiovisual room accumulates heat if equipment runs without air movement. No club task may turn a heat notice into a competition. Adult supervision, rest, water, shade, and a shorter route are resources; a token, seal, or public promise cannot substitute for them.

KMA's current heatwave safety guide states public precautions and displays warning-related response guidance at https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do. The page is an official public-safety guide, not a fictional Haneul school rule. Haneul's three heat states, shade locations, material effects, and stop conditions are Work decisions, while 150 owns the school response and 060 owns municipal-space authority.

## Cold, frost, and winter surfaces

**Status:** Work decision for fictional winter surface and body-resource conditions.

Cold makes a route slower through clothing, gloves, reduced dexterity, and shorter comfortable waiting. Haneul's exposed north gate, bus platform, market arcade edge, and west annex connection have different wind and shelter conditions. The school lobby is warmer than the forecourt, but entering the lobby requires crossing the gate and threshold. A person who can wait indoors may still need to return across a cold or wet route.

Frost or ice is recorded when the surface reflects light irregularly, a shoe or caster slips in a controlled check, or facilities staff marks the route unsafe. A visually dry ramp can remain slick in shadow. The 1:20 west-annex ramp is a design value, not a guarantee of traction; its non-slip surface, handrails, landings, and canopy reduce but do not eliminate risk. Salt, grit, mats, and a temporary barrier are maintenance responses, not permission for a student to inspect an unsafe surface.

Cold also changes paper and electronics. A phone battery may lose charge, a laminated sign may become brittle, and a wet glove may not operate a small control. Family work and transport are part of the resource model: a late bus can extend exposure without changing the school’s published schedule. The school office or municipal authority may close a route, but this H2 does not assign the decision.

KMA's official warning page is the source-verified channel for current cold, snow, wind, and other hazard notices: https://www.weather.go.kr/weather/warning/status.jsp?printable=true. No claim about Haneul's winter temperature, snowfall frequency, or frost date is externally asserted. The surface states, reference zones, material effects, and fictional maintenance responses are Work decisions.

## Wind, lightning, and falling objects

**Status:** Source-verified hazard categories plus Work decision for Haneul's exposed edges.

The campus and city have four wind-sensitive zones: the north canopy, the west covered connection, the south athletic field, and the market arcade's open edge. Loose paper, umbrellas, temporary signs, and lightweight display boards are vulnerable before a person feels a dangerous gust. The service lane's vehicle movement can add local turbulence around the civic building corner. A sign tied to a fence is not automatically safe because it is outside the school parcel.

Lightning changes the availability of the sports field, channel ledge, bus platform, and open market edge. The safest available building is not necessarily the nearest building if the route crosses an exposed surface. The school adviser or city authority may move people to shelter under their procedures; a club leader cannot invent a lightning clearance interval or direct people across a closed route. A weather notice changes physical access, not property ownership.

Falling-object risk includes a branch, loose canopy panel, sign, construction material, or market awning. The observation record names the object, location, wind condition, people moved, barrier, and inspection owner. A photograph documents appearance but does not certify structural safety. The Education Facilities Safety and Maintenance Standard frames school safety and maintenance objects; direct current official page: https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2100000208251.

## Visibility, fog, and darkness

**Status:** Work decision for observation limits; no real-world visibility average is asserted.

Visibility is recorded from the observer's position, not from a narrator's all-seeing map. In clear daylight a person at the north gate can see the forecourt, main door, and the first section of the lobby. The market support and arcade break the line toward the lower passage. Fog, rain film, glare, darkness, a bus, or a delivery vehicle can reduce that line without moving any building.

Haneul uses four visibility states: sign-readable, route-readable, person-readable, and unsafe-to-rely-on. “Person-readable” means a moving person can be distinguished from a fixed object; it does not mean face or intention can be identified. The audiovisual booth's wired glass gives occupancy movement but not readable documents. The library vestibule gives a partial silhouette but not a private conversation. Later blocking must specify distance, lighting, angle, and obstruction before claiming recognition.

Artificial light is layered. The school forecourt, lobby, west connection, bus shelter, and market arcade have different fixtures and shadows. A phone flashlight helps a user inspect a local surface but can cause glare for another person or conceal a sign behind the beam. The city's emergency marking and school's exit marking remain separate systems. No club map arrow is an emergency light.

KMA's official current hazard and warning pages provide forecast and warning context but do not establish Haneul's fictional visibility categories: https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do and https://www.weather.go.kr/weather/warning/status.jsp?printable=true. Haneul's sight distances, states, fixtures, and observer protocol are Work decisions.

## Drainage, runoff, and standing water

**Status:** Work decision consistent with 030's flood infrastructure and 050/060 route geometry.

The school forecourt drains east toward a covered channel that does not connect directly to the Seongnae flood channel without municipal infrastructure. The west-annex ramp has a crossfall away from the door and a downspout discharge that terminates outside the 1.5-metre landing. The market arcade drains toward the lower passage, where the city pumps and gauge operate. The bus platform uses separate curb inlets; a blocked inlet can create a puddle without reaching the 1.2-metre flood trigger.

Standing water is recorded by depth band: film, ankle-depth hazard, wheel-obstructing pool, and closure condition. These bands are Work descriptions, not statutory thresholds. A film can make a sign reflection misleading; an ankle-depth pool can hide a grate; a wheel-obstructing pool can stop a route long before the municipal closure. ## Indoor thermal and acoustic conditions

**Status:** Work decision for physical indoor conditions; no unverified building-performance claim.

The main block has three indoor acoustic zones. The lobby and central spine reflect footsteps and trolley noise; ordinary classrooms absorb some sound through doors and wall panels; the gym and auditorium carry voices across large volumes. The west audiovisual booth is more isolated with its door closed but shares corridor sound when the door opens. Mechanical noise is strongest near the east service corridor and south gym junction. A person can be visually near another person while acoustically unable to hear them.

Thermal comfort differs by orientation. North classrooms receive stable indirect light; south rooms and the gym warm faster in direct sun; the west annex receives late-day heat through its exterior wall; the library vestibule is buffered by two doors. These are fictional qualitative conditions, not measured energy performance or national climate claims. A fan, open window, or portable device changes sound and access; it cannot be added invisibly to a scene.

The audiovisual booth's two breakers, twelve-seat limit, separate battery bench, and adult-present charging remain 030/150 constraints. Heat or stale air can require the room to close without proving an electrical fault. A school facilities response is separate from the city or youth-centre response. The physical condition can be observed by a student, but the authority to declare the room safe belongs to the responsible school process.

The Education Facilities Safety and Maintenance Standard, effective 2022-01-14, provides an official maintenance framework at https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2100000208251. ## Air quality, pollen, and smoke

**Status:** Work decision using official weather and safety-information channels; no fictional pollution reading is presented as measured fact.

Air conditions affect outdoor work, ventilation, smell, and perception. Haneul records an air-quality state only when an official alert or a facilities observation identifies it: ordinary, caution, or indoor-preference. The state does not infer a pollutant concentration. A person with asthma, allergy, migraine, or smoke sensitivity may need a different participation mode without disclosing a diagnosis to the club.

The school north gate receives street exhaust; the south field has more open air but less shelter; the west covered connection can trap smell after rain; the library vestibule offers a two-door buffer; and the youth-centre basement is not an air-quality refuge because it is restricted during flood warnings and has its own storage risks. Smoke from a fire or electrical fault is an emergency condition, not a normal air-quality state. The 2022 fire history remains in 030 and 050; no culprit or new event is assigned here.

Ventilation changes a room's sound, temperature, and door state. An open window can improve air but admit rain, noise, pollen, or a sight line. A closed room can reduce exterior exposure but accumulate heat or stale air. The responsible facilities process chooses the safe condition; the club cannot promise that opening a door is harmless. A public notice must distinguish official alert from a student's smell report.

KMA's current public weather and hazard pages provide the official information channel for weather-related air and safety notices: https://www.weather.go.kr/weather/warning/status.jsp?printable=true and https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do. ## Weather-dependent material and clothing

**Status:** Work decision for material and bodily resource effects.

Paper, adhesive, electronics, metal, fabric, and painted surfaces respond differently to weather. A paper notice can swell at the market arcade edge; adhesive can loosen on a wet board; a phone can lose charge in cold; a metal handrail can become hot in direct sun; and a fabric banner can pull against a tie in wind. These are material mechanics for later blocking, not a claim that a particular item always fails.

The club's physical materials have storage states: dry indoor, covered ventilated, exposed temporary, and quarantined after water or smoke contact. The fire-rated paper archive in the west annex is separate from batteries; 030 and 050 own that relation. A wet task card is evidence of exposure, not automatically evidence of the time or person responsible. Receipts, keys, and official records require their own record owner under 150 and 020.

Clothing changes movement and visibility. A hood narrows peripheral vision, gloves reduce small-button dexterity, a wet sleeve can make a handrail slippery, and a heavy bag changes ramp effort. A person may choose an observer role, remote contribution, or a shorter route. That choice is consent and resource management, not disloyalty to a game territory. No token or seal may compel a student to work in unsafe weather.

KMA's official public safety guidance is the source-verified route for warnings and precautions: https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/heatwave.do and https://www.weather.go.kr/weather/warning/status.jsp?printable=true. ## Environmental observation protocol

**Status:** Work decision establishing a repeatable observation record without converting weather into plot instructions.

An environmental observation records date and time, observer position, route segment, official warning state if any, surface state, visibility state, temperature or sensation only when measured by an identified instrument, wind effect, water or debris, accessible-route width, obstruction owner if known, and action taken. “Cold,” “loud,” or “blocked” alone is insufficient. The observer must say whether the statement is measured, reported, or inferred.

The school-side observation code uses `E-S` for school parcel, `E-C` for city route, and `E-Y` for youth-centre property. It never replaces the G-L01 task code or a municipal incident number. A school adviser may attach an `E-C` observation to a cancelled student task, but cannot convert it into a city closure. A youth-centre coordinator may attach an `E-Y` record to a basement restriction, but cannot edit the Seongnae gauge.

Observations expire as physical descriptions when the surface, barrier, light, or weather state changes. A dated photograph can preserve appearance but must identify direction and scale. A later writer may use the observation as a constraint only if the location and state match; no generic “it was raining” sentence can establish that a ramp, bus bay, or classroom was inaccessible.
