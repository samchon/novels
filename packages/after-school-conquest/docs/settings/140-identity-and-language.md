**Status:** Work decision — representation canon.

Characters may describe themselves, decline a label, use different words with different audiences, or change a label over time. The narrator does not assign an identity from appearance, accent, paperwork, clothing, disability aid, family origin, or relationship. A category used by an institution may be necessary for a form while remaining incomplete as lived description.

The prose gives a person ordinary preferences, expertise, humour, and contradiction. No identity is a shorthand for virtue, danger, poverty, foreignness, or access wisdom. When a label matters to a decision, the text states who uses it, for what purpose, and whether the person has corrected it.

**Status:** Work decision — linguistic evidence rule.

A translation records source, audience, purpose, and uncertainty. Words such as *nunchi* or *jeong* are retained only when their cultural work cannot be carried by a flat synonym; surrounding action conveys enough meaning for an English reader. A translator may provide alternatives and ask the speaker to choose.

The setting forbids “perfect translation” claims when register, joke, legal force, or emotional implication changes. An official translation may have a responsible service; a peer paraphrase is labelled as such. Misunderstanding is repaired through clarification, not by making the non-English speaker magically fluent in the narrator’s needs.

**Status:** Work decision — dignity rule.

A person may decline a label, translation, photograph, interview, pronoun explanation, family history, or public correction. The founders may record that a request was declined without recording the private reason. Some identity questions remain unresolved because the person has not chosen to answer.

Unfinished knowledge is not a narrative defect. The setting protects a person's right to be known through ordinary action rather than mined for a secret. Any later change in self-description is handled as current truth with a documented transition where an institution needs one, not as evidence that an earlier self was fraudulent.
