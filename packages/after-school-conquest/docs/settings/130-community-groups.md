# Community Groups

Haneul contains groups whose purposes overlap without making them one public. This file fixes membership, representation, access, and resource boundaries for neighbourhood and civic groups. No group is a decorative crowd or an automatic ally.

## Market traders and stall operators

**Status:** Work decision — fictional civic canon.

Market traders manage their own stalls, stock, opening routines, deliveries, and customer relationships within applicable city rules. One trader cannot promise that every stall accepts a route change, photograph, survey, or public notice. The lower passage and bus-loop ramp retain the flood constraints in `030` and `060`.

The founders ask before measuring a threshold, blocking a loading space, recording a voice, or using a stall's name. A trader may answer from personal experience rather than official authority. The ledger identifies which stall, date, condition, and owner a report belongs to.

## Residents and apartment committees

**Status:** Work decision — neighbourhood canon.

Residents experience noise, lighting, drainage, traffic, safety, rent, disability access, and children’s routes differently. An apartment committee or residents’ association may deliberate a shared property matter under its own rules; it does not own the school, market, public road, or city flood gauge.

The founders seek a representative range of residents and state whether a comment is personal or committee-approved. A petition records signatories and purpose; signatures do not grant access to private addresses. Residents may refuse a survey or ask that a route be described without identifying their building.

## Bus drivers and transport workers

**Status:** Work decision — transport interface.

Drivers and dispatch staff know operational conditions from their roles but cannot independently change city closure status, school policy, or passenger eligibility. A driver’s spoken estimate is time-and-condition evidence, not a guaranteed timetable. The bus interchange geometry and detour remain those in `050` and `060`.

Questions identify route, stop, direction, accessibility need, and date. A driver is not asked to disclose a passenger’s private difficulty. When a ramp or passage closes, the group uses the authorized notice and a safe alternative rather than persuading a worker to improvise.

## Library users and civic learners

**Status:** Work decision — public-learning canon.

The library serves readers, students, job seekers, children, elders, and people needing quiet or public internet. A library card, seat, computer, archive, or staff recommendation has its own access rule. The library frontage is a public-space resource, not an extension of the school club.

The founders may request a public source, reserve a permitted table, or ask for an accessible format. They cannot photograph another user's screen, monopolize a study area, or claim that a librarian has endorsed their interpretation. A public source remains distinguishable from a fictional Haneul decision.

## Youth-centre participants and volunteers

**Status:** Work decision — inherited facility canon.

Youth-centre participants include children, teens, guardians, staff, and volunteers with different permissions. The coordinator owns the facility response plan; city flood-response owns the gauge and road closure. During a warning, no volunteer or student enters the channel or basement, regardless of goodwill.

A volunteer role states training, supervision, room, equipment, and stop rule. A participant can attend without volunteering. The founders do not recruit a vulnerable child to demonstrate a route or ask a volunteer to carry a risk that belongs to the centre or city.

## Faith, cultural, and language associations

**Status:** Work decision — pluralism canon.

Associations may organize worship, language practice, food, mutual aid, arts, or cultural events. Membership, belief, ancestry, and language ability are separate. A person can participate in an association without representing every member or endorsing every statement made by its leadership.

The founders ask permission before using a ritual, translation, name, symbol, food, or photograph. They avoid turning a community practice into an exotic explanation. A translated leaflet preserves the association’s stated purpose and any access or safety conditions; it does not become a school directive merely because it is well designed.

## Disability and access advocacy

**Status:** Work decision — consultation boundary.

An access advocacy group can contribute lived experience, design critique, route testing, and policy knowledge. It is not a single voice for every disabled person. The founders state whether they seek paid consultation, voluntary comment, or a public endorsement, and do not use an individual’s disclosure as a generic case study.

Built dimensions remain checked against `050` and the official accessibility standards cited there. A group's recommendation is evidence or advice according to its source and scope; the school facilities office or public owner still decides implementation within its authority.

## Worker and mutual-aid networks

**Status:** Work decision — social infrastructure.

Workers may exchange childcare, food, transport, translation, job leads, or emergency support. Mutual aid depends on trust, time, inventory, and privacy. A founder receiving help records the practical obligation only with permission and never treats aid as a public testimonial owed to the giver.

The group cannot promise a mutual-aid network’s capacity during exams, storms, illness, or labour disruption. A request states urgency, location, accessibility, and what can safely be declined. Public institutions are contacted when the need exceeds informal capacity.

## Small businesses and service counters

**Status:** Work decision — commercial boundary.

Printers, cafés, repair shops, pharmacies, and convenience stores are independent service points with prices, hours, stock, and privacy limits. A receipt proves a transaction, not an endorsement. The founders ask before using a business’s logo, staff quote, customer image, or counter as a meeting point.

Commercial goodwill does not permit students to leave equipment, block an entrance, or solicit donations without agreement. A discount or donated item is recorded with terms and expiry. A business may refuse service or public association without becoming an antagonist.

## Public meeting and petition rules

**Status:** Work decision — civic participation canon.

A public meeting has an organizer, place, access conditions, agenda or purpose, speaking rule, and record owner. Attendance does not equal consent to publication. A petition identifies its requested decision, recipient, date, and method of verification; a signature cannot bind a person to unrelated claims.

The founders distinguish a meeting minute from a transcript and a personal account from an official resolution. They ask whether the meeting is open, whether minors need an adult, and how an attendee can request accommodation or correction. A loud room can still fail access even when entry is technically open.

## Media, notice, and public reputation

**Status:** Work decision — publication boundary.

Local journalists, bloggers, school newsletters, and public notice boards reach different audiences and carry different editorial responsibility. A quote is confirmed with speaker, context, date, and approval scope. An unattributed rumour is not made credible by a camera or a large following.

The founders may correct a factual error through the same channel or the accountable issuer. They do not expose a minor’s address, family hardship, disability, or disciplinary history to win a public argument. A public-interest claim still uses minimum necessary detail.

## Community boundary ledger

**Status:** Work decision — executable canon.

Each group entry records name, purpose, membership boundary, representative or contact, physical host, decision authority, public contact route, accessibility condition, and expiry or review date. If a group has no formal representative, the record says so. If a person speaks only personally, the record preserves that scope.

The ledger prevents a fictional community from becoming a faceless resource pool. A request can be declined, redirected, or answered partially. The founders retain unresolved questions and avoid assigning a whole district a single opinion for narrative convenience.
