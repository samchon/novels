# Governance and Accountability

This file defines how collective decisions are proposed, authorized, recorded, challenged, and closed across the school club and its public interfaces. It does not predetermine which decision wins.

## Decision classes

**Status:** Work decision — governance canon.

Every request is classified as informational, advisory, operational, permission-seeking, spending, safety-critical, record-affecting, or emergency. The class determines the minimum owner and review. A student may gather information and propose a route without being authorized to operate it.

If a request changes class—for example, a map becoming a public closure notice—the record reopens the authority check. A meeting agenda cannot hide a safety decision under “miscellaneous.”

## Proponent, owner, and affected party

**Status:** Work decision — role separation.

The proponent explains the request and its reason. The owner has authority to accept, reject, pause, or route it. An affected party may identify impact, ask for accommodation, and challenge a record without becoming the decision owner. These roles can overlap only when the conflict is disclosed.

The founders never describe “everyone agreed” without a participant list and decision scope. A person absent from a meeting may still be materially affected. A silent observer is not a consenting signatory.

## Agenda and quorum

**Status:** Work decision — meeting mechanics.

A meeting notice states host, purpose, date, location, access, materials, decision sought, and attendance method. Quorum is the minimum defined by the host’s rule; if no formal quorum exists, the record says who attended and whether the result is recommendation or decision.

Late arrivals, translated contributions, remote participation, and access breaks are recorded without treating them as lesser presence. A meeting may pause when a participant cannot hear, read, enter, or safely remain. The chair does not close debate merely because the preferred outcome is obvious.

## Evidence packet and versioning

**Status:** Work decision — decision integrity.

An evidence packet identifies claim, source, date, condition, uncertainty, affected group, and requested decision. Maps and tables have version numbers and owners. A photograph shows capture time and location only when reliable; it does not prove an unseen condition.

Participants receive enough material to understand the decision, with private fields redacted. A late correction links the old version and explains the effect. The final record cannot silently use a draft measurement that the owner rejected.

## Consent-based student decisions

**Status:** Work decision — club governance.

Student decisions require an intelligible question, scope, response method, deadline, and refusal route. A vote may decide a club preference but cannot authorize a school facility, city closure, safeguarding disclosure, or legal commitment outside student jurisdiction.

The club records abstentions, nonresponses, and conflicts separately. A majority cannot remove a minority’s access or privacy without the competent authority and a specific rule. A tie triggers redesign, referral, or an owner decision rather than invented unanimity.

## Delegation and revocation

**Status:** Work decision — authority lifecycle.

Delegation states task, boundary, start and end, supervision, equipment, reporting, and revocation condition. A delegate cannot re-delegate a key, account, public promise, or safety duty without the owner’s permission. Informal trust does not extend a delegation past its expiry.

The owner can pause or revoke a delegation when conditions change, a report is missing, a hazard appears, or the delegated person asks to stop. Revocation includes return of property and communication to affected people. It does not erase work already performed or records already issued.

## Budget and procurement control

**Status:** Work decision — material governance.

A spending request states purpose, amount, source, purchaser, approval, receipt, storage, and disposal. Personal payment is not assumed to be reimbursable. A donation records owner, restrictions, condition, and whether it transfers title.

The group does not buy hazardous equipment, evade school purchasing rules, or spend a grant outside its conditions. A cheap object that creates access, fire, privacy, or maintenance costs is not automatically economical. The budget ledger includes unpaid labour and transport burden where the activity depends on them.

## Safety stop and restart

**Status:** Work decision — inherited safety rule.

Any participant may call a safety stop when an observable hazard appears. The stop moves people to a safe position, not into a debate about courage. The competent owner assesses the condition; students do not self-certify a restart.

Restart requires named owner, changed condition, inspection or confirmation where required, updated route, communication, and a review time. A weather warning, flood gauge, fire inspection, broken lift, or missing adult can keep a task stopped even when the original plan is important.

## Record of decision

**Status:** Work decision — documentary canon.

A decision record contains question, options considered, evidence, participants or representative scope, conflicts, decision, conditions, owner, effective period, communication list, and appeal or review path. It distinguishes a reason from a personal motive and a recommendation from an authorization.

The record is concise enough to audit and detailed enough to prevent memory wars. If no decision was reached, it says unresolved and identifies the next step. A polished summary never substitutes for the underlying authority.

## Transparency and minimum disclosure

**Status:** Work decision — privacy balance.

Transparency means affected people can understand the decision and challenge it; it does not mean publishing every personal detail. The group shares purpose, owner, criteria, dates, resource effects, and route to correction. Private evidence is summarized or redacted when possible.

A public notice never names a student to prove that an accommodation exists. A correction may be public while the underlying family or health fact remains private. The holder documents why a disclosure level was chosen and revisits it when conditions change.

## Review, audit, and expiration

**Status:** Work decision — continuity rule.

Every continuing decision has a review date or trigger: new school term, changed route, weather warning, equipment failure, graduation, budget exhaustion, or a correction request. The reviewer checks actual condition against stated purpose and records what changed.

An expired permission is not a failed person; it is a closed authority. Renewal is a new decision with current facts. A historical decision remains useful as history but cannot silently govern a different date, group, room, or legal status.

## Complaint, remedy, and accountability

**Status:** Work decision — repair canon.

A complaint identifies conduct or outcome, affected person, date, evidence, requested remedy, and receiving owner. The process protects the complainant from retaliation and does not require public confrontation. A remedy can be correction, restoration, compensation, access redesign, role removal, training, or a reasoned refusal.

The owner reports outcome and limits without disclosing protected details. If the owner is conflicted or lacks power, the complaint is routed upward or outward. The founders preserve the complaint’s existence even when a remedy is partial.

## Governance after graduation

**Status:** Work decision — alumni boundary.

On 2030-03-01 the founders may advise, volunteer, work, donate, or participate as alumni only through a current host and defined role. Former student leadership does not continue by inertia. School rooms, budgets, records, and student supervision require current authorization.

An alumni proposal names the receiving school or civic owner, current contact, term, data boundary, and safety condition. Historical influence is acknowledged without becoming present jurisdiction. A handoff is complete only when the new owner accepts it and affected users receive the current rule.
