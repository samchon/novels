
# Formal Motifs

These motifs are recurring forms through which the novel can make obligations, access, and memory legible. They are settings-level objects with owners and uses, not assigned scenes, reveals, or chapter beats.

## Map and scale bar


**Status:** Work decision — formal object canon.

A map carries title, maker, date, orientation, scale, source, version, conditions, and intended audience. A scale bar survives resizing better than a single verbal ratio. The map states whether a line is observed, reported, inferred, or proposed.

Maps never prove permission, current opening, consent, or bodily usability. A corrected map preserves old version and reason where safe. The physical envelopes in `050` and `060` supply the canonical dimensions and ownership boundaries.

## Ledger and balance


**Status:** Work decision — formal object canon.

A ledger lists resource or obligation, owner, beneficiary, amount or state, date, condition, source, confidence, and next review. Balance is not only arithmetic: time, attention, access, trust, care, and repair can be entries. The ledger distinguishes zero, unknown, refused, expired, and not applicable.

No ledger converts a person into property. A balance can reveal an unfair burden and trigger redesign, not a demand that the burden be repaid. Historical entries remain distinguishable from current availability.

## Key and access log


**Status:** Work decision — security motif.

A key log records key or credential identifier, custodian, purpose, issue time, return time, condition, witness or office, and exception. A key’s existence does not prove a door is safe or a person authorized to enter. The audiovisual booth key log and daily access remain school-owned under `030` and `150`.

Lost or late keys trigger owner notification and access review, not a private duplicate. A student cannot sign for an institutional key as a joke. Alumni return all school access unless a current host issues a new role.

## Receipt and proof of handoff


**Status:** Work decision — documentary motif.

A receipt states issuer, recipient, item or service, date, amount or condition, and reference. A photograph, message, signature, or witness may supplement it. Each form of proof has limits; an image of a parcel outside a door is not acceptance.

The motif is used to make hidden labour and ownership visible. A missing receipt remains a discrepancy. Forging, backdating, or deleting a receipt is never repaired by a later accurate total.

## Notice and correction slip


**Status:** Work decision — public communication motif.

A notice has issuer, audience, date, effective period, action, location, contact, access alternative, and version. A correction slip identifies the superseded statement, what changed, why, and where the correction applies. The same audience receives the correction when feasible.

The founders may translate or summarize but label their copy. A school, city, youth centre, or facilities notice retains its own issuer. An attractive poster without authority is a proposal, not a closure.

## Route arrow and detour marker


**Status:** Work decision — spatial motif.

An arrow specifies origin, destination, direction, date, surface, accessibility condition, and owner. A detour marker states why the ordinary route is unavailable, the alternative’s limitations, and update source. The 35-minute accessible flood detour remains a conditional work decision, not a universal guarantee.

Arrows do not cross barriers, water, fire zones, private doors, or a person’s refusal. A changed weather state invalidates a previously safe arrow. The marker is removed or revised by its host when the route changes.

## Clock, bell, and timestamp


**Status:** Work decision — temporal motif.

A time mark states timezone, clock source, precision, and whether it records start, observation, issue, receipt, or expiry. A school bell, phone time, office stamp, and city system may differ by seconds or by authority. The record preserves which clock made the claim.

Timestamps do not reveal motive. A late notice can be corrected without blaming the recipient. Academic and instructional boundaries remain those in `030` and `040`, not a decorative use of a bell.

## Weather gauge and condition strip


**Status:** Work decision — environmental motif.

A condition strip records rain, surface, visibility, temperature or heat state where relevant, wind, daylight, crowd, and route status at a stated place and time. It separates official warning from personal sensation. The yellow flood gauge is municipal; a notebook water mark is an observation.

The strip expires when the condition changes. It cannot certify future weather or substitute for an official closure. A dry morning does not erase a saturated basement risk.

## Room card and capacity


**Status:** Work decision — spatial governance motif.

A room card states host, dimensions, doors, route, capacity, furniture, equipment, supervision, accessibility conditions, noise, lighting, booking, and stop rules. Capacity is not merely chair count; it includes clearances, exit, air, and safe movement. The auditorium, gym, ordinary rooms, and audiovisual booth retain `050` values.

A room card is current only through the host’s inspection and booking. A full room can be unsafe before every chair is occupied. A twelve-seat audiovisual booth never becomes thirteen because a meeting is urgent.

## Consent line and withdrawal mark


**Status:** Work decision — relational motif.

A consent line identifies activity, audience, media, duration, data, risks, host, and withdrawal route. A withdrawal mark records that the person no longer authorizes the defined scope, without requiring a public reason. Consent does not authorize unrelated future uses.

The motif keeps participation visible as a living condition. A person can remain a friend, critic, or beneficiary after withdrawing. Institutional retention duties are explained rather than hidden in a small print clause.

## Repair tag and reopening label


**Status:** Work decision — maintenance motif.

A repair tag identifies asset, defect, date, interim control, owner, and next check. A reopening label states who inspected, what scope was cleared, effective time, conditions, and review. The tag is not a decorative sign and cannot be issued by a student without authority.

One cleared room does not clear a corridor, route, archive, or adjacent wing. A flood or fire record remains historical after repair. Users receive the current label and a route to report renewed failure.

## Handoff card and expiry


**Status:** Work decision — continuity motif.

A handoff card lists outgoing owner, incoming owner, object or obligation, current state, access, open risk, files, contact, effective date, and expiry or review. It is used at term change, absence, graduation, closure, and institutional transfer.

An unaccepted handoff remains pending. A former owner’s memory can help but cannot operate as current authorization. Expiry protects against permanent permissions disguised as tradition.
