# Settings Principles

Principles for authoring a work's canon in `docs/settings`. No item may be excluded; each item binds wherever its condition applies, and citing it asserts that compliance.

## Canon declaration {#canon-declaration}

State every canonical fact explicitly, and label its standing: sourced from a real document or body of scholarship, deliberately decided for this fiction, disputed between sources, or not yet decided. Never let the label drift — a decision presented as research, or an inference presented as a decision, corrupts every downstream citation of the fact.

When sources conflict, record the conflict itself and fix the canonical spelling, dating, or interpretation this work will use. When a matter is left open, say so; downstream layers may not resolve it silently.

Sources: [Jane Friedman's principles for historical fiction](https://janefriedman.com/dos-and-donts-of-writing-historical-fiction/), [Laterpress on the series bible as continuity record](https://www.laterpress.com/craft-of-writing/series-bible/)

## Constraint sufficiency {#constraint-sufficiency}

Make settings detailed enough that downstream writing never has to guess: world rules with their limits, costs, and exceptions; institutions, authority, and incentives; chronology, geography, travel, and material constraints; character resources, limitations, relationships, and knowledge; naming, language, viewpoint, and style conventions; the factual foundations the premise requires.

Sufficiency is judged by the downstream question it answers, not by word count. A rule without its exception, a resource without its quantity, or an authority without its boundary still forces a later scene to invent canon. Mark unresolved matters explicitly instead of filling gaps by habit.

Sources: [Laterpress on the series bible](https://www.laterpress.com/craft-of-writing/series-bible/), [Figments and Fables on series bibles for television and novels](https://figmentsandfables.com/2022/03/29/step-by-step-guide-to-writing-a-series-bible-for-television-and-novels-part-i-of-iii/)

## Internal coherence {#internal-coherence}

The setting system must hold together as one world: no contradictions between files, no impossible timelines, no undefined exceptions relied on elsewhere, no precision the declared sources cannot support, and no predetermined arc smuggled in as fact.

Detail that touches nothing — geography no route crosses, institutions no character meets, numbers no scene spends — is inert; either connect it to a constraint some choice can collide with, or cut it. Read the complete system before storylining begins and whenever a file changes.

## Execution neutrality {#execution-neutrality}

Settings define facts and constraints, not a compulsory scene schedule. They do not prescribe chapter assignments, reveal timing, planned deaths, dialogue, or scene solutions.

Causes, choices, and events belong to storylines; detailed scene execution belongs to scenarios; finished prose belongs to manuscripts. A setting that can only be satisfied by one specific scene played one specific way is a disguised scenario and must be rewritten as the underlying fact or constraint.

## Research integrity {#research-integrity}

Research claims that can change a setting or a scene; skip decorative trivia. For current, disputed, niche, legal, medical, scientific, historical, and cultural claims, consult primary documents, official records, scholarship, critical editions, museums, archives, and specialist institutions before secondary summaries.

Account for the bias and limits of each source, including whose voices it omits. Record disagreement and uncertainty instead of flattening them; paraphrase sources and preserve their material qualifications. Link externally checkable claims to supporting sources — the internal evidence graph verifies structure, never external truth.

Sources: [Jane Friedman's principles for historical fiction](https://janefriedman.com/dos-and-donts-of-writing-historical-fiction/), [Novlr on researching authentic history for fiction](https://www.novlr.org/the-reading-room/how-to-research-and-bring-authentic-history-to-your-fiction)

## Quantitative consistency {#quantitative-consistency}

Check arithmetic, units, chronology, travel time, ages, currency, capacity, and resource flows directly; never trust that plausible-sounding numbers cohere. Distances must survive the declared means of travel, incomes must survive the declared prices, and ages must survive the declared dates.

Precision is a promise: a number stated exactly will be cited exactly downstream. Where the truth is a range or an estimate, declare the range rather than inventing false exactness.
