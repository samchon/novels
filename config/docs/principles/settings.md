# Settings Principles

Principles for authoring a work's canon in `docs/settings`. No item may be excluded; each item binds wherever its condition applies, and citing it asserts that compliance. Where a file holds no unit an item governs, the citation states that scope fact.

## Canon and its sources {#canon-and-sources}

State every canonical fact explicitly and label its standing: established by a document or a body of scholarship, decided for this fiction, disputed between sources, or not yet decided. Never let the label drift — a decision presented as research, or an inference presented as a decision, corrupts every downstream citation of the fact. Where a matter is left open, say so, because no downstream layer may resolve it silently.

Do the research that can change a setting or a scene, and skip decorative trivia. For current, disputed, niche, legal, medical, scientific, historical, and cultural claims, consult primary documents, official records, scholarship, critical editions, museums, archives, and specialist institutions before secondary summaries. Account for each source's bias and limits, including whose voices it omits. Paraphrase rather than copy, preserving the material qualifications a source attaches to its claim; record disagreement rather than flattening it, fixing the spelling, dating, or reading this work will use; and link externally checkable claims to their support, since the evidence graph verifies structure and never external truth.

Sources: [Jane Friedman's principles for historical fiction](https://janefriedman.com/dos-and-donts-of-writing-historical-fiction/), [Novlr on researching authentic history for fiction](https://www.novlr.org/the-reading-room/how-to-research-and-bring-authentic-history-to-your-fiction), [Laterpress on the series bible as continuity record](https://www.laterpress.com/craft-of-writing/series-bible/)

## Constraint sufficiency {#constraint-sufficiency}

Make settings detailed enough that downstream writing never has to guess: world rules with their limits, costs, and exceptions; institutions, authority, and incentives; chronology, geography, travel, and material constraints; naming, language, viewpoint, and style conventions; the factual foundations the premise requires.

Sufficiency is judged by the downstream question it answers, not by word count. A rule without its exception, a resource without its quantity, or an authority without its boundary still forces a later scene to invent canon. Mark unresolved matters explicitly instead of filling gaps by habit.

Sources: [Laterpress on the series bible](https://www.laterpress.com/craft-of-writing/series-bible/), [Figments and Fables on series bibles for television and novels](https://figmentsandfables.com/2022/03/29/step-by-step-guide-to-writing-a-series-bible-for-television-and-novels-part-i-of-iii/)

## Figures and relations {#figures-and-relations}

Every figure the work relies on gets its own addressable unit: what it wants and refuses, what it can and cannot do, what it knows and believes wrongly, what it owes and is owed, how it speaks, and what would make it act against its habit. A figure that exists only inside another figure's entry cannot be cited by the scene that needs it.

A relation is a fact in its own right, not the sum of two entries, and it is recorded from both sides wherever the sides differ: what binds the pair, what each believes the other believes, what debt, rank, secret, or obligation runs between them, and what has already changed it. Households, offices, crews, and factions hold relations that no member's entry contains; those take units of their own.

Sources: [Palmer on social and intermental minds in fiction](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/24307), [Laterpress on the series bible](https://www.laterpress.com/craft-of-writing/series-bible/)

## Internal coherence {#internal-coherence}

The setting system must hold together as one world: no contradictions between files, no impossible timelines, no undefined exceptions relied on elsewhere, no precision the declared sources cannot support, and no predetermined arc smuggled in as fact. Check quantities directly instead of trusting that plausible-sounding numbers cohere — arithmetic, units, chronology, travel time, ages, currency, capacity, resource flows — so that distances survive the declared means of travel, incomes the declared prices, and ages the declared dates. Precision is a promise: where the truth is a range, declare the range instead of inventing exactness.

Detail that touches nothing — geography no route crosses, institutions no figure meets, numbers no scene spends — is inert; connect it to a constraint some choice can collide with, or cut it. Read the complete system before storylining begins and whenever a file changes.

Sources: [Figments and Fables on series bibles for television and novels](https://figmentsandfables.com/2022/03/29/step-by-step-guide-to-writing-a-series-bible-for-television-and-novels-part-i-of-iii/), [Jane Friedman's principles for historical fiction](https://janefriedman.com/dos-and-donts-of-writing-historical-fiction/)

## Execution neutrality {#execution-neutrality}

Settings define facts and constraints, not a compulsory scene schedule. They do not prescribe chapter assignments, reveal timing, planned deaths, dialogue, or scene solutions.

Causes, choices, and events belong to storylines; detailed scene execution belongs to scenarios; the finished work belongs to the last layer. A setting that can only be satisfied by one specific scene played one specific way is a disguised scenario and must be rewritten as the underlying fact or constraint.

Sources: [Plan-and-Write](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33017378), [Detailed Outline Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10077)
