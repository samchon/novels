# Common Principles

Principles every storyline, scenario, and manuscript file adopts, whatever the work's subject, genre, or ending. No item may be excluded.

## Narrative throughline and unit handover {#narrative-throughline}

Every sequence, chapter, and scene of a long-form work is more than a good standalone episode: it inherits and transforms one of the questions, desires, pressures, relationships, or changes the work holds over time. When a unit has a direct successor, the exit state of the earlier unit operates as the entry condition of the next. Unresolved pressure must persist, worsen, transform, resolve at a cost, or be deliberately replaced; it never disappears without account.

A unit that opens a new character, place, era, or event must connect to the reader's already-formed investment. The connection may be the same character's continuing goal, another character carrying an earlier consequence, a moved object or piece of information, a repeated image with altered meaning, or a cause answered by its delayed effect. A transition is never a bare "and then"; the reader must be able to tell whether it is a "therefore", a "but", a "nevertheless", or an "at this price".

This principle does not demand physical continuity of time and place. A work may leap decades, continents, and focal characters, or show a result before returning to its cause. A transition earns the leap by revealing, early, which question, object, emotion, relationship, or consequence the reader carries into the new time and place — not by filling the calendar with explanation. If, after the jump, the reader is still reading the next transformation of the same work, the leap is a connection; if characters and pressures are all abandoned and a new premise must be learned from zero, it is a break.

An H2 owns a promise and an irreversible change in the whole work; an H3 owns the options altered by the previous chapter's outcome; an H4 owns the actions and reactions in which that choice actually collides. If deleting a unit or moving it far away leaves the surrounding comprehension, tension, character judgment, and the ending's meaning almost unchanged, strengthen its coupling to the throughline, merge it, or cut it.

Not every unit must launch the immediately next event. The last event of a local branch, an aftermath, a side event of a parallel front, or the final scene of the work may instead close its pressure at a real cost, change the meaning of its parent unit, or leave a state, contrast, or piece of information collected later. Do not force a nonexistent forward hook onto such units — but do not preserve a unit merely for being a self-contained event if cutting it changes nothing in character understanding, pressure, or the ending.

Chronicle, episodic, ensemble, fragmentary, and non-causal forms are not exempt. They may replace event causality with accumulating perspective, contrast, consciousness, place, or formal rule, but the arrangement and repetition must change the reader's interpretation step by step and be collected by the ending.

Sources: [Trabasso & van den Broek on causal networks](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(85)90049-X), [Trabasso & Sperry on causal relatedness and importance](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(85)90048-8), [Zwaan, Langston & Graesser's event-indexing model](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x)

## Reader investment and character accumulation {#reader-investment}

The work never substitutes information volume or crisis size for the reader's reason to read the next unit. It builds a concrete question the reader holds, an outcome the reader wants changed, and a choice not yet settled, and it sustains whichever axis of investment the work needs — comprehension, attention, emotional involvement, or presence in the story world — through scene-level action and sensation.

The most basic cue for understanding a character is the current goal, the reason it is wanted, and the resistance and cost accepted while pursuing it. Show what a character protects, abandons, and affects in daily life before explaining a résumé or a trait sheet. The reader need not like or morally endorse a character, but must be able to predict and reinterpret the character's choices from that character's own knowledge, desires, relationships, and pressures.

The reader's model of a character accumulates unit by unit. A new choice confirms or complicates earlier successes, failures, lies, wounds, debts, attachments, and habits; a character is never reset to an amnesiac functionary to make the plot move. Place an emotional peak or a loss only after the reader has experienced the value and the ordinary state of what is at stake, and scale it to the accumulated meaning.

Investment is not identical to constant crisis or fast pacing. Waiting, labor, humor, observation, and recovery contribute when they change the reader's question or the character's goal. Conversely, exposition, procedure, and event summary that change neither a character's judgment nor the world's response cannot justify a scene by information alone.

Sources: [Green & Brock on narrative transportation](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701), [Busselle & Bilandzic's narrative engagement scale](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213260903287259), [Cohen on identification with characters](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327825MCS0403_01), [character goal pursuit and narrative engagement](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2019.1601569)

## Causal progression {#causal-progression}

A significant event in an ordered narrative follows intelligibly from earlier choices, conditions, chance, or change. Each event connects to its neighbors through at least one of cause, obstruction, reaction, or consequence, and the work's central changes belong to a causal chain running from opening to ending. If deleting or swapping the immediately preceding unit would leave the current unit's comprehension, pressure, choice, and consequence almost unchanged, the link is weak: redesign the order or the function.

Juxtaposition, montage, fragmentation, and nonlinear arrangement are available, but they are not exemptions from mere listing. Such forms need an alternative connective principle the reader can track — a recurring question, image, space, consciousness, procedure, or contrast — and rearranging them must change meaning or emotional pressure.

Conflict may arise not only from malice but from incompatible desires, values, information, resources, environment, and time. When chance produces a decisive result, match it to the degree of plausibility and contingency the work has promised, and never delete a core problem by sudden convenience.

Sources: [Trabasso & van den Broek on causal networks](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(85)90049-X), [Trabasso & Sperry on causal relatedness and importance](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(85)90048-8), [Zwaan, Langston & Graesser's event-indexing model](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x)

## Canon integrity {#canon-integrity}

Use canon exactly as the settings declare it: never contradict a declared fact, never resolve a matter the settings leave open, and never promote an undecided matter to fact by inference alone.

When a draft needs a new fact, do not smuggle it into prose: fix it first in the work package's `docs/settings`, then use it.

## Specificity {#specificity}

Prefer the objects, habits, sensations, comparisons, and choices of this work's situations, narrators, characters, and environments over abstract evaluative words and the auto-completion of convention. Choose detail by its contribution to the scene's function and focus, not by quantity.

Inspect familiar metaphors, stock gestures, uniform sentence rhythm, and modifiers that state an emotion on the scene's behalf; distinguish deliberate use from unexamined repetition. Distinctiveness comes from the work's internal context and mode of expression, not from random strangeness.

Sources: [Boston University on concrete action](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2019/how-to-create-suspense/), [Purdue OWL on building and revealing characters](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/fiction-basics/building_and_revealing_characters.html)

## Hierarchical refinement {#hierarchical-refinement}

The writing process refines in the order `settings and principles → storylines → scenarios → manuscripts`, and the three narrative artifacts share one hierarchy.

An H2 is a sequence carrying one central question, pressure, or change to its end; an H3 is a chapter that develops it by changing the available options and their costs; an H4 is a scene continuous in time, place, focalization, and function. Each unit's entry state arises from the previous unit's exit state, and even across cuts in time, place, or focus, at least one of the prior outcome, an unresolved question, a moved object, or a continued desire is handed over.

A scenario is not a film script but a scene specification fixing entry state, focalization, scene function, transition, information change, and exit state, adding goal and obstruction where characters act. Its entry states why this scene is needed now; its exit leaves, as workable fact, what the next scene differs by. It does not write the finished prose in advance.

Sources: [Plan-and-Write](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33017378), [Detailed Outline Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10077), [Dynamic Hierarchical Outlining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13575), [event-indexing model](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x)

## Revision order {#revision-order}

Revise from large decisions to small expressions: first the whole structure and its intended change, then the function and order of chapters and scenes, last sentences and diction.

When a world fact changes, start from settings; when an event's cause changes, from storylines; when scene execution changes, from scenarios; when only expression changes, revise the manuscript alone. When a discovery in a lower layer changes an upper decision, update the upper document first and come back down.

Sources: [University College Dublin on revising fiction](https://www.ucd.ie/writingcentre/t4media/Creative%20Writing%20Revising%20and%20Editing%20Fiction.pdf)

## Evidence discipline {#evidence-discipline}

The H2, H3, and H4 units of storylines, scenarios, and manuscripts directly cite the same-level upstream units they inherit and the settings they actually use; each file answers its principle checklists at file scope.

An `@evidence` states what is inherited and how it is realized; an `@evidenceReview` states what was actually verified, together with the current fingerprint. A setting unused by the unit, or a checklist item a work truthfully sits outside of, may be excluded with a concrete scope reason. Lineage can never be excluded, and common principles can never be excluded. False citations written to pass compilation and meaningless "not applicable" notes are forbidden.

Sources: [`@ttsc/evidence` documentation](https://github.com/samchon/ttsc/tree/master/packages/evidence)
