# Scenario Principles

Criteria for scenario files that turn storylines into production-capable initial scripts in which situations, actions or processes, reactions, and required speech progress concretely.

## Script blocks {#script-blocks}

After the evidence comment beneath each H4, begin the authored body with the first executable action block and no separate planning summary. Each action paragraph carries one followable action beat or continuous movement and breaks at a meaningful change in action, reaction, temporal condition, or spatial condition. Separate dialogue by speaker, placing interruptions, silence, and simultaneous action beside the action whose outcome they change.

Do not compress an entire scene into one explanatory paragraph or hide decisive dialogue as quotation inside exposition. Do not fracture every gesture into its own line and destroy flow. Use no fixed line or paragraph count; choose blocks whose execution order and reaction boundaries are immediately legible.

This item asks about the readability and block structure of an initial script. Action progression and dialogue action govern which actions and exchanges must be written, and the common substantive-completion obligation governs sufficient scene content.

Sources: [BBC Screenplay Format on separate action and dialogue presentation](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/screenplay.pdf), [BBC Taped Drama Format on action description and speaker blocks](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/bbctapeddrama.pdf), [Academy Nicholl on readable screenplay format and resources](https://www.oscars.org/nicholl/screenwriting-resources)

## Scene entry state {#scene-entry-state}

Establish the time, place, participants or active forces, focal access or observation range, and physical or social pressure inherited when each scene begins. Include the trigger that starts the scene.

Do not merely list planning fields. Make clear where and under which conditions the first action or process can occur.

This item establishes the current scene's starting conditions. Narrative state continuity governs whether those conditions accurately inherit settings and earlier units.

Sources: [Oregon State University on the effect of concrete time and place on a scene](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-setting), [Zacks and colleagues on temporal, causal, character, location, and goal changes as event boundaries](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8710938/), [BubbleCow on scene goals, conflict, and outcomes](https://bubblecow.com/blog/writing-craft/pacing-and-scenes/scene-structure-goal-conflict-and-outcome/)

## Scene exit state {#scene-exit-state}

Leave what changed at the end of each scene in an observable state. Establish the change in choice, relationship, knowledge, resources, position, emotion, or reader interpretation that the next scene inherits.

A scene may return to its starting conditions, but the result added by that repetition or stasis must remain clear.

This item establishes the state left by the current scene. Narrative unit connection governs the meaning that state has for the next unit.

Sources: [Zacks and colleagues on temporal, causal, character, location, and goal changes as event boundaries](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8710938/), [BubbleCow on scene outcomes creating subsequent conditions](https://bubblecow.com/blog/writing-craft/pacing-and-scenes/scene-structure-goal-conflict-and-outcome/), [Harvard Writing Center on transitions revealing relations between what precedes and follows](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/transitions)

## Action progression {#action-progression}

In a goal-directed scene, write who attempts what, what obstructs the attempt, and how the participants respond so temporal and causal relations are legible in presentation order. When observation, ritual, procedure, or a nonhuman process is central, do not invent an obstruction; write what changes through which order and interaction. Incorporate movement, objects, environment, procedural requirements, bodily limits, and necessary time into the actual progression.

Do not solve a problem with information or resources absent from settings. Leave the concrete progression by which the scene's forces change rather than imposing a fixed beat template or shot list.

This item asks about execution order within a scene. Narrative unit connection governs the relationship between scenes.

Sources: [Zwaan, Langston, and Graesser on continuity of time, space, causality, protagonist, and intention](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x), [Oregon State University on environment acting on action and conflict](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-setting)

## Dialogue action {#dialogue-action}

In a scene with dialogue, write the work performed by each utterance and the other participant's response as part of the progression. Questions, lies, bargains, refusals, silence, and interruptions that change choice, knowledge, power, or relationship receive their actual words and the action and response at that moment, so the next layer need not invent content, tactic, or result. Summarize only incidental conversation that affects none of them.

Do not repeat already shared facts merely to explain them to the reader or fill harmless chatter to make the document resemble a script.

This item asks about the tactics and consequences performed by speech and silence. Action progression governs overall sequence including speaking time, and knowledge state governs information before and after an exchange.

Sources: [Center for Fiction on dialogue moving character and event](https://centerforfiction.org/writing-tools/tips-for-writing-dialogue/), [the Cambridge Companion to Narrative on the function and selection of fictional dialogue](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-narrative/dialogue/DA260BBCE7DBBA9BCB4196CE7F35ABDC)

## Knowledge state {#knowledge-state}

Track what each participant knows, believes, suspects, and misunderstands before and after the scene. Changed information travels by an actual channel such as document, observation, speech, silence, or absence.

When the reader is ahead of or behind a participant, establish the difference and the interval for which it persists.

This item asks how information moves within a scene. Narrative state continuity governs preservation of knowledge between scenes, and storyline information design governs when information reaches the reader.

Sources: [Brewer and Lichtenstein on information order, suspense, curiosity, and surprise](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00017568), [Zwaan, Langston, and Graesser on continuity of character, intention, and causality](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x)
