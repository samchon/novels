# Storyline Principles

Criteria for storyline files that determine causes, choices, events, and changes.

## Information design {#information-design}

For each unit, decide what the reader knows, asks, and is led to believe incorrectly. Develop and pay off important disclosures from earlier planted facts, and do not let surprise invalidate those facts.

Answer, transform, or deliberately leave open the questions and promises the work opens. Do not manufacture secrets for a work that does not use tension or reversal.

This item asks how information is disclosed across the complete work. Narrative information entry governs whether information is intelligible when it first appears, and scenario information state governs what each participant knows inside a scene.

Sources: [Barthes on the structure of question, delay, and answer](https://archive.org/details/sz0000bart), [Brewer and Lichtenstein on information order and suspense, curiosity, and surprise](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00017568), [Oregon State University on foreshadowing](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-foreshadowing)

## Resolution and aftermath {#resolution-aftermath}

A closing unit decides which accumulated questions, choices, relationships, and images to close and which to leave open. The result must be intelligible from earlier conditions and leave what changed, or ultimately did not change, after the ending.

An open ending still gives the reader grounds for judgment and a reason to stop. Do not force every work to resolve everything or attach an epilogue.

This item asks how the ending handles prior accumulation. Storyline ending-position obligation governs which H2 is the last unit of the current delivery, and storyline epilogue obligation governs the function of a separate unit after the main resolution.

Sources: [Burton and Poster on the range from open to closed endings](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/prompts-you-need-to-help-you-write-the-book-you-want-to-write/beginnings-and-endings/B192F95BCB024C6EBB7F2A5C918EB5FC), [Purdue OWL on plot, climax, resolution, and theme](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/writing_in_literature/writing_in_literature_detailed_discussion/reading_a_novel_or_story.html)

## Thematic development {#thematic-development}

Let the work's central concerns emerge through repetition and difference in events, choices, relationships, places, images, and form. Do not append an answer from outside the work.

The work may choose one position or several interpretations. In either case, recurring elements must not repeat an unchanged meaning but persist or transform in each new context.

Sources: [UNC Writing Center on meaning arising through a fiction's form and detail](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/literature-fiction/), [Magliano and colleagues on character-goal tracking and thematic understanding](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4266429/)
