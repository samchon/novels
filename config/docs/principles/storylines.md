# Storyline Principles

Principles for the layer that owns causes, choices, events, and changes. No item may be excluded; each item binds wherever its condition applies, and citing it asserts that compliance.

## Character agency {#character-agency}

When a unit contains a meaningful choice, make intelligible which of the chooser's current desires, values, knowledge, relationships, or pressures it comes from. Not acting, hesitating, complying, and watching can also function as choices whose consequences are accepted.

A choice against established disposition needs the situation or shift of awareness that made it possible. Never use a character merely as a vehicle for events the author decreed.

A unit with no choosing agent is outside this principle's scope.

Sources: [Purdue OWL on characters and desire](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/fiction-basics/characters_and_fiction_writing.html), [Purdue OWL on goals and conflict](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/fiction-basics/writing_compelling_characters.html), [reading of character goal shifts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4266429/), [character goal pursuit and narrative engagement](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2019.1601569)

## Characterization {#characterization}

A character's disposition and state may be narrated directly as needed, but action, speech, thought, gesture, and effect on others must concretize or complicate that narration. Choose deliberately between agreement and gap between what a character claims about themselves and what the reader observes.

A character may change or refuse to change; either way, responses to experience and pressure must be intelligible without contradicting the established disposition.

A unit that characterizes no one is outside this principle's scope.

Sources: [Oregon State University on characterization](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-characterization-literature-definition-and-examples), [Purdue OWL on revealing characters](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/fiction-basics/building_and_revealing_characters.html)

## Dramatic tension {#dramatic-tension}

A unit that intends tension holds a question the reader grips, a difference between possible outcomes, and an uncertainty that keeps the outcome unsettled. Scale and kind of risk follow what the characters or the narration hold important, not the genre's default stakes.

Modulate pressure in ways fitted to the work: rising cost, shrinking options, waiting, information gaps, internal contradiction.

A unit not aimed at tension — transition, recovery, meditation, atmosphere — is outside this principle's scope so long as it owns another narrative function.

Sources: [Open University on suspense](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-3.4), [UC San Diego on conflict, crisis, and foreshadowing](https://extendedstudies.ucsd.edu/news-events/extended-studies-blog/the-call-to-creative-writing-finding-your-voice-telling-your-story)

## Action and consequence {#action-consequence}

Write narratively weighted action not as a list of motions but as a process linking the actor's intention, the given conditions, the response, and the result. Match the size and kind of consequence to the weight the work gives the action, and leave behind what actually changed among body, information, resources, relationships, perception, and emotion.

Do not let status or ability from the settings decide success automatically; let situation and choice operate. A unit that handles no action or consequence is outside this principle's scope.

## Foreshadowing and payoff {#foreshadowing-payoff}

Foreshadowing has a lineage of planting, development, and payoff. A planting works naturally in its own moment as action, object, speech, or image while opening a later possibility; development does not repeat the same cue unchanged but shifts its risk or interpretation; payoff connects the earlier detail to a new meaning, producing surprise and recognition together.

Do not stress a hint like a core promise when no payoff is planned, and do not let a core resolution depend on a rule or ability never planted.

A unit that plants, develops, and pays off nothing is outside this principle's scope.

Sources: [Oregon State University on foreshadowing](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-foreshadowing), [University of Minnesota on plot, suspense, and surprise](https://manifold.open.umn.edu/read/c10-plot-rodak-storey/section/d89285f2-a958-4caa-9c4d-7f0f612724b8)

## Information control and payoff {#information-payoff}

Distinguish the information the reader needs to understand the current narration from the information deliberately withheld. Do not dump names, rules, relationships, and time shifts at once; introduce new information connected to context already given.

Ground in advance any condition decisive for understanding a later outcome, without breaking the surprise and plausibility the work has promised. A revelation extends or legitimately revises earlier facts rather than arbitrarily voiding them, and leaves behind a change in character judgment, relationships, situation, or reader interpretation.

A unit responsible for no new information or revelation is outside this principle's scope.

Sources: [Harvard College Writing Center on connecting information](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/transitions), [Brewer & Lichtenstein's structural-affect theory](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00017568)

## Scene function {#scene-function}

Every scene owns a function among event, recognition, relationship, emotion, atmosphere, image, rhythm, or reader interpretation. An action scene uses goal, obstruction, choice, and result as needed; a scene without people can function through environment, traces, absence, and the arrangement of sensation; minimal change and stillness are possible.

What the scene's duration adds to its neighbors and to the whole must be explainable. Merge or cut scenes that repeat a function without new effect; a scene that could be deleted while choices and reader expectations stay the same lacks a reason to stand alone.

## Resolution and aftermath {#resolution-aftermath}

A unit responsible for closure chooses which of the work's built-up questions, relationships, images, and formal promises it closes and which it leaves open.

A closed ending makes its outcome intelligible from earlier conditions and choices; an open ending provides the meaning of not answering and the grounds on which the reader will judge. The closing weight of each major element follows the weight the work accumulated in it.

Make identifiable what changed after the end, or what finally did not change. A unit with no closing function is outside this principle's scope.

Sources: [Texas A&M University Writing Center on plot and resolution](https://writingcenter.tamu.edu/guides/resources/analyzing-novels.html), [Southeastern Louisiana University's ending review questions](https://www.southeastern.edu/wp-content/uploads/omni-misc-files/acad_research/programs/writing_center/handouts/pdf_handouts/readingandwritingabo.pdf)

## Thematic coherence {#thematic-coherence}

Theme emerges from the repetitions and differences made by events, characters, relationships, places, images, voice, and form, not from a correct answer appended from outside the work.

The work may aim at one position or hold several interpretations open, but its chosen elements must connect to a central concern instead of scattering into unrelated claims. Symbols and recurring images keep, extend, or invert their meaning by context while still carrying their scene's own function.

A unit with no thematic responsibility is outside this principle's scope.

Sources: [Texas A&M University Writing Center on theme](https://writingcenter.tamu.edu/guides/resources/analyzing-novels.html), [UW–Madison Writing Center on cohesion](https://writing.wisc.edu/handbook/style/connectingideas/)
