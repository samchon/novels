# Settings Obligations

## Delivery scope {#delivery-scope}

Define the work this package will complete and deliver now. State whether it is a standalone work, one volume, one part, a completed serial, or the currently delivered portion of an ongoing serial, and how it relates to any larger work.

This item asks for the boundary of the delivery. Storyline ending-position obligation governs the actual final sequence and its function.

Sources: [Furneaux on provisional closure and continuation in serial narrative](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/virtual-play-and-the-victorian-novel/continuation-attachment-and-william-makepeace-thackeray/85BD61006C84A23DF2563A6993771323), [Burton and Poster on the relationship among beginnings, endings, form, and length](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-book-you-need-to-read-to-write-the-book-you-want-to-write/BCFED30FA31DEC5A505CDE80AC6C5675)

## Work promise {#work-promise}

Define the central experience the work intends to give its reader. State which question, emotion, understanding, pleasure, or formal effect it will develop through the ending and how that promise selects among the work's possible choices.

This does not require one theme or conclusion. Where the work uses several effects, make clear what they serve together.

Sources: [Jakobson on the dominant that governs a work's other elements](https://archive.org/details/readingsinrussia0000mate), [Burton and Poster on testing chapter function and how a work lands](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/book-you-need-to-read-to-write-the-book-you-want-to-write/beginnings-and-endings-tension-and-pace/7B84CB546C696FD1B6A8AB753522B42C)

## Reader access {#reader-access}

Define how the reader gains access to events and characters. Record whichever of narrator, focalization, psychic distance, reliability, available information, and conditions for perspective change the work uses.

When the work has no fixed narrator or focal subject, state the authority and transition principles of documents, dialogue, observation, or any other delivery mode.

Sources: [Genette on the distinction between voice and focalization](https://archive.org/details/narrativediscour0000gene), [Living Handbook of Narratology on the possibility of narratorless narrative](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/44.html), [Fludernik on the diverse forms and functions of second-person narrative](https://www.jstor.org/stable/42946261), [Burton and Poster on choosing and changing viewpoint](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/prompts-you-need-to-help-you-write-the-book-you-want-to-write/point-of-view/9E9D1AA8E2841E6508D5864791DDE0FE)
